LESSON 6.6 Testing & Assumptions

Testing Trends & the No-Anticipation Assumption

How can an empirical researcher formally test whether pre-trends are parallel? Discover the extended group-specific trend regression from page 14 of the primary source, explore quadratic extensions for non-linear drift, and master the No-Anticipation assumption that prevents behavioral reactions from contaminating nominal pre-periods.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Formulate the group-specific linear trend regression using pre-treatment observations.
  • Set up and conduct the statistical hypothesis test for parallel linear pre-slopes (\(H_0: \beta_3 = 0\)).
  • Understand quadratic trend extensions that allow for non-linear secular curvature.
  • Define the No-Anticipation assumption and explain why advance announcements contaminate pre-treatment data.
  • Analyze the cigarette tax case study where anticipatory behavior biases the estimated policy impact.

1. The Group-Specific Trend Model (Source Page 14)

When researchers possess panel or repeated cross-sectional data spanning multiple pre-treatment periods (\(t = -T_0, \dots, 0\)), they can test whether the treated and control groups had statistically indistinguishable pre-treatment growth rates.

Following the exact specification on page 14 of the source:

Group-Specific Linear Trend Regression (Pre-Treatment Sample)
\[y_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 D_i + \beta_2 \text{Trend}_t + \beta_3 (D_i \cdot \text{Trend}_t) + \mathbf{W}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\Gamma} + \varepsilon_{it}\]
Where \(D_i\) is the treated group indicator, \(\text{Trend}_t \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}\) is a continuous time counter, \(D_i \cdot \text{Trend}_t\) is the group-specific trend interaction, and \(\mathbf{W}_{it}\) is a vector of additional control variables.
CONTROL SLOPE
\(\beta_2\)

Measures the baseline linear growth rate per period experienced by the untreated control group before the policy.

DIFFERENTIAL SLOPE
\(\beta_3\)

Measures the difference in linear trend slope between the treated group and the control group prior to treatment.

2. The Formal Hypothesis Test for Parallel Pre-Trends

To evaluate whether pre-trends were statistically parallel, we formulate a restriction on the interaction coefficient \(\beta_3\):

Hypothesis Test for Equal Linear Slopes
\[H_0: \beta_3 = 0 \quad \text{versus} \quad H_1: \beta_3 \neq 0\]
Under \(H_0\): Both groups share the identical linear slope \(\beta_2\) in the pre-period (supporting the parallel trends assumption).
Under \(H_1\): The treated group’s pre-trend slope (\(\beta_2 + \beta_3\)) differs significantly from the control group’s slope, signaling identification failure.

Source Note: Quadratic Trend Extension

As noted on page 14 of the source, economic variables often accelerate or decelerate over time. Researchers can test for non-linear pre-trend differences by adding quadratic terms:
\(+ \beta_4 \text{Trend}_t^2 + \beta_5 (D_i \cdot \text{Trend}_t^2)\)
A joint \(F\)-test of \(H_0: \beta_3 = 0 \text{ and } \beta_5 = 0\) then tests for both linear and quadratic pre-trend equivalence.

3. The No-Anticipation Assumption

In addition to parallel trends, Difference-in-Differences requires that units do not change their behavior in anticipation of future treatment:

Definition: The No-Anticipation Assumption
\[y_{it}(D_i) = y_{it}(0) \quad \text{for all } t < t_{\text{implementation}}\]
Treatment status must have zero causal effect on the outcome prior to the official implementation date.
PRIMARY SOURCE CASE

The Cigarette Tax Announcement

Consider the primary source example from page 14:

  • \(t = 0\) (Baseline): Normal smoking behavior in both states.
  • \(t = 1\) (Announcement): State legislature announces that a major cigarette tax will take effect at \(t = 2\). Smokers in the treated state start stocking up or quitting early.
  • \(t = 2\) (Official Rollout): The tax officially takes effect.
The Contamination Problem: Although the law officially begins at \(t = 2\), the nominal "pre-period" at \(t = 1\) is already partially treated! Calculating DD using \(t=1\) as the pre-treatment baseline will produce a heavily biased estimate of the true tax elasticity.

5. Common Methodological Pitfall

METHODOLOGICAL CAUTION Common Mistake: Ignoring Anticipatory Contamination

"The policy took effect on July 1, so our pre-period is January 1 to June 30."

Correction: If the law was debated in parliament and signed into law in March, firms and households likely adjusted their behavior long before July 1.

Using observations immediately preceding official implementation when anticipation is present will contaminate the baseline, attenuating or distorting the estimated treatment effect. Best practice is to define the pre-period strictly prior to the policy announcement.

QUICK CHECK Interpreting a Pre-Trend F-Test

In a pre-trend model with quadratic interactions, you test \(H_0: \beta_{\text{linear}} = 0 \text{ and } \beta_{\text{quad}} = 0\) and obtain \(F = 0.82\) with \(p = 0.44\). Does this support or contradict the validity of a DD design?

Show Answer & Explanation
ANSWER: It SUPPORTS the validity of the DD design.

WHY?
  1. The null hypothesis \(H_0\) states that the treated and control groups shared both identical linear slopes and identical quadratic curvature in the pre-period.
  2. A \(p\)-value of \(0.44 > 0.05\) means we fail to reject \(H_0\).
  3. There is no statistically significant evidence of pre-trend divergence, lending strong empirical credibility to the parallel trends assumption!
TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Pre-treatment parallel trends can be formally evaluated using the interaction model \(y_{it} = \dots + \beta_3 (D_i \cdot \text{Trend}_t)\).
  • Testing \(H_0: \beta_3 = 0\) examines whether treated and control groups exhibited equal linear pre-slopes.
  • Non-linear pre-trend curvature can be investigated by adding quadratic terms \(\text{Trend}_t^2\) and \(D_i \cdot \text{Trend}_t^2\).
  • The No-Anticipation assumption requires that economic agents do not alter their behavior before the policy takes effect.
  • When policies are announced in advance, nominal pre-periods become contaminated, necessitating the use of clean pre-announcement baselines.
