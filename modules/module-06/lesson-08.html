1. The Philadelphia / Pittsburgh Case Study Setup

Pages 15–16 of the primary source introduce an educational reform evaluation with three binary dimensions:

  • City (\(P_c\)): Philadelphia (\(P_c = 1\)) versus Pittsburgh (\(P_c = 0\)).
  • Grade (\(J_i\)): High School Juniors (\(J_i = 1\)) versus Sophomores (\(J_i = 0\)).
  • Time (\(T_t\)): Post-Reform (\(T_t = 1\)) versus Pre-Reform (\(T_t = 0\)).

The Policy: An intensive tutoring program was introduced only for Philadelphia high school juniors in the post-period.

SOURCE SPECIFICATION The Full Saturated DDD Regression (Page 15)
\[\begin{aligned} y_{ict} = \beta_0 &+ \beta_1 P_c + \beta_2 J_i + \beta_3 T_t \\ &+ \beta_4 (P_c \cdot J_i) + \beta_5 (P_c \cdot T_t) + \beta_6 (J_i \cdot T_t) \\ &+ \mathbf{\beta_7 (P_c \cdot J_i \cdot T_t)} + \varepsilon_{ict} \end{aligned}\]
Where \(y_{ict}\) is the standardized test score of student \(i\) in city \(c\) at time \(t\). The eight coefficients correspond exactly to the eight distinct city-grade-time group means.

2. The 2×2×2 Group Means Decomposition (Source Page 16)

Evaluating the saturated regression equation across all eight combinations yields the complete decomposition of group means:

PITTSBURGH (\(P = 0\)) — CONTROL CITY

Comparison City · No Tutoring Policy
Grade Pre-Reform (\(T=0\)) Post-Reform (\(T=1\)) \(\Delta\) (Time Change)
Sophomores (\(J=0\)) \(\beta_0\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_3\) \(\Delta_{\text{Pitt Soph}} = \beta_3\)
Juniors (\(J=1\)) \(\beta_0 + \beta_2\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_2 + \beta_3 + \beta_6\) \(\Delta_{\text{Pitt Jr}} = \beta_3 + \beta_6\)
Within-Pittsburgh DD (Grade Secular Difference): \(\text{DD}_{\text{Pitt}} = \Delta_{\text{Pitt Jr}} - \Delta_{\text{Pitt Soph}} = (\beta_3 + \beta_6) - \beta_3 = \beta_6\)

PHILADELPHIA (\(P = 1\)) — TREATED CITY

Target City · Intensive Tutoring Reform for Juniors
Grade Pre-Reform (\(T=0\)) Post-Reform (\(T=1\)) \(\Delta\) (Time Change)
Sophomores (\(J=0\)) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_3 + \beta_5\) \(\Delta_{\text{Philly Soph}} = \beta_3 + \beta_5\)
Juniors (\(J=1\)) ★ (Treated) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_4\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_3 + \beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7\) \(\Delta_{\text{Philly Jr}} = \beta_3 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7\)
Within-Philadelphia DD (Shock + Tutoring Effect): \(\text{DD}_{\text{Philly}} = \Delta_{\text{Philly Jr}} - \Delta_{\text{Philly Soph}} = \beta_6 + \beta_7\)
THE DDD CANCELLATION Subtracting the Comparison Contrasts Yields \(\beta_7\)
\[\text{DDD} = \text{DD}_{\text{Philly}} - \text{DD}_{\text{Pitt}} = (\beta_6 + \beta_7) - \beta_6 = \mathbf{\beta_7}\]
Equivalently by grade: \(\text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} - \text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = (\beta_5 + \beta_7) - \beta_5 = \mathbf{\beta_7}\). Both the city shock \(\beta_5\) and secular trend \(\beta_6\) cancel out cleanly!

3. Connected Derivation: Proving That \(\beta_7 = \text{DDD}\)

Now we follow the 3-step algebraic subtraction from page 16 of the source:

Derivation Goal

Isolate the Triple Interaction Parameter \(\beta_7\)

Demonstrate how subtracting the Sophomore Difference-in-Differences from the Junior Difference-in-Differences purges the city-wide time shock \(\beta_5\).

1

Compute the Difference-in-Differences for Juniors

\(\text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} = \beta_5 + \beta_7\)

Subtract Pittsburgh Junior change from Philadelphia Junior change:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} &= \Delta_{\text{Philly Jr}} - \Delta_{\text{Pitt Jr}} \\ &= (\beta_3 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7) - (\beta_3 + \beta_6) = \mathbf{\beta_5 + \beta_7} \end{aligned}\]
Why this step works

Junior changes remove baseline city gaps and grade differences, but they leave \(\beta_5\) (the Philadelphia citywide shock) mixed with the policy effect \(\beta_7\).

2

Compute the Difference-in-Differences for Sophomores

\(\text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = \beta_5\)

Subtract Pittsburgh Sophomore change from Philadelphia Sophomore change:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} &= \Delta_{\text{Philly Soph}} - \Delta_{\text{Pitt Soph}} \\ &= (\beta_3 + \beta_5) - \beta_3 = \mathbf{\beta_5} \end{aligned}\]
Why this step works

Because Sophomores were never eligible for the tutoring program, their DD contrast isolates purely the Philadelphia citywide shock \(\beta_5\)!

3

Subtract the Two DD Contrasts to Find DDD

\(\text{DDD} = \beta_7\)

Take the difference between the Junior DD and the Sophomore DD:

\[\text{DDD} = \text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} - \text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = (\beta_5 + \beta_7) - \beta_5 = \mathbf{\beta_7}\]
Why this step works

Subtracting \(\beta_5\) purges the citywide shock, leaving only the true causal effect of the tutoring program on Philadelphia Juniors!

LEVEL MODEL VS. CHANGE MODEL & CONTROLS

Level vs. Change: In a level model, we estimate 8 parameters. If data are first-differenced across time, the time-invariant terms drop out, yielding a change specification with fewer parameters.

With vs. Without Controls: Without controls (\(\mathbf{X}_{ict}\)), the regression coefficients map directly to sample cell means. With controls, interpretations become conditional on holding student socioeconomic background constant.

5. Module 06 Comprehensive Knowledge Map

Congratulations on completing Module 6! Review the complete interconnected conceptual synthesis below:

1. OVB DEFINITION
Omitted Variable Bias

Occurs when an included regressor accidentally absorbs the influence of a left-out relevant variable.

2. CORRELATION CONDITION
\(X_1'X_2 \neq 0\)

The omitted variable must be linearly associated with the included regressor(s) to pass its effect along.

3. RELEVANCE CONDITION
\(\beta_2 \neq 0\)

The omitted variable must have a true non-zero causal effect on outcome \(y\) in the population DGP.

4. MATRIX BIAS FORMULA
\((X_1'X_1)^{-1}X_1'X_2 \beta_2\)

Decomposes as the linear projection of \(X_2\) on \(X_1\) times the effect of \(X_2\) on \(y\).

5. BIAS VS PRECISION
Expected Value vs Spread

Never judge a specification purely by standard errors: a small SE around a biased estimate is invalid!

6. RANDOMIZED EXPERIMENTS
\(\operatorname{Cov}(D, X) = 0\)

Physical random assignment prevents baseline covariates from systematically contaminating treatment estimates.

7. CONTROLS IN RCTS
\(\hat{\sigma}^2 \downarrow \implies \operatorname{SE} \downarrow\)

Pre-treatment controls are not needed for unbias, but they shrink error variance and standard errors for free.

8. 2×2 DD SPECIFICATION
\(\beta_1 + \beta_2 T + \beta_3 D + \beta_4 (TD)\)

Controls for baseline group disparity (\(\beta_3\)) and common secular time trends (\(\beta_2\)).

9. DD INTERACTION COEFFICIENT
\(\beta_4 = \Delta_{\text{Trt}} - \Delta_{\text{Ctrl}}\)

The difference between group changes over time isolates the causal treatment impact under parallel trends.

10. PARALLEL TRENDS
Counterfactual Trend Parity

The core counterfactual assumption that groups would have changed at the same rate without treatment.

11. NO ANTICIPATION
Zero Pre-Treatment Impact

Agents must not adjust behavior before implementation; advance announcements contaminate nominal pre-periods.

12. DDD LOGIC
\(\text{DDD} = \text{DD}_1 - \text{DD}_2\)

Subtracts an unaffected within-state comparison group's DD to purge concurrent region-specific shocks.

13. TRIPLE INTERACTION
\(\beta_7 (S \cdot G \cdot T)\)

In a saturated OLS model, \(\beta_7\) directly estimates the causal DDD effect while controlling for all two-way shocks.

QUICK CHECK Master Econometrician Synthesis

Suppose an econometrician runs a DDD regression and finds \(\beta_5 = +4.0\) (citywide shock) and \(\beta_7 = +3.0\) (triple interaction). If they had only estimated a standard DD on Juniors, what would their estimated treatment effect have been, and why would it be biased?

Show Answer & Explanation
ANSWER: Standard DD would report \(+7.0\), which is upwardly biased by \(+4.0\).

WHY?
  1. In the Junior DD: \(\text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} = \beta_5 + \beta_7 = 4.0 + 3.0 = 7.0\).
  2. This \(+7.0\) estimate erroneously credits the entire citywide educational improvement (\(\beta_5 = 4.0\)) to the tutoring program.
  3. By taking the third difference against Sophomores (\(\text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = \beta_5 = 4.0\)), DDD correctly purges this confounding: \(\text{DDD} = 7.0 - 4.0 = \mathbf{3.0}\).
TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The Philadelphia/Pittsburgh case study illustrates how DDD isolates reforms using City \(\times\) Grade \(\times\) Time variation.
  • The full regression contains 8 parameters representing all 8 possible group-time cell means.
  • Junior DD captures \(\beta_5 + \beta_7\); Sophomore DD captures \(\beta_5\); their difference isolates \(\beta_7\).
  • Without controls, regression estimates correspond directly to sample cell averages; with controls, estimates become conditional on student covariates.
  • You have now mastered Omitted Variable Bias, Variance Trade-Offs, Randomized Controlled Trials, Difference-in-Differences, Parallel Trends, and DDD!
