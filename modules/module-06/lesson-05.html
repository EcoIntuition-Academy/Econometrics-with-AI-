LESSON 6.5 Identification

Parallel Trends & DD Identification

Difference-in-Differences is NOT automatically causal simply because we have two groups observed across time. Its empirical validity rests squarely upon a single counterfactual assumption: Parallel Trends. Explore why untreated changes must be comparable, recreate the canonical diagrams from pages 12–13 of the source, and understand how pre-trend divergence destroys identification.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Formulate the Parallel Trends identifying assumption using potential outcomes notation.
  • Understand why Difference-in-Differences allows baseline level disparities while demanding trend parity.
  • Reconstruct the unobservable counterfactual path: \(\mathbb{E}[y_{i1}(0) \mid D_i=1] = \mathbb{E}[y_{i0} \mid D_i=1] + \big(\mathbb{E}[y_{i1} \mid D_i=0] - \mathbb{E}[y_{i0} \mid D_i=0]\big)\).
  • Inspect and contrast valid parallel pre-trends with divergent, invalid pre-trends.
  • Analyze how non-parallel secular growth biases the estimated treatment coefficient upward or downward.

1. The Identifying Assumption: Parallel Trends

In observational studies, we can never observe what would have happened to the treated group in the post-period had the policy never been implemented. To construct this missing counterfactual, Difference-in-Differences makes a foundational assumption:

Definition: The Parallel Trends Assumption
\[\mathbb{E}[y_{i1}(0) - y_{i0}(0) \mid D_i = 1] = \mathbb{E}[y_{i1}(0) - y_{i0}(0) \mid D_i = 0]\]
In plain language: In the absence of treatment, the average outcome of the treated group would have changed by the exact same amount over time as the average outcome of the control group.

Notice what this assumption says—and what it does not say:

  • Levels may differ: The treated group can have higher crime, lower wages, or higher GDP at baseline. The initial gap \(\beta_3\) is fully permitted.
  • Trajectories must match: What must be equal across groups is the untreated change over time. The control group serves as the measuring stick for the background economic weather.

2. Visualizing the Counterfactual Gap (Source Page 12)

Page 12 of the primary source introduces the classic geometric representation of Difference-in-Differences identification:

Canonical Geometric Identification of the Treatment Effect

The control group experiences a change of \(\Delta_{\text{Control}} = 180 - 140 = +40\). Under parallel trends, had the treated group remained untreated, it also would have increased by \(+40\) (moving from \(120\) to \(80\)). Because it actually rose to \(46\), the true causal impact is the vertical gap between the observed outcome and the counterfactual: \(\beta_4 = 80 - 46 = 34\).

3. Inspecting Pre-Trends: Valid vs. Invalid Trajectories

Because the counterfactual in the post-period is unobservable, how can economists evaluate whether parallel trends is plausible? Page 13 of the source highlights the standard diagnostic: inspecting pre-treatment trajectories across multiple pre-periods.

VALID: PARALLEL PRE-TRENDS

Credible DD

Before the policy date, treated and control groups moved in lockstep. This parallel pre-treatment behavior provides strong credibility that their trajectories would have continued in parallel without treatment.

INVALID: DIVERGENT PRE-TRENDS

Biased DD

The treated group was already growing faster prior to the policy! A standard DD will conflate this pre-existing upward momentum with the reform, producing a spurious, upwardly biased treatment effect.

5. Common Methodological Pitfall

METHODOLOGICAL CAUTION Common Mistake: Claiming Parallel Trends is "Proved"

"Because pre-treatment trends were statistically identical from t = -3 to t = 0, we have definitively proved the parallel trends assumption."

Correction: Parallel trends in the pre-period is a diagnostic check, not mathematical proof of post-period validity!

A concurrent macroeconomic shock (e.g., a local factory opening, an oil boom, or a complementary state law) could hit the treated group at the exact same time the policy was enacted. Even with immaculate pre-trends, concurrent confounding shocks will invalidate DD identification.

QUICK CHECK What is the Counterfactual Formula?

Under parallel trends, how is the unobserved counterfactual post-treatment outcome for the treated group, \(\mathbb{E}[y_{i1}(0) \mid D_i=1]\), constructed from observable data?

Show Answer & Explanation
ANSWER: \(\mathbb{E}[y_{i1}(0) \mid D_i=1] = \mathbb{E}[y_{i0} \mid D_i=1] + \big(\mathbb{E}[y_{i1} \mid D_i=0] - \mathbb{E}[y_{i0} \mid D_i=0]\big)\).

WHY?
  1. Start with the treated group’s actual baseline outcome: \(\mathbb{E}[y_{i0} \mid D_i=1]\).
  2. Add the change experienced over time by the untreated control group: \(\Delta_{\text{Control}} = \mathbb{E}[y_{i1} \mid D_i=0] - \mathbb{E}[y_{i0} \mid D_i=0]\).
  3. This imputes what the treated group would have achieved had it grown at the same rate as the control group. The treatment effect is then the observed treated post outcome minus this counterfactual value!
TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Difference-in-Differences is identified if and only if the Parallel Trends assumption holds.
  • Parallel trends states that without treatment, the treated group would have followed the same trajectory over time as the control group.
  • Baseline outcome levels can differ between groups (\(\beta_3 \neq 0\)); what must be comparable is the untreated change over time.
  • Because post-treatment counterfactuals are unobservable, econometricians inspect pre-treatment periods as a vital diagnostic test.
  • Pre-trend divergence signals that the control group is an invalid counterfactual, resulting in biased DD estimates.
