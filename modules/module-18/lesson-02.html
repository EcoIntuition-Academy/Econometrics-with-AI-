LESSON 18.2 Estimation & Odds Ratios

Likelihood, Base Categories & Relative Odds

Construct the multinomial sample log-likelihood using choice indicators, examine maximum likelihood optimization under base-category normalization, derive the relative odds ratio, and establish the exact interpretation of coefficients as relative log-odds.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Construct the sample log-likelihood function for multinomial discrete choice using binary indicator variables \(d_{ij} = \mathbf{1}(y_i = j)\).
  • Understand how the likelihood is numerically maximized over the non-base coefficient vectors \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\beta}_J\) with \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0 = \mathbf{0}\) held fixed.
  • Derive the relative odds ratio \(\frac{P(y_i=j)}{P(y_i=0)} = \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j)\) and the linear relative log-odds formula.
  • Identify and avoid the pervasive student error of confusing relative log-odds coefficients with marginal probability effects.

1. The Multinomial Likelihood Function

To estimate the parameter vectors \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\beta}_J\) via Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), we define a set of mutually exclusive binary choice indicators for each individual \(i\) across all \(J + 1\) alternatives:

\[ d_{ij} = \mathbf{1}(y_i = j) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if individual } i \text{ chooses alternative } j \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Because every individual chooses exactly one alternative, \(\sum_{j=0}^J d_{ij} = 1\) for each observation. The probability of the realized choice for individual \(i\) is written compactly as:

\[ L_i = \prod_{j=0}^J [P(y_i = j \mid \mathbf{x}_i)]^{d_{ij}} \]

Taking the natural logarithm and summing across all \(n\) independent observations produces the sample log-likelihood function:

LOG-LIKELIHOOD Multinomial Log-Likelihood Function
\[ \ell(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^J d_{ij} \log \left[ \frac{\exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j)}{1 + \sum_{k=1}^J \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_k)} \right] + \sum_{i=1}^n d_{i0} \log \left[ \frac{1}{1 + \sum_{k=1}^J \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_k)} \right] \]
ESTIMATION PRINCIPLE:
Normalization \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0 = \mathbf{0}\) is fixed by construction, eliminating \(K\) redundant parameters.
Parameter Dimension Estimation maximizes \(\ell(\boldsymbol{\beta})\) with respect to \(J \times K\) parameters (\(K\) parameters for each of the \(J\) non-base alternatives).
Concavity The log-likelihood function of the standard MNL model is globally strictly concave, ensuring a unique maximum likelihood estimator \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\).

2. Odds Ratios & Relative Log-Odds

In binary logit, we evaluated the odds of success: \(\frac{P}{1 - P}\). In multinomial choice, we evaluate the odds of choosing alternative \(j\) relative to the designated base category \(0\).

DERIVATION JOURNEY · THE RELATIVE LOG-ODDS LINEARITY
Step 1: Take the ratio of choice probabilities

Divide the probability of alternative \(j\) by the probability of base category \(0\). The common denominator \(D_i = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^J \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_k)\) cancels out algebraically:

\[ \frac{P(y_i = j \mid \mathbf{x}_i)}{P(y_i = 0 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)} = \frac{\frac{\exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j)}{D_i}}{\frac{1}{D_i}} = \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j) \]
WHY? The common competition pool \(D_i\) affects all options equally, leaving the pair-wise relative odds dependent exclusively on \(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j\).
Step 2: Take the natural logarithm

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides linearizes the exponential expression:

\[ \log \left[ \frac{P(y_i = j \mid \mathbf{x}_i)}{P(y_i = 0 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)} \right] = \log\left(\exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j)\right) = \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j \]
WHY? The natural logarithm is the inverse of the exponential function, converting the multiplicative odds ratio into an additive index.
Step 3: Differentiate with respect to regressor \(x_{ir}\)

Take the partial derivative of the relative log-odds with respect to covariate \(x_{ir}\):

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial x_{ir}} \log \left[ \frac{P(y_i = j \mid \mathbf{x}_i)}{P(y_i = 0 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)} \right] = \beta_{jr} \]
KEY RESULT: \(\beta_{jr}\) is the exact semi-elasticity of the relative odds against the base category!
RELATIONSHIP Relative Odds & Log-Odds Equations
\[ \frac{P(y_i = j)}{P(y_i = 0)} = \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j) \] \[ \log \left[ \frac{P(y_i = j)}{P(y_i = 0)} \right] = \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j = \beta_{j0} + \beta_{j1}x_{i1} + \dots + \beta_{jK}x_{iK} \]
INTERPRETATION:
Additive Log-Odds A one-unit increase in \(x_{ir}\) increases the log-odds of choosing alternative \(j\) over the base category \(0\) by exactly \(\beta_{jr}\).
Multiplicative Odds A one-unit increase in \(x_{ir}\) multiplies the relative odds ratio \(\frac{P(y_i=j)}{P(y_i=0)}\) by the factor \(\exp(\beta_{jr})\).

3. Coefficient Interpretation vs. Marginal Effects

The most critical conceptual trap in multinomial logit analysis concerns the physical meaning of the estimated coefficients \(\hat{\beta}_{jr}\).

COMMON STUDENT MISTAKE · COEFFICIENTS ARE NOT PROBABILITY EFFECTS

MISCONCEPTION: "If the estimated coefficient on education for engineer is \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{eng, educ}} = +0.40\), an extra year of education raises the probability of choosing engineer by 40 percentage points (or 0.40)."

CORRECTION: \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{eng, educ}}\) measures the change in relative log-odds against the base category (e.g., waiter). It implies that an extra year of education multiplies the relative odds of choosing engineer over waiter by \(\exp(0.40) \approx 1.49\) (+49%).

The effect on the probability of choosing engineer depends on what happens to all other alternatives. If education increases the attractiveness of being a professor even more (\(\hat{\beta}_{\text{prof, educ}} = +1.20\)), the probability of choosing engineer can actually decrease, despite having a positive coefficient! Probability changes require computing non-linear marginal effects (covered in Lesson 18.3).

Relative Comparison Between Two Non-Base Alternatives

What if we wish to compare two non-base alternatives, such as Option 2 (Engineer) versus Option 1 (Teacher)? The relative log-odds between any two options is simply the difference of their coefficients:

\[ \log \left[ \frac{P(y_i = 2)}{P(y_i = 1)} \right] = \log \left[ \frac{P(y_i = 2)/P(y_i = 0)}{P(y_i = 1)/P(y_i = 0)} \right] = \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_2 - \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_1 = \mathbf{x}_i'(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2 - \boldsymbol{\beta}_1) \]

This confirms that the choice of base category is purely an organizational normalization; the relative ranking and odds between any two alternatives remain invariant to which option is labeled as base category \(0\).

5. Quick Knowledge Check

Question: Interpreting Differences in Log-Odds

In an occupational choice model with Base Category = Waiter (0), Teacher (1), and Engineer (2), the estimated coefficients on years of schooling are: \(\hat{\beta}_{1, \text{educ}} = 0.50\) and \(\hat{\beta}_{2, \text{educ}} = 0.80\). What is the estimated effect of an extra year of schooling on the relative log-odds of choosing Engineer versus Teacher?

Hint: Recall that \(\log(P_2 / P_1) = \log(P_2 / P_0) - \log(P_1 / P_0)\).

Solution:
The relative log-odds between Engineer (2) and Teacher (1) is: \[ \log\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) = \mathbf{x}_i'(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2 - \boldsymbol{\beta}_1) \] Taking the derivative with respect to education gives: \[ \frac{\partial \log(P_2 / P_1)}{\partial \text{educ}} = \hat{\beta}_{2, \text{educ}} - \hat{\beta}_{1, \text{educ}} = 0.80 - 0.50 = +0.30 \] An extra year of education increases the log-odds of choosing Engineer rather than Teacher by \(0.30\), which multiplies the odds of choosing Engineer over Teacher by \(\exp(0.30) \approx 1.35\) (+35%).

Lesson 18.2 Summary

In this lesson, we analyzed the estimation and structural interpretation of multinomial logit models:

  • Likelihood Construction: Mutually exclusive choice indicators \(d_{ij}\) define the sample log-likelihood across \(n\) observations and \(J\) non-base options.
  • Relative Odds: The ratio of probabilities satisfies \(\frac{P(y_i=j)}{P(y_i=0)} = \exp(\mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j)\).
  • Log-Odds Interpretation: A one-unit change in \(x_{ir}\) increases the log-odds of option \(j\) relative to the base category by exactly \(\beta_{jr}\).
  • Vital Distinction: Coefficients are log-odds parameters, not probability effects; evaluating changes in actual choice probabilities requires non-linear marginal effects.
