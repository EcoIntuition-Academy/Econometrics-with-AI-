LESSON 18.5 Choice Attribute Modeling

Conditional Logit & Alternative-Specific Characteristics

Extend discrete choice beyond individual-specific variables to alternative-specific attributes, derive the conditional logit choice probability with common parameters, contrast with MNL, and synthesize the discrete choice framework.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Motivate the necessity of Conditional Logit when regressors vary across alternatives (e.g., travel cost, travel time, ticket prices) rather than individuals.
  • Specify the Conditional Logit utility model \(U_{ij} = \mathbf{z}_{ij}'\boldsymbol{\alpha} + \varepsilon_{ij}\) with alternative-varying regressors and a common preference vector \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\).
  • Derive the choice probability \(P(y_i = j) = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{z}_{ij}'\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{\sum_{k=1}^J \exp(\mathbf{z}_{ik}'\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\) and understand why it is termed "conditional".
  • Contrast Multinomial Logit and Conditional Logit in a structured comparison table, synthesize the discrete choice curriculum, and conclude the course.

1. Motivation: Alternative-Specific Regressors

In Lessons 18.1 through 18.4, we explored the standard Multinomial Logit (MNL) model. In MNL, the explanatory variables \(\mathbf{x}_i\) represent individual-specific characteristics (e.g., age, income, education, gender). These covariates are constant across all alternatives for a given person; to explain why people choose different options, MNL estimates different coefficient vectors \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_j\) for each alternative.

In many economic environments, however, the decisive variation lies in the characteristics of the alternatives themselves. Consider:

  • Commuter Mode Choice: An individual chooses among Car, Bus, and Bike. The key determinants are not just the commuter's wage, but the travel time and travel cost of each specific mode.
  • Consumer Product Selection: A shopper chooses among three brands of milk. The choices depend on the shelf price, fat content, and expiry date of each brand.
  • Recreation Site Choice: An angler chooses a lake based on distance to lake, fish stocking density, and boat ramp fees.

When covariates vary across both individuals \(i\) and alternatives \(j\), the standard MNL model is structurally ill-suited. We need McFadden's Conditional Logit (CL) model.

2. Conditional Logit Specification & Choice Probability

The Conditional Logit model re-specifies latent utility such that regressors carry both an individual index \(i\) and an alternative index \(j\):

SPECIFICATION Conditional Logit Utility & Choice Probability
\[ U_{ij} = \mathbf{z}_{ij}'\boldsymbol{\alpha} + \varepsilon_{ij} \] \[ P(y_i = j) = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{z}_{ij}'\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{\sum_{k=1}^J \exp(\mathbf{z}_{ik}'\boldsymbol{\alpha})} \]
WHERE:
\(\mathbf{z}_{ij}\) Vector of alternative-specific regressors varying across both individuals \(i\) and alternatives \(j\) (e.g., travel time \(z_{ij1}\) and travel cost \(z_{ij2}\)).
\(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\) Common parameter vector shared across all alternatives (e.g., the marginal utility of income or the disutility of commute time).
\(\varepsilon_{ij}\) i.i.d. Gumbel (Type I Extreme Value) error terms, leading directly to closed-form logit probabilities.

WHY IS IT CALLED “CONDITIONAL”?

As highlighted on Page 9 of the lecture source: it is called "conditional" because the choice probability is explicitly conditional on the characteristics of the available alternatives in the choice set. If a city opens a new express transit lane that cuts bus travel time by 15 minutes, bus choice probabilities rise automatically across all commuters because \(z_{i, \text{bus}, \text{time}}\) has decreased.

Lecture Travel Mode Choice Application

In the travel mode choice setting with three alternatives (Car, Bus, Bike), the utility specification is:

\[ U_{ij} = \alpha_1 (\text{Time}_{ij}) + \alpha_2 (\text{Cost}_{ij}) + \varepsilon_{ij} \]

Because consumers dislike both transit delay and out-of-pocket expense, economic theory predicts \(\alpha_1 < 0\) and \(\alpha_2 < 0\). The ratio of coefficients yields the classic Value of Travel Time Savings (VTTS):

\[ \text{VTTS} = \frac{\partial U / \partial \text{Time}}{\partial U / \partial \text{Cost}} = \frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2} \quad (\$/\text{minute or } \$/\text{hour}) \]

This single parameter ratio informs billions of dollars in public transit infrastructure and highway investment.

3. Empirical Applications: Marketing, Transportation & Valuation

As noted on Page 10 of the lecture text, Conditional Logit is widely deployed across three major fields of applied quantitative economics:

  • Transportation Economics: Modeling choice of commute mode (car, rail, bus, rideshare), airline itinerary selection, and congestion pricing response.
  • Quantitative Marketing: Analyzing supermarket scanner data where consumers select among competing cereal, yogurt, or soft drink brands whose prices and promotional discounts vary weekly.
  • Environmental & Resource Economics: Non-market valuation via discrete choice experiments (DCEs) and recreational demand models, measuring willingness-to-pay for lake water quality, forest conservation, or renewable energy tariffs.

4. Multinomial Logit vs. Conditional Logit Comparison

Understanding when to employ MNL versus Conditional Logit is a fundamental econometric competency:

Feature Multinomial Logit (MNL) Conditional Logit (CL)
Primary Variation Individual characteristics (people differ) Alternative attributes (options differ)
Regressor Notation \(\mathbf{x}_i\) (constant across \(j\)) \(\mathbf{z}_{ij}\) (varies across \(i\) and \(j\))
Parameter Vector \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_j\) (different per alternative, \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0 = \mathbf{0}\)) \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\) (common across all alternatives)
Number of Slopes \(J \times K\) parameters \(M\) parameters (dimension of \(\mathbf{z}_{ij}\))
Typical Use Case Occupational choice, college major selection Travel mode, supermarket brand choice, housing

7. Quick Knowledge Check

Question: Common Parameter vs. Alternative-Specific Parameters

In a Conditional Logit model of supermarket brand choice, why is the price coefficient \(\alpha_{\text{price}}\) restricted to be common across all brands, whereas in a Multinomial Logit model of occupation choice, the education coefficients \(\beta_{j, \text{educ}}\) are permitted to vary across occupations?

Hint: What varies in each model: the person's characteristics or the option's attributes? Does a dollar have a different marginal utility depending on which brand of milk you buy?

Solution:
In Conditional Logit, price \(z_{ij}\) is an attribute of the option. The parameter \(\alpha_{\text{price}}\) represents the consumer's marginal disutility of spending an extra dollar; economic theory implies that a dollar out-of-pocket exerts the same disutility regardless of which brand is purchased. In MNL, however, education \(x_i\) is an attribute of the person, and different careers reward education at completely different rates (e.g., medical doctor vs waiter), requiring alternative-specific parameters \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_j\).
