LESSON 5.7 Nonlinear Effects & Synthesis

Dummy Variables in Log-Linear Models & Module Review

When the dependent variable is in logarithmic form, a dummy coefficient cannot be read directly as an exact percentage change. Derive the exact exponential transformation \(100[\exp(\beta_2) - 1]\%\), inspect the benchmark example, and synthesize all core concepts across the Module 5 curriculum.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Distinguish between log differences, multiplicative outcome ratios, relative changes, and exact percentage changes.
  • Apply the exact formula \(100[\exp(\beta_2) - 1]\%\) to convert semi-elastic dummy coefficients into exact percentage impacts.
  • Verify that when \(\beta_2 = 0.10\), the exact percentage effect is \(\approx 10.5\%\), exceeding the naive \(10\%\) approximation.
  • Synthesize the complete Module 5 curriculum: from hypothesis testing foundations through Wald/F restrictions to indicator variables.

1. The Four-Stage Sequence: From Log Difference to Percentage

Consider the canonical semi-log specification with an indicator variable: \[ \ln(y_i) = \beta_1 + \beta_2 D_i + \varepsilon_i \] Evaluating group expectations in terms of \(\ln(y)\): \[ \mathbb{E}[\ln y \mid D=1] - \mathbb{E}[\ln y \mid D=0] = (\beta_1 + \beta_2) - \beta_1 = \beta_2 \] While \(\beta_2\) is the exact difference in logarithmic units, practical economic analysis requires the impact on original outcome levels \(y\) (wages, prices, output). To find this effect, follow this structured four-stage conceptual sequence:

Stage 1
LOG DIFFERENCE
\(\Delta \ln y = \beta_2\)

Additive difference in log scale

Stage 2
OUTCOME RATIO
\(\frac{y_1}{y_0} = \exp(\beta_2)\)

Multiplicative level multiplier

Stage 3
RELATIVE CHANGE
\(\frac{y_1 - y_0}{y_0} = \exp(\beta_2) - 1\)

Fractional departure from base

Stage 4
PERCENT CHANGE
\(100[\exp(\beta_2) - 1]\%\)

Exact percentage impact in units of \(y\)

EXACT FORMULA Semi-Logarithmic Indicator Transformation
\[ \%\Delta y = 100 \left[ \exp(\beta_2) - 1 \right]\% \]
VS. NAIVE APPROXIMATION:
Naive Approximation \(100 \beta_2\%\) (first-order Taylor expansion \(\exp(\beta_2) - 1 \approx \beta_2\))
Exact Calculation \(100 [\exp(\beta_2) - 1]\%\) accounts for continuous compounding curvature

Primary Source Numerical Benchmark: \(\beta_2 = 0.10\)

Suppose OLS estimation of \(\ln(\text{wage})\) yields \(\hat{\beta}_2 = 0.10\):

  • Naive Approximation: \(100 \cdot (0.10) = 10.0\%\).
  • Exact Exponential: \(\exp(0.10) \approx 1.10517\).
  • Exact Percentage: \(100 \cdot [1.10517 - 1]\% = \mathbf{10.5\%}\).

The true percentage effect is \(10.5\%\), which exceeds the naive approximation by half a percentage point. For larger coefficients (e.g. \(\beta_2 = 0.40\)), the exact increase is \(\exp(0.40) - 1 \approx 49.2\%\)—meaning the naive \(40\%\) rule misses the mark by over 9 percentage points!

3. Module 5 Synthesis: The Curricular Knowledge Flow

Congratulations on completing Module 5! Below is the integrated conceptual journey linking all seven lessons:

5.1
Hypothesis Foundations: Null \(H_0: \beta_2 = 0\) vs. two-sided alternative \(H_1: \beta_2 \neq 0\); Type I error controlled by \(\alpha = 0.05\) (\(\pm 1.96\)).
5.2
Directional Testing & Power: One-sided 1.645 critical threshold; Type II error rate \(\beta_{\text{err}}\); statistical power \(1 - \beta_{\text{err}}\).
5.3
Linear Restrictions: Matrix unification \(H_0: R\beta = q\); \(R\) is \(J \times K\); \(J\) counts restrictions, \(K\) counts regressors.
5.4
Wald & F Statistics: Scale discrepancy by covariance; \(W \sim \chi^2_J\); finite-sample \(F(J, n-k) = W/J\); loss-of-fit \(R^2\) formulation.
5.5
Binary Indicators: Partition samples with \(D \in \{0, 1\}\); base mean \(\beta_1\); coded mean \(\beta_1 + \beta_2\); \(\beta_2\) is group expectation gap.
5.6
Controls & Categories: Parallel regression lines; \(m - 1\) dummy rule; reference categories; avoiding the Dummy Variable Trap.
5.7
Log-Dummy Transformation: Log difference \(\to\) outcome ratio \(\to\) exact percentage \(100[\exp(\beta_2) - 1]\%\) vs. approximation \(100\beta_2\%\).
TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • In semi-log models \(\ln(y) = \beta_1 + \beta_2 D + \varepsilon\), the coefficient \(\beta_2\) is the difference in logs, not the exact percentage change.
  • The exact percentage change in the level of \(y\) is \(100[\exp(\beta_2) - 1]\%\).
  • For small coefficients (\(|\beta_2| \le 0.10\)), the approximation \(100\beta_2\%\) is acceptable for quick intuition, but for \(\beta_2 = 0.10\), the exact effect is \(10.5\%\).
  • For larger coefficients, using the naive linear approximation causes severe reporting errors. Always apply the exact exponential formula in empirical research!
