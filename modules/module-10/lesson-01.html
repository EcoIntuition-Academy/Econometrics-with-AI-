The First Differences Model

Purging time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity \(c_i\) through adjacent temporal differencing and analyzing the resulting disturbance properties.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Formulate the unobserved effects panel data model \(y_{it} = c_i + \mathbf{x}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \varepsilon_{it}\).
  • Derive the first-difference transformation and prove that \(c_i - c_i = 0\), eliminating time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity.
  • Understand why no assumptions regarding the level of \(c_i\) are required once differencing eliminates the unobserved component.
  • Analyze the differenced disturbance structure \(\Delta \varepsilon_{it}\) and understand how shared underlying errors induce negative serial correlation across adjacent periods.

1. Removing the Time-Invariant Effect

In empirical microeconomics and policy evaluation, panel data track the same cross-sectional units across multiple time periods \(t = 1, 2, \dots, T\).

PANEL DATA SPECIFICATION WITH UNOBSERVED HETEROGENEITY
\[ y_{it} = c_i + \mathbf{x}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \varepsilon_{it} \]

where \(y_{it}\) is the dependent variable for unit \(i\) at time \(t\), \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\) is a vector of time-varying explanatory variables, \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) is the structural coefficient vector, and \(\varepsilon_{it}\) is the idiosyncratic time-varying disturbance. The term \(c_i\) represents unobserved time-invariant individual heterogeneity (e.g., innate ability or fixed geographic features).

If \(c_i\) is correlated with any regressor in \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\), leaving \(c_i\) in the composite error term induces endogeneity (\(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{it}(c_i + \varepsilon_{it})] \neq \mathbf{0}\)), rendering Pooled Ordinary Least Squares inconsistent.

CORE STRATEGY

Subtract the previous period from the current period. Because \(c_i\) is constant over time, it drops out completely: \(c_i - c_i = 0\).

\[ \Delta y_{it} = \Delta \mathbf{x}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \Delta \varepsilon_{it} \]

2. Derivation Journey: The First-Difference Transformation

ALGEBRAIC DERIVATION

Eliminating Unobserved Individual Effects

GOAL: Purge the unobserved, time-invariant individual heterogeneity \(c_i\) to achieve consistent OLS estimation.
1

Specify the Current Period Model

Write the structural linear relation for unit \(i\) at time \(t\):

\[ y_{it} = c_i + \mathbf{x}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \varepsilon_{it} \]
2

Specify the Previous Period Model

Write the relationship for the immediately preceding period \(t-1\):

\[ y_{i,t-1} = c_i + \mathbf{x}_{i,t-1}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \varepsilon_{i,t-1} \]
3

Subtract the Two Equations

Subtract the lagged equation directly from the current equation:

\[ (y_{it} - y_{i,t-1}) = (c_i - c_i) + (\mathbf{x}_{it} - \mathbf{x}_{i,t-1})'\boldsymbol{\beta} + (\varepsilon_{it} - \varepsilon_{i,t-1}) \]
4

Cancellation: \(c_i - c_i = 0\)

Because \(c_i\) does not change across periods, \(c_i - c_i = 0\). Using difference notation \(\Delta\):

\[ \Delta y_{it} = \Delta \mathbf{x}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \Delta \varepsilon_{it} \]
KEY RESULT
The unobserved individual effect \(c_i\) has dropped out completely from the differenced equation.
BIG PICTURE Identical Terms Cancel

First differencing removes every component that is constant over time for a given unit. Once \(c_i\) is eliminated through differencing, assumptions directly concerning the level of \(c_i\) are no longer needed for estimating the differenced equation in the same way as models that leave it in the composite error.

3. The Differenced Disturbance & Induced Serial Correlation

While first differencing purges the unobserved heterogeneity \(c_i\), it fundamentally alters the dynamic error structure. Let us examine adjacent differenced disturbance terms:

Differenced Error in Period \(t\)
\(\Delta \varepsilon_{it} = \mathbf{+\varepsilon_{it}} - \varepsilon_{i,t-1}\)

Contains the current shock \(\mathbf{+\varepsilon_{it}}\) with positive sign.

Differenced Error in Period \(t+1\)
\(\Delta \varepsilon_{i,t+1} = \varepsilon_{i,t+1} \mathbf{-\varepsilon_{it}}\)

Contains the exact same shock \(\mathbf{-\varepsilon_{it}}\) with negative sign!

The Source Intuition for Negative Serial Correlation

The differenced error terms can become negatively serially correlated because adjacent differences contain the same original disturbance \(\varepsilon_{it}\) with opposite signs (\(+\varepsilon_{it}\) in \(\Delta \varepsilon_{it}\) and \(-\varepsilon_{it}\) in \(\Delta \varepsilon_{i,t+1}\)). If the original \(\varepsilon_{it}\) are i.i.d. white noise: \[ \text{Cov}(\Delta \varepsilon_{it}, \Delta \varepsilon_{i,t+1}) = \mathbb{E}[(\varepsilon_{it} - \varepsilon_{i,t-1})(\varepsilon_{i,t+1} - \varepsilon_{it})] = -\sigma_\varepsilon^2 < 0 \] yielding an autocorrelation of \(\text{Corr}(\Delta \varepsilon_{it}, \Delta \varepsilon_{i,t+1}) = -0.5\). Therefore, standard OLS variance formulas after differencing require adjustment.

WHY IT MATTERS

Even if \(c_i\) is massively correlated with \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\), differencing physically removes \(c_i\) from the estimation equation.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

Differencing purges time-invariant confounders at the cost of sacrificing \(N \times 1\) observations (the baseline period \(t=1\) is lost).

TRY THIS

QUICK CHECK

Why does first differencing eliminate \(c_i\)?

HINT: Consider whether \(c_i\) varies over time or stays constant across adjacent periods \(t\) and \(t-1\).

ANSWER: Because \(c_i\) is time-invariant for each unit \(i\).

WHY? When subtracting period \(t-1\) from period \(t\), the term becomes \(c_i - c_i = 0\), completely removing the unobserved effect regardless of whether it is correlated with explanatory variables.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • The panel model separates the time-invariant unit effect \(c_i\) from the idiosyncratic error \(\varepsilon_{it}\).
  • First differencing subtracts adjacent observations and eliminates \(c_i\) because it is constant over time.
  • The resulting model is:
    \[\Delta y_{it} = \Delta \mathbf{x}_{it}'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \Delta \varepsilon_{it}\]
  • Adjacent differenced disturbances can become serially correlated because they share an underlying error term with opposite signs.
