First Differences vs Fixed Effects

Comparing within-group demeaning with adjacent differencing, establishing the \(T=2\) equivalence theorem, and evaluating consistency across panel estimators.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Distinguish the identifying variation used by the Fixed Effects (Within) transformation versus the First Differences (FD) transformation.
  • Prove that FD and FE yield mathematically identical coefficient estimates when \(T = 2\).
  • Understand why the estimators diverge when \(T > 2\) and recognize that relative efficiency depends on error autocorrelation.
  • Master the comprehensive panel data model summary comparing Pooled OLS, Random Effects, Fixed Effects, and First Differences under alternative endogeneity conditions.
1

Two Alternative Transformations to Eliminate \(c_i\)

Both the Fixed Effects (within) model and the First Differences model share the exact same primary objective: eliminating the time-invariant unobserved individual effect \(c_i\) without requiring strong orthogonality assumptions. However, they achieve this through fundamentally distinct algebraic operations.

TRANSFORMATION A

Fixed Effects (Within Transformation)

“Compare each observation with the unit's time average.”

\[ (y_{it} - \bar{y}_i) = (\mathbf{x}_{it} - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_i)'\boldsymbol{\beta} + (\varepsilon_{it} - \bar{\varepsilon}_i) \]

Relies on deviations from the unit-specific time mean \(\bar{y}_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T y_{it}\). Uses all time periods simultaneously to construct the benchmark.

TRANSFORMATION B

First Differences (FD Transformation)

“Compare each observation with the immediately previous period.”

\[ (y_{it} - y_{i,t-1}) = (\mathbf{x}_{it} - \mathbf{x}_{i,t-1})'\boldsymbol{\beta} + (\varepsilon_{it} - \varepsilon_{i,t-1}) \]

Relies on changes from the previous period. Uses purely local, adjacent temporal variation (\(t\) vs \(t-1\)).

2

The \(T=2\) Equivalence & Divergence When \(T > 2\)

KEY SOURCE RESULT

The First Difference estimator and the Fixed Effects estimator produce the exact same coefficient estimates when \(T = 2\). When \(T > 2\), the models diverge and yield different estimates because they exploit different variation in the data.

Why Are They Identical When \(T = 2\)?

With two time periods (\(t=1, 2\)), the individual time average for any variable \(w_{it}\) is simply \(\bar{w}_i = \frac{w_{i1} + w_{i2}}{2}\). The demeaned values in period 1 and period 2 are:

\[ \begin{aligned} w_{i1} - \bar{w}_i &= w_{i1} - \frac{w_{i1} + w_{i2}}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}(w_{i2} - w_{i1}) = -\frac{1}{2}\Delta w_{i2} \\ w_{i2} - \bar{w}_i &= w_{i2} - \frac{w_{i1} + w_{i2}}{2} = +\frac{1}{2}(w_{i2} - w_{i1}) = +\frac{1}{2}\Delta w_{i2} \end{aligned} \]

Both demeaned observations are exact linear scalar multiples of the single first difference \(\Delta w_{i2}\). Consequently, the OLS slope computed on mean-deviated data is mathematically identical to the OLS slope computed on differenced data: \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{FE}} \equiv \widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{FD}}\).

Why Do They Diverge When \(T > 2\)?

Consider \(T = 4\):

FE Identification (T = 4)

Compares every observation against the 4-period mean: \((y_{i1} - \bar{y}_i), (y_{i2} - \bar{y}_i), (y_{i3} - \bar{y}_i), (y_{i4} - \bar{y}_i)\). Every data point is anchored to the global center of unit \(i\).

FD Identification (T = 4)

Forms 3 adjacent transitions: \((y_{i2} - y_{i1})\), \((y_{i3} - y_{i2})\), and \((y_{i4} - y_{i3})\). Ignores distances from the global mean and focuses exclusively on period-to-period velocity.

Summary of Panel Data Models

The table below synthesizes the core properties of primary panel estimators under alternative relationships between unobserved heterogeneity \(c_i\) and regressors \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\), directly from the source lecture notes:

Model Run Key Condition on \(c_i\) When \(c_i\) Uncorrelated with \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\) When \(c_i\) Correlated with \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\)
Pooled OLS Requires \(c_i\) uncorrelated with \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\) Consistent Inconsistent
Random Effects (GLS) Requires \(\mathbb{E}[c_i \mid \mathbf{x}_{it}] = 0\) Consistent & Efficient Inconsistent
Fixed Effects (Within, LSDV) Allows \(c_i\) arbitrarily correlated with \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\) Consistent Consistent†
First Differences (FD) Allows \(c_i\) arbitrarily correlated with \(\mathbf{x}_{it}\) Consistent Consistent†
† Crucial Source Note on Relative Efficiency: Whether Fixed Effects or First Differences is more efficient when \(T > 2\) depends strictly on the serial correlation structure of the idiosyncratic disturbances \(\varepsilon_{it}\):
  • If \(\varepsilon_{it}\) are serially uncorrelated, Fixed Effects is more efficient because within demeaning preserves spherical error properties better than differencing.
  • If \(\varepsilon_{it}\) follow a random walk (\(\varepsilon_{it} = \varepsilon_{i,t-1} + u_{it}\)), First Differences is more efficient because differencing purges the unit root, leaving i.i.d. innovations \(\Delta \varepsilon_{it} = u_{it}\).
There is NO universal ranking stating that FE is always superior or FD is always superior.

WHAT TO NOTICE

In FE, there are 4 points centered on the origin. In FD, there are only 3 points because 1 observation is lost to form differences (\(T-1\)).

Both methods eliminate \(c_i\), but with \(T > 2\), the distance of a point from the 4-period mean is not the same as its adjacent change from the previous year.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

FD and FE estimate identical slopes at \(T=2\). At \(T > 2\), differing point estimates reflect their distinct weighting of short-run versus medium-run variations.

5

Concept Check

CHECK 10.2 Equivalence Theorem

Under what exact time-dimension condition are First Differences (FD) and Fixed Effects (FE) coefficient estimates guaranteed to be mathematically identical?

Hint: Think about the smallest number of time periods required to compute both a difference and a mean.
ANSWER:

They are identical when \(T = 2\).

WHY?

With only two time periods, the deviation from the two-period mean is exactly half of the first difference: \((w_{i2} - \bar{w}_i) = \frac{1}{2}(w_{i2} - w_{i1})\). Because demeaned data is a constant scalar multiple of differenced data, the OLS regression slope is identical. When \(T > 2\), FE uses all time periods to demean while FD uses adjacent pairs, causing the estimates to diverge.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • Alternative Transformations: FE removes \(c_i\) by subtracting the unit time mean (\(x_{it} - \bar{x}_i\)); FD removes \(c_i\) by subtracting the adjacent period (\(x_{it} - x_{i,t-1}\)).
  • T = 2 Equivalence: With two periods, demeaning and differencing contain equivalent identifying variation, yielding identical point estimates \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{FE}} \equiv \widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{FD}}\).
  • T > 2 Divergence: For \(T > 2\), FE and FD use different linear combinations of the data and yield different point estimates.
  • Context-Dependent Efficiency: FE is more efficient if errors \(\varepsilon_{it}\) are serially uncorrelated; FD is more efficient if errors follow a unit root / random walk. Neither estimator is universally superior.
