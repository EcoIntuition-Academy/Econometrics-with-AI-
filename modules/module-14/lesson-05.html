LESSON 14.5 Local Effects

LATE, Monotonicity & the Wald Estimator

When randomized offers induce only a subset of participants to take treatment, instrumental variable methods scale the Intent-to-Treat effect by the first-stage compliance rate to uncover the Local Average Treatment Effect.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define the Monotonicity condition and explain why ruling out defiers allows causal attribution.
  • Define the Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE) as the treatment effect for compliers.
  • Calculate the Wald estimator step-by-step from observed group means and compliance rates.
  • State and evaluate the three core IV assumptions: Random Assignment, Exclusion Restriction, and Monotonicity.

1. Monotonicity & The Complier Subpopulation

Random assignment \(Z_i\) determines who is offered treatment, but imperfect compliance means treatment receipt \(T_i\) is not guaranteed. To learn the effect of treatment itself, we require that the assignment offer acts weakly in one direction.

CORE IDENTIFYING ASSUMPTION
Monotonicity (No Defiers)

Monotonicity assumes that being offered treatment can only weakly increase, never decrease, the likelihood of taking treatment: \[ T_i(1) \ge T_i(0) \quad \text{for all } i \] This explicitly rules out the existence of Defiers (\(T_i(0)=1, T_i(1)=0\)).

Control Arm (\(Z = 0\))
Always-takers: \(T = 1\)
Compliers: \(T = 0\)
Never-takers: \(T = 0\)
Treated Arm (\(Z = 1\))
Always-takers: \(T = 1\)
Compliers: \(T = 1\) (Switched!)
Never-takers: \(T = 0\)
CAUSAL ESTIMAND
Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE)

LATE is the average treatment effect for the subpopulation of Compliers whose treatment status is induced or shifted by the randomized assignment / instrument: \[ \text{LATE} = E[Y_i(1) - Y_i(0) \mid \text{Complier}] \]

2. Derivation Journey: Numerical LATE Example

Consider a concrete trial with 100 control subjects (\(Z=0\)) and 100 treatment-assigned subjects (\(Z=1\)), containing 30 Always-takers, 25 Compliers, 45 Never-takers, and 0 Defiers.

DERIVATION JOURNEY · THE WALD RATIO
Step 1: Calculate Intent-to-Treat (ITT)

Difference in outcome means between assigned arms:

\[ \text{ITT} = E[Y \mid Z = 1] - E[Y \mid Z = 0] = 65 - 60 = 5 \]
Step 2: Calculate First-Stage Compliance Shift

Difference in treatment take-up rates between assigned arms:

\[ \text{First Stage} = E[T \mid Z = 1] - E[T \mid Z = 0] = 0.55 - 0.30 = 0.25 \]
Step 3: Compute the Wald Estimator

Scale the ITT outcome shift by the proportion of participants who actually took treatment:

\[ \hat{\beta}_{\text{LATE}} = \frac{\text{ITT}}{\text{First Stage}} = \frac{5}{0.25} = 20 \]
Key Result: For compliers, receiving treatment increases the outcome by 20 units. The overall ITT was diluted to 5 because compliers represent only 25% of the total sample.
WALD ESTIMATOR FORMULA Binary Instrument & Treatment
\[ \hat{\beta}_{\text{LATE}} = \frac{E[Y \mid Z = 1] - E[Y \mid Z = 0]}{E[T \mid Z = 1] - E[T \mid Z = 0]} \]

The Wald estimator expresses the instrumental variables estimator with a single binary instrument \(Z\) and a single binary endogenous treatment \(T\). The numerator captures the reduced-form ITT effect, and the denominator captures the first-stage compliance rate.

1. RANDOM ASSIGNMENT

\(Z_i\) is independent of potential outcomes and compliance types: \(Z_i \perp (Y_i(0), Y_i(1), T_i(0), T_i(1))\).

2. EXCLUSION RESTRICTION

\(Z_i\) affects \(Y_i\) solely through treatment \(T_i\): no direct pathway exists from offer to outcome.

3. MONOTONICITY

No defiers: assignment offer only encourages treatment participation (\(T_i(1) \ge T_i(0)\)).

SCALING FACTOR

Dividing by \(\Delta T\) scales the assignment effect up to reflect full treatment.

WEAK IV DANGER

If \(\Delta T \approx 0\), the ratio explodes and standard errors diverge.

COMPLIER TARGET

LATE applies specifically to compliers, not necessarily always- or never-takers.

4. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Why does the Wald estimator divide the Intent-to-Treat (ITT) effect by the first-stage effect?

HINT: Consider who generated the ITT outcome difference, and what fraction of the sample they comprise.

ANSWER: Because ITT reflects the outcome impact of randomized assignment, while the first stage measures how much assignment changes actual treatment receipt.

WHY? Under random assignment, exclusion restriction, and monotonicity, non-compliers contribute zero to the ITT; dividing by the first stage scales the assignment effect back up to recover the genuine causal treatment effect for compliers.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Monotonicity assumes no defiers exist, ensuring the randomized offer only moves individuals into treatment, never out of it.
  • Always-takers and Never-takers exhibit zero behavioral response to the offer, meaning any ITT difference is caused entirely by Compliers.
  • The Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE) identifies the average treatment impact strictly for the complier subpopulation.
  • The Wald estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{LATE}} = \frac{E[Y|Z=1] - E[Y|Z=0]}{E[T|Z=1] - E[T|Z=0]}\) computes LATE from observed sample means.
  • Three core assumptions are required for valid LATE identification: Random Assignment, Exclusion Restriction, and Monotonicity.
