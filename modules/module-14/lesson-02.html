LESSON 14.2 Estimation

Estimating Experimental Effects: ITT, Controls & Repeated Outcomes

Randomized treatment offers identify the Intent-to-Treat (ITT) effect directly via reduced-form regressions. Learn how to specify models with multiple treatment arms, incorporate pre-experiment covariates, model multi-period outcomes via Difference-in-Differences, and cluster standard errors over time.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Specify the reduced-form Intent-to-Treat (ITT) regression model \(Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_i + \beta_2 x_i + \varepsilon_i\).
  • Explain why ITT preserves randomization validity even when compliance is imperfect.
  • Formulate multi-arm experimental regressions relative to an omitted baseline category.
  • Estimate treatment-time interaction models across pre, during, and post treatment periods.
  • Apply the within-unit clustering warning when analyzing repeated observations over time.

1. The Intent-to-Treat (ITT) Specification

CORE DEFINITION

Intent-to-Treat (ITT) Effect

The Intent-to-Treat (ITT) effect measures the average difference in outcomes between those randomly assigned (or offered) treatment and those not offered treatment, regardless of whether participants actually comply with their assignment.

Because randomization directly determines treatment assignment \(Z_i\), the assignment dummy is strictly uncorrelated with the disturbance term \(\varepsilon_i\):

EXPERIMENTAL REDUCED FORM (ITT REGRESSION)
$$Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_i + \beta_2 x_i + \varepsilon_i$$
where \(Z_i = 1\) indicates assignment to treatment, \(Z_i = 0\) indicates control, and \(x_i\) represents pre-treatment control variables. The coefficient \(\beta_1\) captures the ITT effect.
PRECISION BENEFIT

Role of Pre-Experiment Controls

Including pre-treatment covariates \(x_i\) that explain variation in the outcome lowers the residual error variance \(\sigma^2_{\varepsilon}\), reduces coefficient standard errors, and increases statistical power without biasing \(\beta_1\).

2. Multiple Treatment Arms

When an experiment tests several distinct interventions (e.g., cash transfer vs. in-kind voucher), researchers add separate dummy variables for each arm:

MULTI-ARM EXPERIMENTAL SPECIFICATION
$$Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_{1i} + \beta_2 Z_{2i} + \beta_3 x_i + \varepsilon_i$$
One group is omitted as the baseline reference category (control). Coefficient \(\beta_1\) evaluates Treatment 1 against control, and \(\beta_2\) evaluates Treatment 2 against control.

3. Repeated Observations & Treatment-Time Interactions

When participants are observed both before and during treatment, the experiment can be analyzed using a Difference-in-Differences interaction specification:

TWO-PERIOD DID INTERACTION MODEL
$$Y_{it} = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_i + \beta_2 \text{intreat}_t + \beta_3(Z_i \cdot \text{intreat}_t) + \beta_4 x_i + \varepsilon_{it}$$
where \(\text{intreat}_t = 1\) during the treatment period and \(0\) before. The interaction coefficient \(\beta_3\) captures the differential treatment effect over time.

When data span three periods—pre-treatment, in-treatment, and post-treatment—the model incorporates two time indicators and two interaction terms:

THREE-PERIOD DID INTERACTION MODEL
$$Y_{it} = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_i + \beta_2 \text{intreat}_t + \beta_3(Z_i \cdot \text{intreat}_t) + \beta_4 \text{post}_t + \beta_5(Z_i \cdot \text{post}_t) + \beta_6 x_i + \varepsilon_{it}$$
Omitted reference category: control group in the pre-treatment period.

Coefficient Interpretation Map

\(\alpha\): Control group mean in pre-treatment period.
\(\beta_1\): Pre-treatment difference (expected 0 by randomization).
\(\beta_2\): Common control change from pre to in-treatment.
\(\beta_3\): Treatment effect during the active intervention.
\(\beta_4\): Common control change from pre to post-treatment.
\(\beta_5\): Post-treatment effect for the treated group.
COMPARATIVE TRAJECTORY EXPRESSION
$$[(\alpha + \beta_1 + \beta_4 + \beta_5) - (\alpha + \beta_1)] - [(\alpha + \beta_2) - \alpha] = \beta_5 - \beta_2$$

This expression isolates the treatment group's post-treatment change relative to the control group's in-treatment change. Even if baseline differences exist (\(\beta_1 \neq 0\)), difference-in-differences controls for them, isolating true treatment-time dynamics.

4. Within-Unit Serial Correlation & Clustering

INFERENCE WARNING

Repeated Observations Are Not Independent by Default

When the same units (individuals, firms, schools) are observed repeatedly over time, the disturbance term \(\varepsilon_{it}\) is almost certainly correlated across time within each unit.

Failing to cluster standard errors by unit can drastically underestimate standard errors, leading to severely inflated false-positive rates and misleadingly narrow confidence intervals.

6. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

What does the Intent-to-Treat (ITT) effect measure in a randomized experiment?

HINT: Consider whether ITT groups participants by their assigned treatment offer or by their actual take-up.

ANSWER: It measures the average difference in outcomes by randomized treatment assignment or offer, not by whether treatment was actually taken.

WHY? Randomization directly dictates the assignment indicator \(Z_i\), ensuring that \(Z_i\) is orthogonal to unobserved potential outcome confounders. Comparing outcomes across \(Z\) provides an unbiased estimate of the policy effect of making the intervention available, even when some participants fail to comply.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The ITT regression \(Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_i + \beta_2 x_i + \varepsilon_i\) evaluates the causal effect of treatment assignment.
  • Pre-treatment covariates absorb outcome variance and shrink standard errors without biasing \(\beta_1\).
  • Multi-arm trials specify dummy indicators for each active treatment relative to an omitted baseline control.
  • Treatment-time interaction models (\(Z_i \cdot \text{intreat}_t\)) isolate differential changes across experimental phases.
  • Repeated observations over time require cluster-robust standard errors to prevent false-positive inflation.
