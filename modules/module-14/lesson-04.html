LESSON 14.4 Compliance

Noncompliance, Treatment Assignment & Compliance Types

In economic field trials, researchers randomize treatment offers, but participants choose whether to comply. Understanding noncompliance bridges experimental trials and instrumental variables.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Distinguish strictly between randomized treatment assignment \(Z\) and actual treatment receipt \(T\).
  • Explain why Intent-to-Treat (ITT) measures the effect of an offer rather than the effect of taking the treatment.
  • Define the four potential compliance types: Always-takers, Compliers, Never-takers, and Defiers.
  • Construct the potential treatment status table across assignment states \(Z \in \{0, 1\}\).

1. Treatment Assignment \(Z\) vs. Treatment Receipt \(T\)

In controlled laboratory settings, researchers can often force treatment delivery. In economic field settings, individuals have agency: they can decline free job training, skip doctor appointments, or purchase microloans from alternate lenders.

EXOGENOUS INSTRUMENT
Assignment \(Z_i \in \{0, 1\}\)

Whether participant \(i\) was randomly assigned or offered the treatment. This variable is strictly controlled by the researcher and guaranteed independent of potential outcomes by randomization.

ENDOGENOUS CHOICE
Receipt \(T_i \in \{0, 1\}\)

Whether participant \(i\) actually received or participated in the treatment. Because participants decide whether to comply with their assignment, \(T_i\) is endogenous and self-selected.

REDUCED-FORM ITT REGRESSION Assignment Effect
\[ Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 Z_i + \varepsilon_i \]

\(\beta_1\) identifies the effect of being offered / assigned treatment: \(E[Y_i \mid Z_i = 1] - E[Y_i \mid Z_i = 0]\). When noncompliance is present, \(Z_i \neq T_i\) for some subjects, meaning \(\beta_1\) does not capture the biological or direct economic impact of actually receiving treatment.

INTUITION · THE COOKIE ANALOGY

Offering a child a cookie (\(Z = 1\)) does not guarantee they eat the cookie (\(T = 1\)). Some children love cookies and sneak one even when not offered; others refuse cookies no matter what; and some eat only when offered. Measuring happiness by offer (\(Z\)) tells you the value of the gesture; measuring happiness by eating (\(T\)) requires accounting for who complied.

2. The Four Compliance Types

Under the potential outcomes framework, every individual possesses two potential treatment receipt values: \(T_i(1)\) (treatment taken if assigned \(Z=1\)) and \(T_i(0)\) (treatment taken if assigned \(Z=0\)). This generates four distinct behavioral profiles:

ALWAYS-TAKER

Takes treatment regardless of assignment state:

\(T_i(0) = 1, \quad T_i(1) = 1\)
COMPLIER

Complies strictly with assignment offer:

\(T_i(0) = 0, \quad T_i(1) = 1\)
NEVER-TAKER

Refuses treatment under any offer:

\(T_i(0) = 0, \quad T_i(1) = 0\)
DEFIER

Does the opposite of assignment (ruled out):

\(T_i(0) = 1, \quad T_i(1) = 0\)
Field Experiment Example: College Scholarship Offer

Randomized offer \(Z = 1\) represents a tuition scholarship; \(T = 1\) represents college enrollment:

Always-Taker: Attends college regardless of financial aid.
Complier: Attends college if and only if offered the scholarship.
Never-Taker: Enters family trade or military; never attends college.
Defier: Attends only if denied aid (assumed nonexistent).
Compliance Type If Control (\(Z = 0\)) If Treated (\(Z = 1\)) Behavioral Response to Offer
Always-taker \(T = 1\) \(T = 1\) No change in status (\(\Delta = 0\))
Complier \(T = 0\) \(T = 1\) Switches into treatment (\(\Delta = +1\))
Never-taker \(T = 0\) \(T = 0\) No change in status (\(\Delta = 0\))
Defier \(T = 1\) \(T = 0\) Switches out of treatment (\(\Delta = -1\))

First-Stage Insight: Under monotonicity (no defiers), the first-stage difference in treatment take-up E[T|Z=1] − E[T|Z=0] = 0.55 − 0.30 = 0.25 exactly equals the population proportion of compliers (25.0%). The randomized offer successfully induced 25.0% of the population into treatment.
CONTROL TAKE-UP

In the unassigned arm, only Always-takers access treatment: \(E[T \mid Z=0] = \pi_{\text{AT}}\).

TREATED TAKE-UP

In the offered arm, Always-takers and Compliers participate: \(E[T \mid Z=1] = \pi_{\text{AT}} + \pi_{\text{C}}\).

FIRST-STAGE SHIFT

Subtracting the two arms cancels \(\pi_{\text{AT}}\), cleanly isolating the complier share \(\pi_{\text{C}}\).

NEVER-TAKERS

Contribute zero take-up in both arms, mechanically diluting the aggregate ITT effect.

GATEWAY TO LATE

This first-stage take-up shift forms the exact denominator of the Wald instrumental variable estimator.

4. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Who changes treatment status because of randomized assignment under monotonicity?

HINT: Consider which subgroup has \(T=0\) under control but switches to \(T=1\) when offered treatment.

ANSWER: Compliers.

WHY? Under monotonicity (no defiers), Always-takers take treatment under both conditions (\(T=1\)) and Never-takers reject treatment under both conditions (\(T=0\)). Only Compliers switch status in response to the randomized offer (\(T=0 \to T=1\)).

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Randomized assignment \(Z_i\) is exogenous, but actual treatment receipt \(T_i\) is endogenous whenever noncompliance occurs.
  • ITT regression identifies the causal effect of being offered treatment, not necessarily the biological or direct effect of receiving it.
  • The population divides into four potential compliance types: Always-takers, Compliers, Never-takers, and Defiers.
  • Always-takers and Never-takers exhibit invariant treatment status across arms, meaning assignment offers create zero behavior change for them.
  • The first-stage difference in treatment take-up \(E[T|Z=1] - E[T|Z=0]\) isolates the fraction of compliers in the population under monotonicity.
