LESSON 14.3 Research Integrity

Preregistration & Multiple Hypothesis Testing

Credible experimental science requires locking statistical specifications before inspecting data realizations and adjusting inference when evaluating multiple outcomes or subgroups simultaneously.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define preregistration and explain how it separates planned confirmatory analyses from post-hoc exploratory searching.
  • Understand how p-hacking and researcher degrees of freedom degrade nominal statistical significance.
  • Trace the accumulation of false-positive (Type I) risk when testing multiple outcomes or subgroups.
  • Apply the Bonferroni adjustment and evaluate Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) control principles.

1. Why Preregister Experimental Analyses?

In an ideal trial, every analytical choice is made to address the underlying causal question rather than to locate an artificially low p-value. In practice, researchers encounter dozens of subtle discretionary choices: which outcomes to highlight, which control variables to insert, which subgroups to split, and how to define treatment windows.

FORMAL DEFINITION
Preregistration

Preregistration is the formal public documentation of a study's primary hypotheses, outcome variables, sample selection rules, and exact estimation strategy prior to observing the realization of experimental outcomes.

DATA-DRIVEN SEARCH

Running scores of regression variants until one yields a statistically significant coefficient creates biased, unreplicable empirical claims.

p-HACKING RISK

When researchers selectively report only specifications crossing \(p < 0.05\), nominal significance levels lose their probabilistic meaning.

CLEAR RESEARCH DESIGN

A pre-analysis plan holds researchers accountable by clearly distinguishing confirmatory tests planned in advance from exploratory post-hoc findings.

INTUITION · THE PUZZLE ANALOGY

Solving a jigsaw puzzle requires placing pieces where they naturally belong according to the picture on the box. If you carve and rearrange the pieces after opening the box until they arbitrarily fit your favorite pattern, you have not solved the puzzle—you have manufactured an illusion. Preregistering the analysis plan is writing down the assembly rules before opening the box.

Public Trial Registry: Economics experiments frequently archive pre-analysis plans in the Social Science Registry (the AEA RCT Registry), providing an immutable, timestamped record of primary endpoints before data collection concludes.

2. The Multiple Hypothesis Testing Problem

Field trials often gather extensive survey instruments capturing hundreds of individual indicators—educational test scores, attendance rates, household expenditures, health metrics, and behavioral choices across diverse demographic subsets.

1 HYPOTHESIS TESTED

Probability of false discovery under a true null at \(\alpha = 0.05\):

\(\Pr(\text{False Discovery}) = 0.05\)
20 INDEPENDENT HYPOTHESES

Probability of finding at least one false discovery across 20 true nulls:

\(1 - (1 - 0.05)^{20} \approx 64.2\%\)

Evaluating multiple outcomes or conducting extensive subgroup analyses without formal statistical adjustment dramatically increases the probability of reporting chance noise as a breakthrough treatment effect.

ERROR-RATE CONTROL Bonferroni Adjustment
\[ \alpha_{\text{adjusted}} = \frac{\alpha_{\text{target}}}{m} \]

Where \(m\) represents the total number of tested hypotheses and \(\alpha_{\text{target}}\) is the desired overall Family-Wise Error Rate (typically 0.05). By requiring each individual hypothesis to meet this stricter threshold, the probability of committing any Type I error across all \(m\) tests remains strictly bounded by \(\alpha_{\text{target}}\).

NOMINAL ALPHA

Applies only to a single standalone statistical hypothesis test in isolation.

FWER INFLATION

The probability of at least one Type I error scales exponentially with test count: \(1 - (1-\alpha)^m\).

BONFERRONI BOUND

Divides nominal alpha by the test count \(m\) to cap aggregate false rejections strictly.

PREREGISTRATION RESTRICTION

Designating primary vs. exploratory outcomes in advance restricts \(m\), preserving power.

REPLICABILITY

Disciplined multi-testing corrections ensure published findings reflect real causal mechanisms.

4. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Why can testing many experimental outcome variables create misleadingly significant empirical findings?

HINT: Think about what happens when you roll a 20-sided die 20 times—even with a 5% chance per roll, rolling at least one 20 becomes very likely.

ANSWER: Because repeated testing increases the chance that at least one outcome appears statistically significant due to random noise alone.

WHY? Each individual test has a 5% false rejection rate under the null. When 20 outcomes are tested, the chance of at least one accidental false positive rises to approximately 64%. Preregistering primary endpoints or applying Bonferroni corrections controls the aggregate Family-Wise Error Rate.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Preregistration defines hypotheses, variables, and estimation strategies before observing data realization, separating confirmatory tests from exploratory search.
  • p-Hacking and researcher degrees of freedom degrade nominal significance levels by opportunistically selecting favorable specifications.
  • The AEA Social Science Registry provides an immutable public repository for archiving pre-analysis plans before field implementation.
  • Evaluating multiple outcomes without correction inflates the Family-Wise Error Rate, creating high probabilities of accidental false discoveries.
  • The Bonferroni correction caps aggregate false discovery risk by requiring each test to surpass a stricter threshold \(\alpha / m\).
