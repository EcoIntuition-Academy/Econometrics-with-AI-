LESSON 3.1 Explained vs Residual Variation

Goodness of Fit & R-Squared

Discover how the total variation in an economic outcome is partitioned into explained and residual components, derive \(R^2\) through summations and projection matrix \(M_0\), and understand why strong in-sample fit never implies causal identification.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Define goodness of fit in linear regression as the predictive power of regressor deviations.
  • Follow the step-by-step summation derivation of the sum-of-squares decomposition \(\text{SST} = \text{SSR} + \text{SSE}\).
  • Derive the matrix formulation of \(R^2\) using the demeaning residual-maker matrix \(M_0\).
  • Distinguish total, explained, and residual sum of squares geometrically and algebraically.
  • Explain why a high \(R^2\) measures predictive association within sample but does not prove causality.
📌 Notation Note

The source notes sometimes denote the OLS estimate by \(b\). Throughout this website, we write the OLS estimate as \(\hat{\beta}\) and reserve \(b\) for a generic candidate coefficient vector. We write \(\beta\) for the true population coefficient, \(\hat{y} = X\hat{\beta}\) for fitted values, \(e = y - \hat{y}\) for sample residuals, and \(\varepsilon\) for population disturbances.

1. The Fundamental Question of Goodness of Fit

Goodness of fit evaluates how effectively variation in the explanatory variables \(X\) accounts for variation in the dependent outcome \(y\) around its sample mean.

📌 Source Definition: Goodness of Fit

Goodness of Fit: How good is variation in \(X\) at predicting variation in \(y\)?

The most widespread metric is the coefficient of determination, denoted \(R^2\). Intuitively, \(R^2\) measures how well deviations of the regressors \(x_i\) from their sample means predict deviations of the dependent variable \(y_i\) from its sample mean \(\bar{y}\).

Intuition Before Algebra: Partitioning an Individual Deviation

Suppose a student's test score \(y_i\) differs from the class average \(\bar{y}\). Why does this difference occur? Part of that difference may be captured by the regression model (study hours, attendance), while the rest remains unexplained (individual mood, exam idiosyncrasies).

2. Sum-of-Squares Decomposition: Summation Approach

To determine how total variation partitions across the full sample of \(n\) observations, we square and aggregate the individual deviation identity using OLS residual properties.

Mathematical Derivation

Sum-of-Squares Variance Decomposition

What are we trying to show?

Prove that under OLS with an intercept, total centered sample variation (\(\text{SST}\)) cleanly splits into explained variation (\(\text{SSR}\)) plus residual variation (\(\text{SSE}\)), with zero cross-term.

1

Start with the OLS fitted-value decomposition

Express each individual observation in terms of its OLS fitted value \(\hat{y}_i = x_i'\hat{\beta}\) and sample residual \(e_i\):

\(y_i = \hat{y}_i + e_i = x_i'\hat{\beta} + e_i\)
Why this step works By definition of the OLS residual, every observation equals its fitted value plus its residual: \(e_i \equiv y_i - \hat{y}_i\).
2

Center around the sample mean

Subtract the sample mean \(\bar{y}\) from both sides of the identity:

\(y_i - \bar{y} = (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y}) + e_i\)
Why this step works With an intercept included in \(X\), the sample mean of OLS residuals is zero (\(\bar{e} = 0\)), which guarantees that the average fitted value equals the average outcome: \(\bar{\hat{y}} = \bar{y}\).
3

Square both sides and sum across all observations

Aggregate squared centered deviations across the entire sample \(i = 1, \dots, n\):

\(\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[(\hat{y}_i - \bar{y}) + e_i\right]^2\)
Why this step works Summing squared deviations measures total sample dispersion (proportional to sample variance) of \(y\) around its mean.
4

Expand the binomial expression

Expand \((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\) for each summand:

\(\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y})^2 + 2\sum_{i=1}^n (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y})e_i + \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2\)
Why this step works Algebraic expansion creates three distinct components: explained squared deviations, unexplained squared residuals, and a cross-product interaction term.
5

Eliminate the cross-product term via OLS orthogonality

Key Result

Analyze the interaction summation using \(\hat{y}_i = x_i'\hat{\beta}\):

\(2\sum_{i=1}^n (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y})e_i = 2\hat{\beta}'\sum_{i=1}^n x_i e_i - 2\bar{y}\sum_{i=1}^n e_i = 2\hat{\beta}'\mathbf{0} - 2\bar{y}(0) = 0\)
Why this step works OLS normal equations enforce regressor-residual orthogonality (\(X'e = \mathbf{0} \implies \sum x_i e_i = \mathbf{0}\)). Furthermore, when an intercept is included, \(\sum e_i = 0\). Thus the cross-product vanishes identically.
6

Conclude the fundamental sum-of-squares identity

Fundamental Theorem

With the cross-term eliminated, total variation equals explained plus unexplained variation:

\(\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y})^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2 \iff \text{SST} = \text{SSR} + \text{SSE}\)
Why this step works The orthogonality of the OLS projection guarantees that the fitted variation and residual variation do not overlap.
💡

Big Picture: What Did We Actually Learn?

The variation of \(y\) around its sample mean separates into two mutually orthogonal pieces: explained variation (\(\text{SSR}\)) captured by the regressors, and residual variation (\(\text{SSE}\)) left untouched. The coefficient of determination \(R^2\) simply measures the fraction of total centered variation accounted for by the fitted model.

KEY EQUATION

Coefficient of Determination (R²)

$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\text{SSE}}{\text{SST}} = \frac{\text{SSR}}{\text{SST}}$$
WHERE
\(\text{SST} = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2\)
Total Sum of Squares: Total sample variation of observed outcomes \(y_i\) around sample mean \(\bar{y}\).
\(\text{SSR} = \sum_{i=1}^n (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y})^2\)
Regression Sum of Squares: Variation of fitted values \(\hat{y}_i = x_i'\hat{\beta}\) explained by regressors around \(\bar{y}\).
\(\text{SSE} = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2\)
Sum of Squared Residuals: Unexplained variation remaining in the sample residuals.
\(R^2 \in [0, 1]\)
Proportion of Variance Explained: Fraction of total outcome variation accounted for by the regression model with intercept.
TOTAL VARIATION (SST) = 100% \(R^2 + (1 - R^2) = 1\)
EXPLAINED (R² = 68%)
RESIDUAL (1 − R² = 32%)
\(\text{SSR} = \text{Variation captured by regressors}\) \(\text{SSE} = \text{Variation left in residuals}\)

3. Matrix Version using the Demeaning Matrix M₀

The matrix formulation provides an elegant, coordinate-free perspective on goodness of fit. Instead of evaluating observations individually, we represent sample centering through an orthogonal projection operator.

💡 Concept: M₀ is the Demeaning Operator

Let \(\iota = [1, 1, \dots, 1]'\) be an \(n \times 1\) column vector of ones. The projection matrix onto \(\iota\) is \(P_0 = \iota(\iota'\iota)^{-1}\iota' = \frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'\). Its orthogonal complement, the demeaning operator \(M_0\), is defined as:

\(M_0 = I - P_0 = I - \iota(\iota'\iota)^{-1}\iota' = I - \frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'\)

For any \(n \times 1\) vector \(z\), multiplying by \(M_0\) removes the sample mean \(\bar{z}\) from every observation: \(M_0 z = z - \bar{z}\iota\).

Concrete Vector Intuition: How M₀ Works

Original Vector \(z\)
\([7,\; 9,\; 11]'\)
\(\text{mean } \bar{z} = 9\)
\(\xrightarrow{M_0}\)
Demeaned Vector \(M_0 z\)
\([-2,\; 0,\; 2]'\)
\(\text{mean } = 0\)

The transformed vector has sample mean zero. Notice that \(M_0 y = y - \bar{y}\iota\) subtracts the scalar mean \(\bar{y}\) from every row of \(y\).

Matrix Derivation

Matrix Formulation of R² via M₀

What are we trying to show?

Demonstrate that pre-multiplying the sample equation by \(M_0\) isolates centered quadratic forms, yielding \(R^2 = 1 - \frac{e'e}{y'M_0 y}\).

1

Pre-multiply the OLS regression equation by M₀

Apply the demeaning operator to both sides of \(y = X\hat{\beta} + e\):

\(M_0 y = M_0 X\hat{\beta} + M_0 e \iff y - \bar{y}\iota = (X\hat{\beta} - \bar{y}\iota) + e\)
Why this step works With an intercept, OLS residuals sum to zero (\(\iota'e = 0\)), which means \(P_0 e = \frac{1}{n}\iota(\iota'e) = \mathbf{0}\). Therefore, \(M_0 e = (I - P_0)e = e\).
2

Compute the inner product of centered outcomes

Square both sides in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) using the symmetric, idempotent property \(M_0'M_0 = M_0\):

\(y'M_0 y = \hat{\beta}'X'M_0 X\hat{\beta} + 2e'M_0 X\hat{\beta} + e'M_0 e\)
Why this step works For any vector \(v\), \(v'M_0 v = (M_0 v)'(M_0 v)\) computes the sum of squared deviations from the sample mean.
3

Apply normal equations orthogonality

Cross Term = 0

Evaluate the cross-product term using OLS orthogonality \(X'e = \mathbf{0}\):

\(e'M_0 X\hat{\beta} = e'(I - P_0)X\hat{\beta} = e'X\hat{\beta} - (e'P_0)X\hat{\beta} = 0 - 0 = 0\)
Why this step works OLS sample residuals are orthogonal to every column of \(X\) (\(X'e = \mathbf{0}\)) and orthogonal to the constant vector (\(\iota'e = 0\)).
4

State the compact matrix R² formula

Combine the quadratic forms to produce the slick matrix formulation from the source:

\(y'M_0 y = \hat{\beta}'X'M_0 X\hat{\beta} + e'e \implies R^2 = 1 - \frac{e'e}{y'M_0 y} = \frac{\hat{\beta}'X'M_0 X\hat{\beta}}{y'M_0 y}\)
Why this step works \(y'M_0 y = \text{SST}\) is total centered variation, \(\hat{\beta}'X'M_0 X\hat{\beta} = \text{SSR}\) is explained centered variation, and \(e'e = \text{SSE}\) is the unexplained residual sum of squares.

5. Crucial Econometric Caution: R² Does Not Imply Causality

A central theme from Econometrics (3).pdf is that goodness of fit measures predictive linear association within a specific sample, not causal structure.

✓ What R² Tells You

How well deviations in the fitted linear combination of regressors \(x_i'\hat{\beta}\) account for sample movements in \(y_i\) around the sample mean \(\bar{y}\).

✗ What R² Does Not Tell You

Whether intervening to alter \(X\) causes a change in \(Y\). Even with \(R^2 = 0.99\) and \(\hat{\beta} > 0\), exogenous changes to \(x\) may produce zero causal response in \(y\).

Source Example 1: Financial Indices & Dining

When the S&P 500 rises, high-end steakhouse visits increase sharply (\(R^2\) is high). But the stock market does not rise because fund managers eat more steak. Both are driven by common macroeconomic liquidity.

Source Example 2: Hospital Size & Pricing

Smaller rural hospitals frequently exhibit higher per-procedure medical prices. However, this correlation reflects regional lack of competition (local monopoly power) rather than bed capacity causing price hikes.

⚠️ Common Mistake

"A model with R² = 0.85 is a better economic model than one with R² = 0.35."
Correction: Not necessarily. An atheoretical model packed with lagged values or endogenous proxies can produce an \(R^2\) near 1.0 while providing zero causal insight or policy guidance. Econometric validity depends on identification, exogeneity, and research design—not purely variance accounted for.

🧠

Can You Explain It? Conceptual Check

Question: If an empirical wage regression reports \(R^2 = 0.85\), what exactly can you claim—and what can you NOT claim?

Show Explanation
What you CAN say: 85% of the sample variation in wages around the sample mean is statistically captured by the linear combination of included regressors.

What you CANNOT say: You cannot claim that the regressors caused 85% of an individual's wage, nor that manipulating any single regressor (e.g. increasing schooling by 1 year) will automatically produce the predicted wage increase unless exogeneity (\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] = 0\)) is convincingly established.

✅ Quick Check: Sum of Squares

In a sample of 100 observations with an intercept, the total sum of squares is \(\text{SST} = 500\) and the sum of squared residuals is \(\text{SSE} = 125\). What is \(\text{SSR}\) and what is \(R^2\)?

Show Answer & Explanation
ANSWER: \(\text{SSR} = 375\) and \(R^2 = 0.75\).

WHY? With an intercept included, the variance decomposition holds exactly: \(\text{SSR} = \text{SST} - \text{SSE} = 500 - 125 = 375\). Then \(R^2 = \frac{\text{SSR}}{\text{SST}} = \frac{375}{500} = 0.75\), indicating that 75% of the sample variation in \(y\) around its mean is accounted for by the fitted regression model.
📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • Total centered variation decomposes cleanly into explained and residual variation (\(\text{SST} = \text{SSR} + \text{SSE}\)) under OLS with an intercept because residuals are orthogonal to regressors and sum to zero.
  • \(R^2 = \frac{\text{SSR}}{\text{SST}} = 1 - \frac{\text{SSE}}{\text{SST}}\) represents the proportion of sample variance in \(y\) captured by the fitted regression line.
  • The demeaning operator \(M_0 = I - \frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'\) subtracts the sample mean from any vector, expressing \(R^2\) in compact coordinate-free matrix notation as \(1 - \frac{e'e}{y'M_0 y}\).
  • A high \(R^2\) indicates strong predictive in-sample correlation, but never establishes causal identification or policy effectiveness.
