Goodness of Fit & Finite-Sample OLS Properties

Learn how well OLS fits observed data, how \(R^2\) and adjusted \(R^2\) are interpreted, and why OLS possesses critical finite-sample properties such as unbiasedness, variance efficiency (Gauss–Markov / BLUE), degrees-of-freedom variance correction, and exact normal and \(t\) inference.

Lessons in this Module

01

3.1 Goodness of Fit & R-Squared

Understand how variation in regressors predicts variation in \(y\), derive the SST = SSR + SSE decomposition via summation and demeaning matrix \(M_0\), and evaluate the predictive limits of \(R^2\).

3.2 Adjusted R-Squared & Model Comparison

Examine why adding regressors mechanically inflates ordinary \(R^2\), explore the degrees-of-freedom penalty in adjusted \(\bar{R}^2\), and learn rigorous rules for comparing non-nested models.

Model Selection Not Started
03

3.3 R-Squared & F Tests

Construct hypothesis tests for multiple parameter restrictions by comparing unrestricted vs restricted \(R^2\), and understand the geometry of joint statistical significance.

Hypothesis Testing Not Started
04

3.4 OLS Unbiasedness & Omitted Variable Bias

Derive finite-sample conditional unbiasedness under strict exogeneity, and analyze the exact mathematical formula and 2×2 diagnostic table for omitted-variable bias.

Identification & Bias Not Started
05

3.5 Variance of the OLS Estimator

Derive the variance-covariance matrix \(\operatorname{Var}(b \mid X) = \sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}\) under spherical errors, evaluate the diagonal 2-regressor matrix, and inspect standard error precision drivers.

Precision & Sampling Not Started

3.6 Gauss–Markov, BLUE & Estimating Error Variance

Prove that OLS is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE) via non-negative definite matrix \(DD'\), and derive the unbiased residual variance estimator \(s^2 = e'e / (n - k)\).

Efficiency & Estimation Not Started

3.7 Normality, t Statistics, CLTs & Module Review

Connect exact normal error distributions to the Student's \(t_{n-k}\) statistic, contrast the Lindeberg–Lévy and Lindeberg–Feller central limit theorems, and master the full 15-topic Module 3 review.
