LESSON 9.5 Method Choice & Synthesis

Choosing a Correction: GLS, FGLS, HAC & WLS Review

Synthesize the complete econometric toolkit for non-spherical disturbances. Compare when to transform data via GLS, FGLS, Prais–Winsten, or Cochrane–Orcutt, when to robustify inference via Newey–West HAC standard errors, and how these methods connect back to Module 8 Weighted Least Squares.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Determine the appropriate econometric strategy based on what is known or assumed about the error covariance \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\).
  • Review why Generalized Least Squares is BLUE when the non-scalar covariance matrix is known up to scale.
  • Connect time-series quasi-differencing to cross-sectional Weighted Least Squares under heteroskedasticity.
  • Compare OLS, GLS, FGLS, Prais–Winsten, Cochrane–Orcutt, HAC, and WLS across key theoretical dimensions.
  • Integrate the foundational concepts from autocorrelation to HAC inference into one unified framework.
STRATEGY SELECTOR

1. The Non-Spherical Error Decision Map

THE CENTRAL ESTIMATION DILEMMA

When spherical error assumptions fail (\(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\)), the appropriate estimator depends directly on: What do we know about the structure of \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\), and what assumptions are maintained about the data generating process?

SCENARIO 1

True \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is Known

The error covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is completely known up to scale.

$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{y}$$
Applicable Tool: Generalized Least Squares (GLS). Attains the Gauss–Markov bound (BLUE).
SCENARIO 2

AR(1) Assumed (\(\rho\) Unknown)

Disturbances follow \(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\), but \(\rho\) is unknown.

$$\text{Estimate } \widehat{\rho} \implies \text{Quasi-Difference Data}$$
Applicable Tools: Prais–Winsten (preserves \(t=1\)) or Cochrane–Orcutt (drops \(t=1\)).
SCENARIO 3

Agnostic / Unknown Dependence

Retain OLS coefficients and guard against arbitrary autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity.

$$\operatorname{Avar}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{S}^*(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}$$
Applicable Tool: Newey–West HAC Standard Errors with Bartlett kernel weights.
SCENARIO 4

Known Heteroskedastic Form

Cross-sectional variance varies according to \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \sigma^2 h(\mathbf{x}_i)^2\).

$$y_i^* = y_i / h(\mathbf{x}_i), \quad \mathbf{x}_i^* = \mathbf{x}_i / h(\mathbf{x}_i)$$
Applicable Tool: Weighted Least Squares (WLS) using precision weights \(w_i = 1 / \widehat{\sigma}_i^2\).
METHOD SELECTION PRINCIPLE

There is no universal "best" method. The appropriate approach depends on what is known or assumed about the error structure. When the parametric AR(1) model is maintained, FGLS transformations target efficiency. When the dynamic structure is unknown or robust inference is preferred without altering OLS estimates, Newey–West HAC standard errors are indicated.

THEORETICAL UNIFICATION

2. WLS & GLS Efficiency Recaps

CROSS-MODULE LINEAGE

The methods developed in Module 9 share a direct mathematical lineage with the general theory of non-spherical errors from Module 8. Both frameworks replace the naive spherical assumption with optimal linear combinations tailored to error covariance structure.

MODULE 8 BRIDGE

WLS Source Recap

Under heteroskedasticity \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \sigma^2 h(x_i)^2\), dividing the regression by \(h(x_i)\) restores homoskedasticity:

$$\frac{y_i}{h(x_i)} = \left(\frac{\mathbf{x}_i}{h(x_i)}\right)'\boldsymbol{\beta} + \frac{\varepsilon_i}{h(x_i)}$$
The 6-Step WLS Procedure:
  1. Run initial OLS to obtain residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i\).
  2. Model squared residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) using regressors.
  3. Predict observation variance function \(\widehat{\sigma}_i^2\).
  4. Construct precision weights \(w_i = 1 / \widehat{\sigma}_i^2\).
  5. Re-estimate regression by weighted OLS.
  6. Report standard errors from the transformed model.
GAUSS–MARKOV RESULT

GLS Efficiency Recap

Under the general linear model with non-spherical disturbances:

$$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}, \quad \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega}$$

When the covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is known, the GLS estimator is:

$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{y}$$
Gauss–Markov Theorem: Under strict exogeneity and known \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\), GLS is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE), attaining the minimum variance bound.

4. Module 9 Conceptual Roadmap & Review Cards

Trace the intellectual journey of Module 9 from time-ordered data to robust econometric inference:

STAGE 1
Time-Ordered Data

Disturbances exhibit temporal cross-period memory: \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_s] \neq 0\).

STAGE 2
AR(1) Model

\(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\). Positive \(\rho\) causes persistent runs.

STAGE 3
Durbin–Watson Test

\(\text{DW} \approx 2(1 - \widehat{\rho})\). Values \(< 2\) indicate positive serial correlation.

STAGE 4
Quasi-Differencing

Subtracting \(\rho\) times lagged values purges serial correlation.

STAGE 5
FGLS (CO vs. PW)

Prais–Winsten preserves \(t=1\); Cochrane–Orcutt drops it.

STAGE 6
Newey–West HAC

Preserves OLS point estimates; corrects covariance via Bartlett kernel.

14 Core Concept Review Cards

Master every essential definition and formula from the lecture notes:

1. Serial Correlation

\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_s] \neq 0\) for \(t \neq s\). Non-zero covariance between disturbances at distinct periods violates the spherical error assumption.

2. AR(1) SPECIFICATION

\(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\) with \(u_t \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} (0, \sigma^2)\). Current error depends linearly on previous error plus white noise shock.

3. STATIONARITY RESTRICTION

Requires \(|\rho| < 1\), ensuring that the effect of past innovations decays over time and error variance remains finite.

4. OLS UNBIASEDNESS

Under strict exogeneity \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\), OLS point estimates remain unbiased and consistent despite autocorrelation.

5. CONVENTIONAL SE FAILURE

\(\sigma^2(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) is incorrect under autocorrelation, understating sampling variance and inflating t-statistics under positive persistence.

6. DURBIN–WATSON STATISTIC

\(\text{DW} = \frac{\sum (\widehat{\varepsilon}_t - \widehat{\varepsilon}_{t-1})^2}{\sum \widehat{\varepsilon}_t^2} \approx 2(1 - \widehat{\rho})\). Benchmarks: \(\approx 2\) (none), \(< 2\) (positive), \(> 2\) (negative).

7. QUASI-DIFFERENCING

\(y_t - \rho y_{t-1} = (\mathbf{x}_t - \rho \mathbf{x}_{t-1})'\boldsymbol{\beta} + u_t\). Subtracting scaled lag purges AR(1) persistence, leaving spherical innovations \(u_t\).

8. FGLS FOR UNKNOWN ρ

Estimates \(\widehat{\rho}\) from OLS residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_t\), transforms data via \(\widehat{\rho}\), and re-estimates the regression by OLS.

9. COCHRANE–ORCUTT

Applies AR(1) transformation for \(t \ge 2\) and drops observation \(t=1\). The source emphasizes that discarding \(t=1\) causes some efficiency loss.

10. PRAIS–WINSTEN

Retains \(t=1\) by transforming it via \(y_1^* = \sqrt{1-\widehat{\rho}^2}\,y_1\), preserving all \(n\) observations and avoiding Cochrane–Orcutt efficiency loss.

11. COEFFICIENT INVARIANCE

Newey–West HAC does not alter \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\). Point estimates remain untouched; only standard errors and covariance are adjusted.

12. BARTLETT KERNEL

\(w_s = 1 - \frac{|s|}{L+1}\) for \(|s| \le L\). Assigns linearly declining weights to higher lags, guaranteeing positive semi-definiteness of the covariance matrix.

13. BANDWIDTH HORIZON L

Controls how many lag cross-covariances are included. All lags beyond \(L\) receive zero weight in the Newey–West sandwich summation.

14. GLS Efficiency

When the covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is known, Generalized Least Squares (GLS) is Best Linear Unbiased (BLUE).

CONCEPT CHECK

5. Quick Check 9.5

CHECK 9.5 Transformation vs HAC Selection
Question: An applied researcher suspects serial correlation in a quarterly macro model with 60 observations, but is uncertain whether errors follow a clean AR(1) process or a more general dynamic pattern with potential heteroskedasticity. Which econometric procedure from the course is most appropriate, and why?
HINT: Consider which procedure protects inference without forcing the researcher to assume an exact parametric AR(1) specification.

ANSWER: Ordinary Least Squares paired with Newey–West HAC Standard Errors.

WHY? Prais–Winsten and Cochrane–Orcutt strictly assume an AR(1) error specification. If the true disturbance process contains higher-order dynamics or arbitrary heteroskedasticity, those parametric FGLS transformations may be misspecified. In contrast, Newey–West HAC standard errors leave OLS point estimates unchanged and provide consistent asymptotic standard errors robust to both serial correlation (up to bandwidth \(L\)) and arbitrary heteroskedasticity.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • Method Dichotomy: Non-spherical error corrections either transform the data (GLS, FGLS, Prais–Winsten, Cochrane–Orcutt, WLS) or preserve OLS coefficients and adjust standard errors (Newey–West HAC).
  • Prais–Winsten Preservation: When an AR(1) error process is maintained, Prais–Winsten avoids the efficiency loss of Cochrane–Orcutt by retaining observation \(t=1\) via \(\sqrt{1-\widehat{\rho}^2}\).
  • Newey–West Robustness: When the dynamic structure is unknown, Newey–West HAC standard errors provide honest large-sample inference without requiring data transformations.
  • WLS Connection: WLS weights cross-sectional observations by inverse estimated variances, just as quasi-differencing purges serial dependence across time periods.
  • No Universal Hierarchy: The choice between FGLS transformation and HAC covariance adjustment depends on whether the researcher maintains a specific parametric error model or prefers model-agnostic inference.
