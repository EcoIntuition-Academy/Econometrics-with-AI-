LESSON 9.1 Serial Correlation & AR(1)

Time-Ordered Data & AR(1) Autocorrelation

Examine what happens when regression disturbances exhibit temporal persistence, formalize the first-order autoregressive error process, and understand why OLS point estimates can remain unbiased under strict exogeneity even while conventional standard errors fail.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Connect Module 8 non-spherical error theory (\(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\)) to temporal cross-period dependencies (\(t \neq s\)).
  • Define autocorrelation (serial correlation) mathematically as \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_s] \neq 0\) for distinct time periods \(t \neq s\).
  • Deconstruct the AR(1) disturbance process \(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\) with stationarity constraint \(|\rho| < 1\).
  • Distinguish positive persistence (\(\rho > 0\)), independent shocks (\(\rho = 0\)), and alternating dynamics (\(\rho < 0\)).
  • Evaluate why OLS remains unbiased under strict exogeneity, but suffers from efficiency loss and invalid conventional standard errors.

1. From Non-Spherical Errors to Time Series

MODULE 8 REMINDER · NON-SPHERICAL ERRORS

Module 8 established that \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\) represents non-spherical disturbance behavior. In cross-sectional data, this typically takes the form of heteroskedasticity (non-constant diagonal variances). In time-ordered data, the primary breakdown is that disturbances from different periods are correlated across time, populating the off-diagonal elements of \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\).

Formally, time-series autocorrelation is defined as:

AUTOCORRELATION (SERIAL CORRELATION) DEFINITION
$$\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_s] \neq 0 \quad \text{for } t \neq s$$
Autocorrelation is identical to serial correlation: disturbance realizations are related across temporal periods.
INTUITION · PERSISTENCE OF SHOCKS

In economic systems, a shock occurring today does not evaporate immediately—it tends to persist into tomorrow. If an unobserved macroeconomic shock creates a positive regression disturbance in period \(t\), the disturbance in period \(t+1\) is also more likely to be positive.

2. The First-Order Autoregressive Process: AR(1)

The canonical mathematical model for serial dependence in econometrics is the first-order autoregressive error process, denoted AR(1):

THE AR(1) DISTURBANCE PROCESS
$$\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t, \quad |\rho| < 1, \quad u_t \sim \text{iid}(0, \sigma^2)$$
\(\varepsilon_t\) Current regression disturbance, capturing all unobserved contemporaneous factors.
\(\varepsilon_{t-1}\) Previous-period regression disturbance, transmitting historical persistence into period \(t\).
\(\rho\) First-order autocorrelation parameter. Governs the direction and fraction of memory transmitted.
\(u_t\) Fresh white-noise innovation, satisfying classical spherical conditions (\(\mathbb{E}[u_t] = 0\), \(\operatorname{Var}(u_t) = \sigma^2\)).

Interpretation of the Parameter \(\rho\)

\(\rho > 0\) · Positive Persistence

Neighboring disturbances exhibit cyclical clustering. A positive error today tends to be followed by another positive error tomorrow.

\(\rho = 0\) · Zero Autocorrelation

Errors are uncorrelated across time (\(\varepsilon_t = u_t\)). The Gauss–Markov spherical assumption is satisfied.

\(\rho < 0\) · Negative Alternating

Consecutive errors oscillate back and forth across zero, switching between positive and negative values at each step.

3. Consequences of Autocorrelation for OLS

IMPORTANT ASSUMPTION CARD · WHY CAN OLS REMAIN UNBIASED?

Autocorrelation alters the covariance structure of the error vector \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\), setting \(\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\). However, estimator unbiasedness depends entirely on the conditional mean: $$\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \boldsymbol{\beta} + (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}]$$ Because the source maintains strict exogeneity (\(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\)), the expectation vanishes. Autocorrelation does not by itself bias OLS point estimates!

While OLS remains unbiased and consistent under strict exogeneity, the presence of serial correlation imposes severe econometric penalties:

1. OLS is Inefficient (Not BLUE)

The Gauss–Markov theorem fails because errors are non-spherical. OLS no longer has the minimum sampling variance; a GLS transformation achieves lower variance.

2. Conventional Standard Errors are Invalid

The standard formula \(s^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) ignores off-diagonal autocovariances. Under positive autocorrelation, standard errors are severely underestimated.

3. Hypothesis Testing Breaks Down

Deflated standard errors artificially inflate \(t\)-statistics and \(F\)-statistics, resulting in spuriously narrow confidence intervals and inflated Type I error rates.

CONCEPT CHECK

5. Quick Check 9.1

CHECK 9.1 AR(1) Disturbance Persistence
Question: If \(\rho > 0\) in an AR(1) error process, what visual pattern would you expect in neighboring residuals?
HINT: Consider what the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \mid \varepsilon_{t-1}] = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1}\) implies about the sign and magnitude of consecutive disturbance points.

ANSWER: Neighboring residuals will exhibit persistent clustering—prolonged runs above zero followed by prolonged runs below zero.

WHY? In the AR(1) process \(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\), when \(\rho > 0\), each disturbance inherits a positive fraction of the previous period's value. If \(\varepsilon_{t-1} > 0\), the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \mid \varepsilon_{t-1}] = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1}\) is strictly positive, making it much more likely that \(\varepsilon_t\) remains positive as well. Rather than scattering randomly across the zero line like independent white noise, the residual path drifts in smooth, multi-period cyclical excursions.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • Autocorrelation Defined: Disturbances violate the spherical assumption via cross-period correlation: \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \varepsilon_s] \neq 0\) for \(t \neq s\).
  • AR(1) Model: \(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\) where \(|\rho| < 1\) guarantees weak covariance stationarity and non-explosive variance.
  • Autocovariance & Autocorrelation: \(\gamma_s = \frac{\sigma^2 \rho^{|s|}}{1 - \rho^2}\) and \(\text{Corr}(\varepsilon_t, \varepsilon_{t-s}) = \rho^s\), decaying exponentially toward zero.
  • OLS Unbiasedness Preserved: Under strict exogeneity (\(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\)), OLS point estimates remain unbiased and consistent despite autocorrelation.
  • Inference Failure: Autocorrelation renders OLS inefficient (not BLUE) and invalidates conventional standard error formulas, leading to spuriously high \(t\)-statistics under positive persistence.
