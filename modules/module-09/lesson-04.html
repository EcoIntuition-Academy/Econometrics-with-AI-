LESSON 9.4 HAC Covariance & Robust Inference

Newey–West HAC Standard Errors

While GLS and FGLS transform the regression data to satisfy spherical error conditions, Newey–West takes an alternative path: keep the OLS point estimates unchanged and correct the asymptotic covariance matrix. Learn how Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent (HAC) inference works with Bartlett kernel lag weighting.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Distinguish between data transformation (GLS/FGLS) and robust covariance adjustment (Newey–West HAC).
  • Understand what Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent inference achieves in large samples.
  • Recognize that Newey–West leaves \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) completely unchanged and only recalculates standard errors.
  • Analyze how weights \(w_s = 1 - \frac{|s|}{L+1}\) decay linearly as lag length \(s\) increases toward truncation lag \(L\).
  • Evaluate how HAC standard errors correct confidence intervals and hypothesis tests under persistent errors.
ESTIMATION PARADIGM

1. The HAC Strategy: Transform vs. Robustify

When disturbances in time-ordered data violate the spherical error assumption, econometricians face a fundamental architectural choice: transform the regression model (quasi-differencing) to purge autocorrelation, or robustify standard errors directly while retaining original OLS point estimates.

STRATEGY A

GLS / FGLS Transformation

Assumes a specific parametric structure for errors (e.g., AR(1) with \(\varepsilon_t = \rho \varepsilon_{t-1} + u_t\)).

  • Transforms \(y_t\) and \(\mathbf{x}_t\) by quasi-differencing.
  • Produces a new coefficient vector: \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{FGLS}} \neq \widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\).
  • Aims for Gauss–Markov efficiency if the parametric AR(1) specification is correct.
STRATEGY B

Newey–West HAC Robustification

Newey–West keeps OLS coefficients unchanged and adjusts the estimated covariance matrix to accommodate unknown autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity.

  • Leaves \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) completely untouched.
  • HAC = Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent.
  • Guarantees valid large-sample inference without requiring the exact AR(1) model to hold.
CRUCIAL ECONOMETRIC PRINCIPLE · COEFFICIENT INVARIANCE

Newey–West does NOT change your regression coefficients. The estimated slopes and intercepts remain exactly \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\). What changes is the variance-covariance matrix \(\widehat{\mathbf{V}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\), providing honest standard errors, t-statistics, and confidence intervals under autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity.

MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

2. The Newey–West HAC Covariance Estimator

ASYMPTOTIC VARIANCE FOUNDATION

Under serial correlation and heteroskedasticity, the classical Gauss–Markov variance formula \(\sigma^2(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) collapses. The true asymptotic variance takes a robust sandwich form, requiring a non-parametric estimate of the inner spectral kernel:

NEWEY–WEST HAC COVARIANCE MATRIX
$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Avar}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}) &= (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{S}^*(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \quad \\ \mathbf{S}^* &= \sum_{s=-L}^{L} w_s \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_s \end{aligned}$$
WHERE THE COMPONENTS ARE DEFINED AS:
\(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_s\) · Sample Lag-\(s\) Covariance Contribution:
$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_s = \sum_{t=s+1}^{n} \widehat{\varepsilon}_t \widehat{\varepsilon}_{t-s} \mathbf{x}_t \mathbf{x}_{t-s}'$$

Measures cross-period correlation between regressors and residuals separated by \(s\) periods. When \(s = 0\), this collapses to the White heteroskedasticity term.

\(w_s\) · Bartlett Kernel Weight:
$$w_s = 1 - \frac{|s|}{L + 1}$$

A linearly declining weight that assigns full weight (\(w_0 = 1\)) to contemporaneous variance and diminishing weight to higher lags, guaranteeing positive semi-definiteness.

\(L\) · Bandwidth / Truncation Lag:

The maximum lag horizon included in the summation. Autocovariances beyond lag \(L\) (where \(|s| > L\)) receive zero weight.

CONCEPT CHECK

4. Quick Check 9.4

CHECK 9.4 Point Estimates Invariance
Question: Why does the Newey–West HAC procedure leave the OLS point estimates \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) completely unchanged?
HINT: Consider which part of the regression output Newey–West is designed to correct.

ANSWER: Newey–West is a covariance matrix adjustment, not a data transformation. It recalculates standard errors to account for autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity while retaining the original OLS coefficient estimates.

WHY? Under strict exogeneity, OLS point estimates remain unbiased and consistent even when errors are serially correlated. Only the conventional variance formula fails. Therefore, Newey–West robustifies inference without altering point estimates.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • HAC Definition: Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent standard errors guard against both non-constant error variance and cross-period serial correlation.
  • Coefficient Invariance: Newey–West leaves \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) completely unchanged and only recalculates the variance-covariance matrix.
  • Bartlett Weights: Weights \(w_s = 1 - \frac{|s|}{L+1}\) decay linearly as lag length \(s\) increases toward truncation lag \(L\), ensuring a positive semi-definite covariance matrix.
  • Bandwidth Selection: Truncation lag \(L\) determines how many cross-period autocovariance terms are included in the covariance summation.
  • Inference Correction: Under positive autocorrelation, HAC standard errors are typically larger than conventional OLS standard errors, correcting inflated t-statistics and preventing spurious significance.
