LESSON 15.2 IDENTIFICATION & VALIDITY

RD Identification: Continuity, Manipulation & Placebo Tests

Establish the two core identifying assumptions of Regression Discontinuity—continuity of potential outcomes and absence of precise manipulation—and inspect falsification strategies.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • State and explain the primary identifying assumption: Continuity of potential outcomes at the threshold.
  • Analyze confounded discontinuities using simultaneous interventions (the $100,000 scholarship example).
  • Define the No Precise Sorting assumption and contrast political incumbency with estate-tax bracket bunching.
  • Design empirical placebo tests with predetermined covariates to evaluate identifying credibility.

1. Assumption 1: The Continuity Assumption

CORE IDENTIFYING ASSUMPTION · CONTINUITY

Smoothness of Potential Outcomes Across the Cutoff

The continuity assumption requires that all other determinants of the outcome (both observable characteristics and unobserved potential outcomes) evolve smoothly through the cutoff threshold \(a_0\).

Formally, the conditional expectations of the potential outcomes \(E[Y_i(0) \mid a_i = a]\) and \(E[Y_i(1) \mid a_i = a]\) must be continuous functions of the running variable \(a\) at \(a = a_0\). In the absence of treatment, there would be zero discontinuous jump in the outcome at the cutoff.

CRITICAL THREAT · CONFOUNDED DISCONTINUITY

The Scholarship Bundling Trap

Suppose high school seniors take an entrance exam with cutoff \(a_0 = 500\). Students who score \(a_i \ge 500\) receive special academy admission. However, the state government simultaneously awards every student scoring \(a_i \ge 500\) a $100,000 cash college scholarship.

CUTOFF a₀ = 500
→
├─ Special Academy Admission (Intervention A)
└─ $100,000 Scholarship (Intervention B)
→
CONFOUNDED DISCONTINUITY

Econometric Implication: If subsequent wages or graduation rates jump at 500, the researcher cannot determine whether the jump reflects the academic training of the academy or the financial liquidity of the scholarship. No other compound intervention may change discontinuously at the same threshold.

2. Assumption 2: No Precise Manipulation of the Running Variable

For units on opposite sides of the threshold to be locally comparable, agents must not be able to precisely sort themselves across the cutoff line.

NO PRECISE MANIPULATION RULE

Imprecise vs. Precise Control

Economic agents can exert effort to achieve higher values of \(a_i\) (e.g., studying hard to raise exam scores or campaigning actively to win votes). The RD assumption requires only that agents cannot precisely engineer their exact location around \(a_0\). As long as there is an idiosyncratic, unpredictable stochastic component in \(a_i\) near the threshold, local variation remains as-if random.

CASE 1 · INCUMBENCY & VOTE SHARE ASSUMPTION MET

Congressional Elections at 50%

Politicians campaign aggressively to maximize their overall vote share. No candidate can precisely manipulate election-day turnouts, weather, or counting errors to finish at exactly 50.01% rather than 49.99%.

✓ Source Conclusion: Precise manipulation is implausible. The local comparison around 50% is credible.
CASE 2 · FEDERAL ESTATE TAX MANIPULATION CONCERN

Tax Exemption Threshold at $X Million

Estate taxes apply only if reported taxable wealth exceeds a statutory cutoff \(X\). Wealthy families and estate planners face immense financial incentives to time gifts, claim asset valuation discounts, or shift deductions to report an estate just below \(X\).

✗ Source Conclusion: Selective sorting is highly likely. Units just under the threshold systematically differ from those just above.

3. Placebo Tests & Covariate Balance Checks

While the continuity of unobserved potential outcomes is fundamentally untestable, researchers can formally test whether predetermined characteristics or placebo outcomes jump at the cutoff.

DIAGNOSTIC TOOL · PLACEBO TESTS

Placebo Test: Estimate an RD specification using a predetermined variable as the dependent variable instead of the true outcome \(Y_i\). Variables that were determined prior to treatment (e.g., baseline household income, marital status, gender, or parental education) should not exhibit a discontinuity at the cutoff.

Empirical Warning: If baseline household income jumps at the cutoff, or the fraction of married individuals jumps discontinuously from 40% to 60%, the design's credibility is severely compromised. It indicates that the units on either side were not comparable prior to treatment.

4. Heaping and Density Bunching Near the Threshold

When individuals can manipulate the running variable, they tend to bunch or heap near the threshold to secure favorable treatment status. Examining the empirical density of \(a_i\) provides a direct diagnostic check for sorting behavior.

DENSITY WITH HEAPING (BUNCHING) Suspicious Sorting

An abnormal spike in the density of observations right at or just past the cutoff.

Concern: A discontinuous jump in density indicates units selectively concentrated to receive treatment.
SMOOTH DENSITY (NO HEAPING) Continuous

A smooth, continuous distribution of observations across the cutoff line.

Credible: The density passes smoothly through \(a_0\) with no clustering or artificial grouping.
Important Methodological Distinction: Heaping or bunching constitutes strong evidence consistent with manipulation concerns. However, researchers do not automatically declare every observed heap invalid without investigation; round-number reporting preferences (e.g., ages reported in multiples of 5) can create heaping without deliberate strategic sorting. Methodological care is essential.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: If a predetermined covariate (such as parental income or age) jumps sharply at the treatment cutoff, why is that concerning for an RD study?

Consider whether treatment received at the cutoff could have retroactively altered variables measured before the study began.

Solution:
Because it suggests that units immediately above and below the cutoff differ for reasons other than the treatment itself.

A predetermined variable was fixed prior to treatment assignment; treatment cannot retroactively alter parental income or age. If a predetermined covariate jumps discontinuously at the threshold, it reveals that the groups on either side were not comparable to begin with, indicating manipulation, sorting, or an unobserved confounding policy at the boundary.

Lesson 15.2 Summary

The validity of an RD design hinges on the continuity assumption: expected potential outcomes and background characteristics must evolve smoothly through the cutoff in the absence of treatment. While counterfactual continuity cannot be proven directly from data, researchers conduct essential falsification checks: verifying that predetermined covariates and placebo outcomes do not jump, and confirming running-variable density smoothness via the McCrary test.
