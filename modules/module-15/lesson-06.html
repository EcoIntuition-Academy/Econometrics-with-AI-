McCrary Density Testing, RD Diagnostics & Module Review

Master empirical diagnostics for Regression Discontinuity designs: McCrary density testing, covariate balance checks, placebo outcome tests, and the limits of testability.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Formulate and interpret the McCrary (2008) running-variable density test for strategic sorting.
  • Deploy the full battery of falsification checks: placebo outcomes, baseline balance, and bandwidth stability.
  • Articulate what RD assumptions can be tested versus what remains untestable.
  • Synthesize the complete Sharp vs. Fuzzy RD decision framework.

1. The McCrary Density Test

The core identifying assumption of RD is that units cannot precisely manipulate their position along the running variable. If individuals have the knowledge and ability to sort across the threshold to secure treatment, we expect an abnormal accumulation of observations on the favorable side of the cutoff.

FORMAL DIAGNOSTIC

What is the McCrary Test?

The McCrary (2008) density test is a formal diagnostic that evaluates whether the marginal density \(f(a)\) of the running variable changes discontinuously at the cutoff \(a_0\).

\[ \theta = \ln \lim_{a \downarrow a_0} f(a) - \ln \lim_{a \uparrow a_0} f(a) \]

Under the null hypothesis of no precise manipulation, the running variable density is continuous through the cutoff (\(\theta = 0\)). A statistically significant discontinuity (\(\theta \ne 0\)) is strong empirical evidence consistent with strategic sorting or manipulation.

STEP 1

Fine-Grained Histogram

Partition the running variable into finely spaced, equally sized bins to construct an empirical frequency distribution around the cutoff.

STEP 2

Local Kernel Smoothing

Fit separate local polynomial smoothers to the bin counts on the left (\(a < a_0\)) and right (\(a \ge a_0\)) sides of the cutoff.

STEP 3

Log-Height Discontinuity

Test whether the estimated density heights at the threshold differ significantly. A visible jump indicates heaping/sorting.

3. The Complete Suite of RD Empirical Diagnostics

A rigorous empirical RD paper never relies solely on the primary outcome discontinuity. It builds credibility through a battery of falsification and robustness checks:

FALSIFICATION

Placebo Outcome Tests

Estimate the RD specification replacing the true outcome \(Y_i\) with alternative outcomes that could not plausibly be affected by the treatment (e.g., outcomes measured prior to treatment, or outcomes in an unrelated domain).

Expected result: Discontinuity estimate \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{placebo}} \approx 0\). Any significant jump indicates a misspecified functional form or confounded discontinuity.
LOCAL RANDOMIZATION

Covariate Balance Checks

Test whether predetermined baseline characteristics (e.g., parental income, age, gender, prior test scores) are smooth through the threshold.

Expected result: Baseline covariates should show no statistically significant jumps at \(a_0\). Units just above and below should look observably identical on average.
TRANSPARENCY

Graphical Diagnostics

Always visually present binned scatter plots overlaid with fitted polynomial curves. A genuine RD effect should be readily apparent to the naked eye.

Visual integrity: Look out for false discontinuities driven by outliers near the boundaries or severe polynomial swing artifacts.
ROBUSTNESS

Bandwidth Sensitivity Analysis

Re-estimate the treatment effect across a wide grid of bandwidths (\(0.5 \times h^*\) to \(2.0 \times h^*\)) to confirm that substantive conclusions do not hinge on an arbitrary window.

Stability check: Point estimates should remain stable in magnitude and sign across reasonable bandwidth variations.

4. What RD Assumptions Can and Cannot Be Directly Tested

As an applied econometrics practitioner, you must maintain epistemological honesty about what empirical diagnostics can establish versus what remains untestable:

EMPIRICALLY TESTABLE

What We Can Directly Examine

  • Discontinuities in observed covariates: Whether predetermined characteristics jump at the threshold.
  • Discontinuities in placebo outcomes: Whether unaffected metrics remain continuous.
  • Running variable density heaping: Whether frequency histograms exhibit sorting or bunching at the threshold.
  • Bandwidth & functional form stability: Whether estimates persist under varied bandwidths and polynomial choices.
  • First-stage compliance jump: In Fuzzy RD, whether \(\Delta P(D \mid a)\) is large enough to avoid weak IV issues.
FUNDAMENTALLY UNTESTABLE

What We Cannot Directly Test

  • Continuity of unobserved potential outcomes: We cannot directly verify that unobserved omitted variables \(E[Y_i(0) \mid a_i]\) evolve smoothly through \(a_0\).
  • Absolute absence of manipulation: If sorting occurs symmetrically on both sides of the threshold, the McCrary test will not detect a discontinuity in overall density.
  • Exact local random assignment: Local "as-if random" comparability is an identifying assumption maintained by economic reasoning and institutional knowledge.

5. Assessing RD Design Credibility

In empirical practice, Regression Discontinuity is considered one of the most transparent quasi-experimental methods because the assignment mechanism is institutional and observable. However, researchers must interpret diagnostic evidence with appropriate precision:

What Diagnostics Can Do

Diagnostics can reveal evidence inconsistent with key RD assumptions or weaken the credibility of the design when red flags emerge—such as suspicious clustering at the cutoff, discontinuous predetermined characteristics, or erratic instability across bandwidths.

What Diagnostics Cannot Do

Diagnostics cannot directly verify all unobserved continuity conditions. Passing falsification checks confirms that observable implications hold, but the counterfactual potential outcomes \(E[Y_i(0) \mid X_i]\) through the threshold remain inherently unobservable.

Standards for Credible Practice

  • Predetermined Covariates & Placebos: Predetermined covariates and placebo outcomes should not show systematic or substantively meaningful discontinuities at the treatment cutoff.
  • Density Smoothness: The running variable distribution should show no pronounced, asymmetric bunching or strategic threshold sorting under McCrary-style tests.
  • Robustness: Results should be reasonably stable across defensible bandwidth choices and specifications.

6. Sharp vs. Fuzzy RD: Final Comparison

The following comparison synthesizes the foundational distinctions between Sharp and Fuzzy Regression Discontinuity designs across theoretical, empirical, and estimand dimensions:

Dimension Sharp RD Fuzzy RD
Treatment Rule Treatment status is deterministically determined by the cutoff under the Sharp RD design: \(D_i = \mathbf{1}(X_i \ge c)\). Treatment probability changes discontinuously but imperfectly at the cutoff \(c\): \(0 < P(D_i = 1 \mid X_i) < 1\).
Treatment at Cutoff Probability of treatment jumps deterministically from 0 to 1 at the threshold. Probability of treatment exhibits a sharp, nonzero jump at the cutoff, but the jump is strictly less than 1.
Main Estimand Local treatment effect at the cutoff under the maintained Sharp RD identification assumptions. Local Average Treatment Effect near the cutoff for compliers whose treatment status is changed by crossing the threshold, under the maintained IV/RD assumptions.
Basic Estimator Outcome discontinuity at the cutoff:
\(\tau_{\text{SRD}} = \lim_{x \downarrow c} E[Y_i \mid X_i=x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} E[Y_i \mid X_i=x]\) 		Outcome discontinuity divided by treatment-probability discontinuity:
\(\tau_{\text{FRD}} = \frac{\lim_{x \downarrow c} E[Y_i \mid X_i=x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} E[Y_i \mid X_i=x]}{\lim_{x \downarrow c} E[D_i \mid X_i=x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} E[D_i \mid X_i=x]}\)
IV Interpretation No IV rescaling is required in the canonical Sharp design. Threshold crossing acts as an instrument for actual treatment receipt under the maintained assumptions.
Compliance / First Stage Treatment probability changes deterministically according to the cutoff rule. Treatment probability has a nonzero discontinuity at the cutoff but does not jump fully from 0 to 1.

7. Module 15 Review

The core principles of Regression Discontinuity can be synthesized into four foundational conceptual pillars:

CARD 1

RD FOUNDATIONS

  • Running Variable: The continuous assignment or forcing variable \(X_i\) that governs eligibility.
  • Cutoff: The known administrative threshold \(c\) where treatment rules change abruptly.
  • Sharp RD: Design where crossing the cutoff deterministically determines treatment take-up.
  • Local Comparison: Identification relies on comparing units in an immediate neighborhood around \(c\).
CARD 2

IDENTIFICATION

  • Continuity: Potential outcome conditional expectations evolve smoothly through the cutoff in the absence of treatment.
  • No Precise Manipulation: Units cannot selectively fine-tune their running variable to position themselves on the preferred side.
  • Placebo Checks: Falsification tests ensuring outcomes unaffected by treatment exhibit no jump at \(c\).
  • Covariate Smoothness: Predetermined baseline covariates should not jump discontinuously at the cutoff.
CARD 3

ESTIMATION

  • Centered Running Variable: Regressing on \(\tilde{X}_i = (X_i - c)\) gives the treatment indicator coefficient direct cutoff interpretability.
  • Bandwidth: Symmetric window \([c - h, c + h]\) balancing local bias against sample variance.
  • Kernel Weighting: Triangular kernel weighting giving maximal influence to observations nearest the threshold.
  • Local Regression: Estimating separate linear or polynomial regressions on either side of \(c\) within bandwidth \(h\).
CARD 4

FUZZY RD & DIAGNOSTICS

  • Fuzzy RD: Cutoff induces a discontinuous jump in treatment probability rather than deterministic take-up.
  • Wald / LATE: Instrumental variable ratio identifying the treatment effect for threshold compliers.
  • McCrary Density: Formal test checking for suspicious jumps in the marginal density of the running variable.
  • Sensitivity Checks: Verifying that treatment estimates remain robust across alternative bandwidths and specifications.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: Can a researcher ever empirically prove that the core identifying continuity assumption of Regression Discontinuity holds?

Think about the difference between checking observable implications of an assumption and directly observing the counterfactual outcomes needed to prove it.

Solution:
No.

Researchers can examine implications consistent with the RD identifying assumptions—such as smooth predetermined covariates, absence of suspicious density discontinuities, placebo outcomes, and specification stability—but they cannot directly observe and prove that all unobserved potential-outcome relationships are continuous at the cutoff.

In causal inference, potential counterfactual outcomes in the untreated state for treated units at the cutoff (\(Y_i(0)\) when \(X_i \ge c\)) are inherently unobserved. Statistical tests assess observable refutable predictions, but unconfoundedness/continuity remains fundamentally an identifying assumption that can be supported, but never deductively verified from data alone.

Lesson 15.6 Summary

Regression Discontinuity identifies local treatment effects at a known cutoff when units immediately above and below the threshold are credibly comparable. Sharp RD uses a deterministic treatment rule, while Fuzzy RD uses the discontinuity in treatment probability and a Wald/IV interpretation. Density, placebo, covariate and bandwidth diagnostics can strengthen or weaken the credibility of the design, but they cannot directly prove the continuity of all unobserved potential outcomes.
