LESSON 15.5 FUZZY RD & IV

Fuzzy RD, Instrumental Variables & the Wald Estimator

When threshold crossing shifts treatment probability rather than guaranteeing deterministic compliance, the cutoff acts as an instrumental variable to identify the Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE) via the Wald ratio.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Distinguish Sharp RD (deterministic treatment) from Fuzzy RD (probabilistic take-up jump).
  • Map Fuzzy RD into the Instrumental Variables framework with threshold crossing as the instrument.
  • Formulate and compute the Wald estimator as the ratio of outcome jump to compliance jump at the cutoff.
  • Interpret the cutoff LATE estimand for threshold compliers and identify weak first-stage dangers.

1. Sharp vs. Fuzzy Regression Discontinuity

In Sharp RD, crossing the cutoff \(a_0\) changes treatment status deterministically. However, in many health, labor, and social policy interventions, crossing a threshold alters an individual’s eligibility, incentives, or intensity of encouragement, while individual compliance remains voluntary.

DETERMINISTIC ASSIGNMENT
Sharp RD

Crossing the cutoff deterministically decides treatment receipt with 100% compliance: \[ T_i = \mathbf{1}(a_i \ge a_0), \quad P(D_i = 1 \mid a_i) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } a_i < a_0 \\ 1 & \text{if } a_i \ge a_0 \end{cases} \] The treatment probability jumps from exactly 0 to 1 at the threshold.

PROBABILISTIC ENCOURAGEMENT
Fuzzy RD

Crossing the cutoff causes an abrupt jump in the probability of treatment: \[ \lim_{a \downarrow a_0} P(D_i = 1 \mid a_i) \ne \lim_{a \uparrow a_0} P(D_i = 1 \mid a_i), \quad 0 < \Delta P(D) < 1 \] Treatment probability jumps (e.g. from 0.35 to 0.70). Non-compliance is present.

Visual Comparison: Treatment Probability Profile Across Cutoff

Notice how Sharp RD features a full unit step discontinuity (\(\Delta P = 1.0\)), whereas Fuzzy RD exhibits an imperfect step where the compliance gap \(\Delta P\) is strictly between 0 and 1.

2. Fuzzy RD as an Instrumental Variables (IV) Design

Why can we still identify causal effects when compliance is imperfect? Because crossing the cutoff provides quasi-random exogenous variation in eligibility.

CRITICAL IDENTIFICATION NUANCE

Do not state that crossing the threshold is automatically or unconditionally a valid instrument. Rather: Under the maintained fuzzy-RD identification assumptions (continuity of potential outcomes and no precise manipulation), crossing the threshold provides an instrument for actual treatment receipt.

Mapping Fuzzy RD to the IV Framework
Concept Variable Role in Fuzzy RD
Running Variable \(a_i\) Continuous assignment / forcing score determining eligibility
Cutoff Threshold \(c\) Known policy threshold (e.g., \(c = 65\)) generating the rule
Instrument \(Z_i = \mathbf{1}(a_i \ge c)\) Binary indicator for crossing the eligibility threshold
Actual Treatment \(D_i \in \{0, 1\}\) Actual treatment received (e.g., received vaccine or entered program)
Outcome \(Y_i\) Substantive metric of interest (e.g., health outcome, earnings)
INSTRUMENT Cutoff Crossing \(Z_i\)
→
FIRST STAGE Actual Treatment \(D_i\)
→
OUTCOME Final Outcome \(Y_i\)

Crossing the threshold \(Z_i\) affects outcome \(Y_i\) only through its impact on actual treatment receipt \(D_i\) locally at the threshold (local exclusion restriction).

3. The Wald Estimator at the Cutoff

In Sharp RD, the treatment effect is simply the observed vertical jump in \(Y\) at the cutoff. In Fuzzy RD, however, only a fraction of units crossing the threshold actually take up treatment. If we only looked at the jump in \(Y\), we would underestimate the causal effect on those who complied.

RATIO OF ONE-SIDED LIMITS

The Fuzzy RD treatment effect \(\tau_{\text{FRD}}\) is estimated by scaling the jump in outcome by the jump in take-up probability:

\[ \tau_{\text{FRD}} = \frac{\text{Discontinuity in } Y \text{ at cutoff}}{\text{Discontinuity in } D \text{ at cutoff}} = \frac{\lim_{a \downarrow c} E[Y_i \mid a_i = a] - \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[Y_i \mid a_i = a]}{\lim_{a \downarrow c} E[D_i \mid a_i = a] - \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[D_i \mid a_i = a]} \]
Numerator: Reduced Form Jump

Limit from above \(c\) (\(\lim_{a \downarrow c}\)) minus Limit from below \(c\) (\(\lim_{a \uparrow c}\)). Intention-to-treat jump in outcome \(Y\).

Denominator: First Stage Jump

Limit from above \(c\) (\(\lim_{a \downarrow c}\)) minus Limit from below \(c\) (\(\lim_{a \uparrow c}\)). Increase in compliance \(\Delta D\) induced by crossing \(c\).

CLASSICAL APPLICATION
The Age-65 Flu-Shot Encouragement Design

Consider a public health guideline where individuals aged 65 and older receive free seasonal influenza vaccinations and routine clinical reminders:

  • Running variable (\(a_i\)): Age in days or months.
  • Cutoff (\(c\)): 65.0 years.
  • Instrument (\(Z_i\)): \(\mathbf{1}(\text{Age}_i \ge 65)\) (subsidized eligibility & doctor prompts).
  • Actual treatment (\(D_i\)): \(\mathbf{1}(\text{Received flu shot})\).
  • Outcome (\(Y_i\)): Winter hospitalizations for acute respiratory illness.

Why is this Fuzzy? Some under-65 individuals buy vaccines privately (Always-Takers), while some 65+ individuals decline them (Never-Takers). However, at exactly age 65, vaccination probability jumps sharply (e.g. from 32% to 68%, \(\Delta D = +0.36\)). Scaling the drop in hospitalizations by 0.36 yields the causal protective effect of vaccination for compliers.

4. Derivation Journey: The Fuzzy RD LATE Estimand

Connect the Fuzzy RD Wald estimator directly to the Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE) developed in Module 14:

DERIVATION JOURNEY · LOCAL COMPLIER EFFECT
Step 1: Express Conditional Expectations via Potential Outcomes

Using potential outcomes \(Y_i(1)\) and \(Y_i(0)\), the observed outcome is \(Y_i = Y_i(0) + D_i(Y_i(1) - Y_i(0))\):

\[ \lim_{a \downarrow c} E[Y_i \mid a] = \lim_{a \downarrow c} E[Y_i(0) \mid a] + \lim_{a \downarrow c} E[D_i(Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)) \mid a] \] \[ \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[Y_i \mid a] = \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[Y_i(0) \mid a] + \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[D_i(Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)) \mid a] \]
Step 2: Apply the Continuity Assumption

Under continuity of baseline counterfactuals, \(\lim_{a \downarrow c} E[Y_i(0) \mid a] = \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[Y_i(0) \mid a]\). Subtracting cancels the baseline:

\[ \Delta Y_{\text{cutoff}} = \lim_{a \downarrow c} E[Y_i \mid a] - \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[Y_i \mid a] = \tau_{\text{LATE}}(c) \times \left[ \lim_{a \downarrow c} E[D_i \mid a] - \lim_{a \uparrow c} E[D_i \mid a] \right] \]
Step 3: Solve for the Cutoff LATE

Dividing through by the first-stage compliance jump isolates the causal complier effect:

\[ \tau_{\text{FRD}} = \frac{\Delta Y_{\text{cutoff}}}{\Delta D_{\text{cutoff}}} = E[Y_i(1) - Y_i(0) \mid \text{Complier at } a_i = c] \]
Substantive Result: Fuzzy RD identifies the Local Average Treatment Effect for Compliers right at the cutoff. It does not identify the effect for Always-Takers, Never-Takers, or units located far from the threshold.

WHAT TO NOTICE

As compliance jump \(\Delta D\) shrinks, the Wald ratio escalates rapidly even with moderate outcome jumps.

WHY IT MATTERS

Failing to divide by compliance jump confuses Intention-to-Treat (ITT) with the Treatment-on-Treated effect.

ECONOMETRIC LOGIC

Fuzzy RD is two-stage least squares (2SLS) in a local window with cutoff indicator \(Z_i\) as instrument.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: Why does Fuzzy RD divide the outcome jump at the cutoff by the treatment-probability jump?

Think about what fraction of units crossing the cutoff actually received the treatment, and what would happen to the estimated effect if only half of them took it up.

Solution:
Because crossing the cutoff changes treatment receipt imperfectly; the ratio scales the reduced-form outcome discontinuity by the first-stage discontinuity in treatment.

Non-compliers (Always-Takers and Never-Takers) do not change their treatment status at the cutoff. Dividing by the first-stage jump \(\Delta D\) scales the outcome jump up to recover the genuine treatment effect for compliers at the threshold.

Lesson 15.5 Summary

When treatment take-up is imperfect at the threshold, Fuzzy RD treats cutoff crossing as an instrumental variable for actual treatment receipt. The resulting Wald estimator rescales the reduced-form outcome discontinuity by the first-stage jump in treatment probability, identifying the Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE) for compliers at the cutoff. When compliance jumps are small, estimators risk weak-instrument bias and instability.
