LESSON 15.3 ESTIMATION & SPECIFICATION

RD Regression Specifications & Treatment Effects at the Cutoff

Formulate parametric RD regression models, understand why centering the running variable is essential for direct interpretation, and learn modern cautions regarding polynomial orders.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Write and estimate the baseline linear RD regression model.
  • Demonstrate why centering the running variable at the cutoff makes the treatment coefficient directly interpretable.
  • Specify interacted models allowing separate slopes and curvatures on either side of the cutoff.
  • Understand the modern econometric caution against high-order polynomials (Gelman & Imbens 2019).

1. Linear Specification & Controlling for the Forcing Variable

We control for the effect of the running variable \(a_i\) (and other smooth baseline factors) by including functional forms of \(a_i\) directly in the regression model alongside the treatment indicator \(T_i\).

BASELINE LINEAR RD SPECIFICATION Common Slope Model
\[ Y_i = \alpha + \beta T_i + \gamma a_i + \varepsilon_i \]

Here, \(\alpha\) is the baseline intercept, \(\gamma\) models the linear relationship between the running variable \(a_i\) and the outcome \(Y_i\), and \(\beta\) captures the vertical discontinuity (treatment jump) when \(T_i\) switches from 0 to 1.

2. Centering the Running Variable at the Cutoff

A vital standard practice in applied Regression Discontinuity is to center the running variable at the cutoff threshold \(a_0\):

CENTERED RUNNING VARIABLE DEFINITION
\[ \tilde{a}_i = a_i - a_0 \]

The Mathematical Advantage: Directly at the threshold, \(a_i = a_0 \implies \tilde{a}_i = 0\). Centering ensures that distance from the threshold is zero at the policy boundary. When interactions and higher-order terms are included, the coefficient \(\beta\) on \(T_i\) is directly and transparently interpreted as the treatment effect at the cutoff.

3. Polynomial Functional Forms & Modern Best Practices

Depending on the curvature of the true relationship between the outcome and the forcing variable, researchers specify linear or low-order polynomial terms:

LINEAR SPECIFICATION
\[ Y_i = \alpha + \beta T_i + \gamma a_i + \varepsilon_i \]

Assumes a constant linear slope \(\gamma\) on both sides of the cutoff.

QUADRATIC SPECIFICATION
\[ Y_i = \alpha + \beta T_i + \gamma_1 a_i + \gamma_2 a_i^2 + \varepsilon_i \]

Allows smooth parabolic curvature in the underlying outcome profile.

CUBIC SPECIFICATION
\[ Y_i = \alpha + \beta T_i + \gamma_1 a_i + \gamma_2 a_i^2 + \gamma_3 a_i^3 + \varepsilon_i \]

Allows an inflection point; historically popular but now used with caution.

METHODOLOGICAL CAUTION Why Modern Econometrics Discourages High-Order Polynomials

While cubic, quartic, and higher-order polynomials were common 15–20 years ago, modern econometric consensus strongly advises against global high-order polynomials (Gelman & Imbens, 2019).

  • Boundary Instability: High-order polynomials exhibit erratic Runge-type oscillations near boundary points, distorting fitted values right at the cutoff \(a_0\).
  • Excessive Weight on Distant Data: A distant outlier can swing the fitted polynomial curve, artificially creating or masking a discontinuity at the threshold.
  • Overfitting: High-order curves fit sample noise rather than the true underlying conditional expectation.

Current Best Practice: Use local linear or local quadratic models within an appropriately chosen local bandwidth window around \(a_0\).

4. Allowing Separate Slopes: Interacted Specifications

Forcing the regression line to have the exact same slope and curvature on both sides of the cutoff is an unnecessary and restrictive assumption. Treatment may alter not just the level of the outcome, but also how the outcome responds to the running variable.

FULL INTERACTED SPECIFICATION (SEPARATE SLOPES & CURVATURES)
\[ \begin{aligned} Y_i = \; &\alpha + \beta T_i + \gamma_1 \tilde{a}_i + \gamma_2 \tilde{a}_i^2 \\ &+ \delta_1 (\tilde{a}_i \cdot T_i) + \delta_2 (\tilde{a}_i^2 \cdot T_i) + \varepsilon_i \end{aligned} \]

Where \(\tilde{a}_i = a_i - a_0\). The control side (\(T_i = 0\)) has linear slope \(\gamma_1\) and quadratic curvature \(\gamma_2\). The treated side (\(T_i = 1\)) has linear slope \((\gamma_1 + \delta_1)\) and curvature \((\gamma_2 + \delta_2)\).

DERIVATION JOURNEY · TREATMENT EFFECT AT THE CUTOFF
Step 1: Formulate Marginal Treatment Difference

Compute the partial difference of the expected outcome with respect to treatment indicator \(T_i\):

\[ \frac{\partial Y_i}{\partial T_i} = \beta + \delta_1 (a_i - a_0) + \delta_2 (a_i - a_0)^2 \]

Notice: Away from the threshold (\(a_i \ne a_0\)), the additional terms \(\delta_1(a_i - a_0)\) and \(\delta_2(a_i - a_0)^2\) are non-zero. The treatment-control difference varies with distance from the cutoff.

Step 2: Evaluate Exactly at the Cutoff Boundary (\(a_i = a_0\))

Substitute \(a_i = a_0\) into the partial derivative:

\[ \left. \frac{\partial Y_i}{\partial T_i} \right|_{a_i = a_0} = \beta + \delta_1 (0) + \delta_2 (0)^2 = \beta \]
Step 3: Econometric Conclusion
Key Theorem: In this centered interacted specification, \(\beta\) isolates the exact causal discontinuity at the cutoff. Researchers do not need to assume that the treatment effect is constant across all values of \(a_i\); \(\beta\) directly captures the local boundary jump.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: Why do applied econometricians center the running variable at the cutoff (\(\tilde{a}_i = a_i - a_0\)) before estimating interacted RD models?

Consider what the distance term \((a_i - a_0)\) evaluates to right at the policy threshold \(a_i = a_0\).

Solution:
Because distance from the cutoff becomes zero at the threshold, making the treatment coefficient directly interpretable as the discontinuity there in the interacted specification.

In an interacted regression, the effect of \(T_i\) is \(\beta + \delta_1(a_i - a_0) + \dots\). By defining \(\tilde{a}_i = a_i - a_0\), the interaction terms vanish at the boundary (\(a_i = a_0\)), so \(\beta\) measures the exact treatment discontinuity at the cutoff without requiring additional linear combinations or manual adjustments.

Lesson 15.3 Summary

Parametric and local RD regressions isolate the treatment discontinuity by modeling outcome trends on either side of the cutoff. Centering the running variable at the threshold (\(\tilde{a}_i = a_i - a_0\)) ensures that the treatment dummy coefficient directly measures the causal jump at the policy boundary. Interacting treatment with centered distance permits distinct slopes and curvatures, while modern practice relies on local linear specifications to avoid boundary instability from high-order polynomials.
