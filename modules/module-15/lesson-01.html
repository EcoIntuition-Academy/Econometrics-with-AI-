LESSON 15.1 RD FOUNDATIONS

RD Intuition, Cutoffs & Sharp Regression Discontinuity

Discover how administrative cutoffs create quasi-experimental variation, understand the local 'as-if random' identifying comparison, and analyze the mechanics of Sharp Regression Discontinuity.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define the running/forcing variable, treatment cutoff, and treatment indicator.
  • Articulate the core intuition of local comparability near the threshold.
  • Contrast global comparisons with local threshold comparisons using the 50% unionization vote example.
  • Specify Sharp Regression Discontinuity deterministically using indicator notation and examine the score-500 academy application.

1. Regression Discontinuity: Definition & Core Notation

FORMAL DEFINITION · REGRESSION DISCONTINUITY (RD)

In a Regression Discontinuity research design, individuals wind up in the treatment group if they cross a predetermined threshold or cutoff. Treatment assignment is determined by whether an observed continuous variable—termed the running, assignment, or forcing variable—exceeds a known threshold value.

RUNNING VARIABLE
\(a_i\)

The continuous assignment or forcing variable (e.g., test score, vote share, age, wealth).

KNOWN CUTOFF
\(a_0\)

The explicit threshold or policy boundary governing eligibility (e.g., 50% vote, 500 points).

TREATMENT INDICATOR
\(T_i\)

Binary treatment status (\(T_i = 1\) if treated, \(0\) if control) triggered at or near \(a_0\).

2. Core Intuition: Local "As-If Random" Variation

Why is Regression Discontinuity so widely respected in modern applied econometrics? In non-experimental observational data, treated and untreated units often differ fundamentally across countless unmeasured dimensions (e.g., motivation, innate ability, or managerial skill), causing severe omitted variable bias.

IDENTIFICATION LOGIC

The Power of Local Threshold Comparisons

Regression Discontinuity allows researchers to estimate causal effects in non-experimental settings where treatment assignment is as-if random in a narrow window near a cutoff. Units situated immediately above and immediately below the threshold are virtually indistinguishable in both observable background characteristics and unobserved factors.

Crucial Precision: RD does not claim that units with very high and very low values of \(a_i\) are comparable. The identifying comparison is strictly local: under maintained continuity assumptions, any sudden jump in the outcome occurring exactly at the cutoff can be attributed to the causal effect of treatment.

CANONICAL APPLICATION · UNIONIZATION VOTE SHARE

Does Unionization Increase Wages?

When a workplace unionizes, comparing unionized firms to non-unionized firms generally conflates the union causal effect with confounding firm characteristics (e.g., industry conditions, worker discontent, plant size).

The RD Solution: Compare elections where workers narrowly voted to unionize (e.g., 51% in favor) with elections where unionization narrowly failed (e.g., 49% in favor). Within this narrow band around the 50% cutoff, economic conditions, firm profitability, and worker attitudes are nearly identical. Any discontinuous difference in subsequent wages or plant survival on either side of 50% isolates the causal effect of unionization.

3. Graphical Representation of Regression Discontinuity

Graphically, Regression Discontinuity is illustrated by plotting the running variable \(a_i\) on the horizontal axis and the outcome variable \(Y_i\) on the vertical axis. The threshold \(a_0\) is marked with a vertical dashed line.

THE FUNDAMENTAL RD GRAPH

Outcome vs. Running Variable at Cutoff \(a_0\)

Observed Data Control Fit (\(a < a_0\)) Treated Fit (\(a \ge a_0\)) Cutoff \(a_0\) Treatment Effect \(eta\)
Interpretation: The lines fitted on either side are extrapolated to the boundary \(a_0\). The vertical gap between the right-side limit \(Y^+(a_0)\) and the left-side limit \(Y^-(a_0)\) estimates the causal effect \(eta\) at the threshold.

4. Sharp Regression Discontinuity & The Academy Exam

SHARP REGRESSION DISCONTINUITY DEFINITION

In a Sharp Regression Discontinuity Design, crossing the threshold deterministically assigns an individual to treatment. There is zero noncompliance: every unit above the threshold receives treatment, and no unit below receives treatment.

\[ T_i = \mathbf{1}(a_i \ge a_0) = egin{cases} 1 & ext{if } a_i \ge a_0 \ 0 & ext{if } a_i < a_0 \end{cases} \]
CANONICAL APPLICATION · ACADEMIC TEST SCORE CUTOFF

Special Academy Admission at Score 500

Consider students taking a standardized exam with eligibility rules:

  • Students scoring \(a_i \ge 500\) attend an elite special academy (\(T_i = 1\)).
  • Students scoring \(a_i < 500\) attend their local regular school (\(T_i = 0\)).

Comparing Score 499 vs. 500: Does a student with a score of 500 possess inherently superior intellectual capacity compared to a student who scored 499? Almost certainly not—a single multiple-choice question or slight testing noise accounts for the 1-point difference. Under the RD assumptions, units sufficiently close to the cutoff are treated as locally comparable. The discrete jump in their subsequent educational outcomes isolates the causal impact of attending the special academy.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: Why does Regression Discontinuity focus on observations close to the cutoff rather than using all observations equally across the sample?

Consider whether units with extreme values of the running variable (e.g., test score of 200 vs 800) differ in ways other than just program attendance.

Solution:
Because the identifying comparison is local: units immediately above and below the threshold are expected to be most comparable under the RD assumptions.

Units far from the cutoff typically differ substantially in unobserved factors (such as baseline productivity, socioeconomic status, or student aptitude). Comparing all treated units to all control units would reintroduce massive selection bias. In contrast, units narrowly separated by the cutoff threshold are as-if randomly assigned around the eligibility rule.

Lesson 15.1 Summary

Regression Discontinuity designs exploit deterministic or probabilistic policy cutoffs along a continuous running variable. The identifying intuition is local: units immediately above and below the cutoff are treated as locally comparable, creating an "as-if" randomized quasi-experiment. In Sharp RD, crossing the threshold deterministically assigns treatment, and the treatment effect is graphically identified as the vertical jump at the threshold boundary. Next, we examine the core identifying assumptions: continuity of potential outcomes and the absence of strategic sorting.
