LESSON 2.3 Linear Operators

Projection & Residual-Maker Matrices

Explore \(P\) and \(M\) as geometric matrix operators: mapping vectors onto the regressor subspace, extracting orthogonal residuals, and examining their fundamental idempotent and symmetric algebraic properties.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Define the projection matrix \(P = X(X'X)^{-1}X'\) and prove why \(\hat{y} = Py\).
  • Define the residual-maker matrix \(M = I - P\) and prove why \(e = My\).
  • Express the fundamental regression decomposition in operator form: \(y = Py + My = \hat{y} + e\).
  • Understand and verify the algebraic properties of genuine projection operators: idempotence (\(P^2 = P, M^2 = M\)) and symmetry (\(P' = P, M' = M\)).
  • Demonstrate that \(PM = MP = 0\), establishing mutual orthogonality.

1. The Two Fundamental Linear Operators

The geometry of OLS suggests two very special \(n \times n\) linear operators:

  • The Projection Matrix (\(P\)): Keeps only the component of a vector that lives in the subspace spanned by \(X\) (\(\text{Col}(X)\)).
  • The Residual-Maker Matrix (\(M\)): Strips away any component in \(\text{Col}(X)\) and leaves only the component that is orthogonal to that subspace.
PROJECTION OPERATOR The Projection Matrix P ("Hat Matrix")
$$P = X(X'X)^{-1}X' \implies \hat{y} = Py$$
WHERE
\(P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}\) Projection matrix: Linear operator mapping any vector in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) onto \(\text{Col}(X)\)
\(\hat{y} = Py\) Fitted vector: Since \(\hat{\beta} = (X'X)^{-1}X'y\), \(Py = X(X'X)^{-1}X'y = X\hat{\beta}\)

The residual-maker matrix \(M\) is defined by subtracting \(P\) from the identity operator:

RESIDUAL-MAKER OPERATOR The Annihilator Matrix M
$$M = I_n - P = I_n - X(X'X)^{-1}X' \implies e = My$$
WHERE
\(M \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}\) Residual-maker matrix: Projects any vector onto the orthogonal complement \(\text{Col}(X)^\perp\)
\(e = My\) Sample residual vector: \(My = (I - P)y = y - Py = y - \hat{y}\)

2. Compact Regression Decomposition

In perfectly compact operator form, every observed outcome vector \(y\) is partitioned into its explained and unexplained components:

$$y = \underbrace{Py}_{\hat{y} \in \text{Col}(X)} + \underbrace{My}_{e \perp \text{Col}(X)}$$

Because \(P + M = P + (I - P) = I\), the two operators completely partition the \(n\)-dimensional observation space into two mutually orthogonal vector subspaces.

3. Algebraic Properties of Orthogonal Projection Operators

Under the standard Euclidean inner product, a real square matrix represents an orthogonal projection if and only if it is both idempotent and symmetric. Both \(P\) and \(M\) satisfy these fundamental properties:

1. IDEMPOTENCE

\[P^2 = P \quad \text{and} \quad M^2 = M\]

Meaning: Applying the same projection twice gives the same result as applying it once. Once a point's shadow is cast onto the subspace \(\operatorname{Col}(X)\), projecting the shadow again changes nothing.

2. SYMMETRY

\[P' = P \quad \text{and} \quad M' = M\]

Meaning: Both operators equal their transposes. Under the Euclidean inner product, symmetry ensures that projection lines meet the subspace at exact \(90^\circ\) angles, minimizing distance.

3. COMPLEMENTARY DECOMPOSITION

\[PM = MP = \mathbf{0}\]

Meaning: The fitted component \(\hat{y} = Py\) and residual vector \(e = My\) lie in mutually orthogonal subspaces. Their inner product is identically zero: \(\hat{y}'e = y'P'My = \mathbf{0}\).

4. TRACE & RANK

\[\operatorname{tr}(P) = k, \quad \operatorname{tr}(M) = n - k\]

Meaning: The trace of an idempotent matrix equals its rank. \(P\) projects onto the \(k\)-dimensional regressor space, while \(M\) projects onto the \((n - k)\)-dimensional residual space.

◆ WHY IT MATTERS

Whenever you see \(My\) in advanced econometrics (e.g., Frisch–Waugh–Lovell or Fixed Effects), you immediately know it means: "the residuals from regressing \(y\) on \(X\)."

📊 ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

\(M\) acts as a "purging filter": applying \(M\) to any vector removes all linear correlation with the columns of \(X\).
