LESSON 2.7 Algebraic Specialization

Demeaning & the Covariance Form of OLS

Specialize the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem to the intercept constant term: derive the demeaning operator \(M_0\), prove slope invariance, and connect the OLS slope to sample covariance and variance.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Define the demeaning matrix operator \(M_0 = I - \frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'\) using precise vector notation.
  • Show that \(M_0 z = z - \bar{z}\iota\) subtracts the sample mean from every observation.
  • Apply FWL to prove that regressing demeaned \(y\) on demeaned \(X_2\) yields identical slope coefficients.
  • Recover the intercept post-estimation using \(\hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \bar{x}'\hat{\beta}_2\).
  • Derive the familiar simple regression slope \(\hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\text{Cov}(x,y)}{\text{Var}(x)}\) and understand its co-movement interpretation.

1. Demeaning as a Special Case of FWL

Now specialize the FWL partition \(X = [X_1 \;\; X_2]\) by setting \(X_1\) to be just the constant term. Let:

$$X = \begin{bmatrix} \iota & X_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

where \(\iota\) is the \(n \times 1\) vector of ones and \(X_2\) contains the non-constant explanatory variables.

The residual-maker matrix for the vector of ones is:

$$M_0 = I_n - \iota(\iota'\iota)^{-1}\iota'$$

Notice that \(\iota'\iota = \sum_{i=1}^n (1)^2 = n\). Therefore, \(M_0\) simplifies to:

$$M_0 = I_n - \frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'$$
DEMEANING OPERATOR The Mean-Deviation Matrix M₀
$$M_0 z = z - \frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'z = z - \bar{z}\iota$$
WHERE
\(\iota'z = \sum_{i=1}^n z_i\) Inner product: Sum of all elements in vector \(z\)
\(\frac{1}{n}\iota\iota'z = \bar{z}\iota\) Mean vector: An \(n \times 1\) vector whose entries all equal the sample mean \(\bar{z}\)
\(M_0 z\) Demeaned vector: Subtracts the arithmetic sample mean from each coordinate: \([z_1 - \bar{z}, \dots, z_n - \bar{z}]^\top\)

2. OLS on Mean-Adjusted Variables

Now apply the demeaning operator \(M_0\) to both \(X_2\) and \(y\):

$$X_2^* = M_0X_2 = X_2 - \bar{X}_2, \qquad y^* = M_0y = y - \bar{y}\iota$$

The Frisch–Waugh–Lovell formula tells us that the slope coefficients on \(X_2\) in a regression with an intercept are:

$$\hat{\beta}_2 = (X_2^{*'}X_2^*)^{-1}X_2^{*'}y^*$$

Two Equivalent Approaches to OLS

Rather than estimating the regression with an explicit constant vector \(\iota\), one can equivalently:

  1. Subtract sample means from \(y\) and from all non-constant regressors in \(X_2\).
  2. Drop the constant term completely and run regression through the origin on the demeaned variables.

The estimated slope coefficients \(\hat{\beta}_2\) are identically the same! The intercept is then recovered post-estimation: $$\hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \bar{x}_2'\hat{\beta}_2$$

3. The Covariance Form of the Simple Regression Slope

This specialization leads directly to the familiar scalar formula for the slope in simple linear regression. With a single scalar regressor \(x\) and outcome \(y\):

$$x^* = x - \bar{x}\iota, \qquad y^* = y - \bar{y}\iota$$

The slope formula \(\hat{\beta}_1 = (x^{*'}x^*)^{-1}x^{*'}y^*\) becomes:

SAMPLE MOMENT FORM Simple Regression Slope as Covariance / Variance
$$\hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(x, y)}{\text{Var}(x)}$$
WHERE
\(\text{Cov}(x, y)\) Sample covariance: Measures how much \(x\) and \(y\) move together
\(\text{Var}(x)\) Sample variance of \(x\): Measures how much \(x\) moves on its own
💡 Sample Normalization Note

The division \(\text{Cov}(x,y) / \text{Var}(x)\) holds whether you divide both numerator and denominator by \(n\) or by the degrees-of-freedom degrees \(n-1\), because the identical factor cancels completely.
Academic Note: Do not casually call \(\hat{\beta}_1\) a "standardized" coefficient. A standardized regression coefficient normalizes both \(x\) and \(y\) by their standard deviations, yielding the unit-free Pearson correlation \(r_{xy}\). The slope \(\hat{\beta}_1\) is not unit-free; it has units of \(\text{units of } y / \text{units of } x\).

◆ WHY IT MATTERS

This demeaning transformation is the exact mathematical foundation of the Within Estimator used throughout Panel Data econometrics to eliminate unobserved fixed effects!

📊 ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

The slope \(\hat{\beta}_1\) reflects pure co-variation: how deviations from average \(x\) accompany deviations from average \(y\).
