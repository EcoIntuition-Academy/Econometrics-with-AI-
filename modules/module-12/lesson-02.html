LESSON 12.2 Overidentification

Testing Instrument Validity: Sargan & Overidentification

Learn how overidentifying restrictions allow formal econometric testing of instrument orthogonality, discover why statistical rejection does not automatically prove invalidity, and explore the crucial role of heterogeneous treatment effects and LATE.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why overidentification is required for the Sargan test.
  • Distinguish exactly identified from overidentified IV models.
  • Interpret what a rejection of overidentifying restrictions does and does not establish.
  • Connect heterogeneous treatment effects and LATE to instrument disagreement.
Section 1

1. The Sargan Overidentification Test

DEFINITION

Sargan Test

The Sargan test uses overidentifying restrictions to assess whether the instrument set is jointly compatible with the structural model.

REQUIREMENT

\(L > K\): The number of excluded instruments (\(L\)) must strictly exceed the number of endogenous regressors (\(K\)).

EXACTLY IDENTIFIED (\(L = K\))

No Testable Restrictions

There are no overidentifying restrictions available for testing. The moment conditions \(Z'\hat{\varepsilon} = 0\) hold exactly by construction.

OVERIDENTIFIED (\(L > K\))

\(L - K\) Testable Restrictions

Additional instrument restrictions can be compared with the fitted model. If instruments identify conflicting parameter values, the test rejects.

SARGAN TEST STATISTIC

Auxiliary Regression: Regress 2SLS residuals on the instrument set:

$$\hat{\varepsilon}_{2\text{SLS}} = Z\gamma + e$$

Test Statistic & Asymptotic Distribution:

$$S = n R^2 \stackrel{d}{\longrightarrow} \chi^2_{L - K}$$
WHERE: \(n\) = sample size · \(R^2\) = \(R^2\) from the auxiliary regression · \(L - K\) = number of overidentifying restrictions.
Section 2

2. Interpreting Sargan Rejections & LATE

IMPORTANT CAUTION

Rejection Does Not Identify Which Assumption Failed

Rejecting the overidentification test does not automatically identify which instrument or assumption caused the rejection. It establishes statistical tension between the instruments and the joint model assumptions.
1. INSTRUMENT INVALIDITY

Excluded Channel Violation

An instrument may be correlated with the structural disturbance or affect the outcome through an excluded channel.

2. HETEROGENEOUS EFFECTS

Local Effect Divergence

Different valid instruments may identify different local causal effects across distinct sub-populations.

3. DIFFERENT MARGINS

Distinct Sources of Variation

Different instruments can move \(X\) through different economic margins, generating distinct responses.

WORKED INTUITION

Instrument Disagreement: Quarter of Birth vs. Distance to College

Instrument A: Quarter of Birth

May move schooling for individuals on the margin of dropping out of high school.

Instrument B: Distance to College

May move college attendance for individuals deciding whether to enroll in higher education.

Key Point: Even if both instruments are valid, they may identify effects for different subpopulations when treatment effects are heterogeneous. When the null is rejected, it does not automatically establish instrument invalidity.

LATE

Local Average Treatment Effect

An IV estimate describes the average causal effect for units whose treatment status changes because of the instrument. These units are termed compliers. Because distinct instruments induce compliance among different groups, their respective estimates can naturally disagree.

ROBUST VERSION

Sargan-Hansen J-Test

Robust Sargan-Hansen-style versions are used when classical homoskedastic assumptions are inappropriate, evaluating overidentifying restrictions under robust weighting matrices.

EXACT IDENTIFICATION

Overidentification test is not available when \(L = K\). Instrument validity still requires substantive economic justification.
WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

Geometric moment conditions. Each instrument defines a line whose root determines a candidate parameter value for \(\beta\).

WHAT TO NOTICE

In the overidentified cases (\(L = 2\) and \(L = 3\)), the lines do not intersect at the exact same zero point, creating testable residual tension measured by the Sargan test.

WHY IT MATTERS

Overidentification is the only statistical scenario where instrument orthogonality can be subjected to empirical scrutiny rather than pure institutional assumption.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

A failure to reject supports mutual consistency of the instrument set under the assumption of homogeneous treatment effects.

QUICK CHECK

Testing With Equal Instruments and Regressors

Can the Sargan test of overidentifying restrictions be performed when the number of excluded instruments \(L\) equals the number of endogenous regressors \(K\)?

HINT: Consider the degrees of freedom \(L - K\) of the asymptotic \(\chi^2\) distribution.

ANSWER: No, the Sargan test cannot be performed when \(L = K\).

WHY? When \(L = K\), the model is exactly identified. The IV estimator satisfies the sample moment conditions \(Z'\hat{\varepsilon} = 0\) perfectly by construction, leaving zero unconstrained degrees of freedom (\(L - K = 0\)). Overidentifying restrictions exist only when \(L > K\).

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The Sargan test requires an overidentified IV model.
  • Exactly identified models have no overidentifying restrictions to test.
  • Rejection indicates tension between the instrument set and the maintained model assumptions.
  • Rejection does not by itself reveal which instrument or assumption failed.
  • Heterogeneous treatment effects can complicate interpretation of overidentification tests.
