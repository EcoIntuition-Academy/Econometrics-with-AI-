LESSON 12.4 Measurement Error

Measurement Error in Dependent and Independent Variables

Classical measurement error has different consequences depending on whether the noise enters the outcome or the regressor.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Define classical measurement error in \(y\) and \(x\).
  • Explain why classical error in \(y\) increases uncertainty without creating slope bias under the maintained assumptions.
  • Explain why classical error in \(x\) creates regressor–error correlation and attenuation bias.
  • Compare the econometric consequences of outcome noise and regressor noise.
Section 1

1. Classical Measurement-Error Framework

FOUNDATIONAL PREMISE

Location Determines the Econometric Consequence

Observed economic variables are often noisy. The key question is whether the measurement error enters \(y\) or \(x\), because the econometric consequences differ sharply.
TRUE STRUCTURAL MODEL
$$y^* = \alpha + x^*\beta + \varepsilon, \quad \text{with } \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid x^*] = 0$$

\(y^*\) and \(x^*\) represent the true underlying latent constructs of economic interest.

OBSERVED OUTCOME

\(y = y^* + v\)

Where \(v\) is additive measurement error contaminating the dependent variable.

OBSERVED REGRESSOR

\(x = x^* + u\)

Where \(u\) is additive measurement error contaminating the explanatory variable.

CLASSICAL MEASUREMENT-ERROR ASSUMPTIONS
  • Measurement errors have mean zero: \(\mathbb{E}[v] = 0\) and \(\mathbb{E}[u] = 0\).
  • Measurement noise is uncorrelated with the true variables in the maintained setup: \(\text{Cov}(v, x^*) = 0\) and \(\text{Cov}(u, x^*) = 0\).
  • Measurement noise is uncorrelated with the structural disturbance: \(\text{Cov}(v, \varepsilon) = 0\) and \(\text{Cov}(u, \varepsilon) = 0\).
  • Outcome- and regressor-measurement errors are independent where both are considered: \(\text{Cov}(v, u) = 0\).
$$\text{Cov}(v, u) = 0, \quad \text{Cov}(v, x^*) = 0, \quad \text{Cov}(u, x^*) = 0, \quad \text{Cov}(v, \varepsilon) = 0, \quad \text{Cov}(u, \varepsilon) = 0$$
Section 2

2. Measurement Error in the Dependent Variable

ALGEBRAIC DERIVATION

Measurement Error in the Dependent Variable

GOAL: Determine whether classical noise in \(y\) changes the OLS slope.
1

Observed Outcome

We observe \(y\), not the true latent outcome \(y^*\):

\[y = y^* + v \implies y^* = y - v\]
2

Substitute Into the True Model

Substitute \(y^* = y - v\) into the structural equation \(y^* = \alpha + x^*\beta + \varepsilon\) (assuming \(x = x^*\)):

\[y = \alpha + x^*\beta + (\varepsilon + v)\]

Define the composite disturbance \(\varepsilon' = \varepsilon + v\):

\[y = \alpha + x^*\beta + \varepsilon'\]
3

Check Exogeneity

Under the maintained classical assumptions, the added measurement noise \(v\) is unrelated to \(x^*\):

\[\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon' \mid x^*] = \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon + v \mid x^*] = \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid x^*] + \mathbb{E}[v \mid x^*] = 0\]
4

Variance Consequence

Under zero covariance between structural shock \(\varepsilon\) and noise \(v\), the error variance expands:

\[\text{Var}(\varepsilon') = \text{Var}(\varepsilon + v) = \sigma^2_\varepsilon + \sigma^2_v\]
KEY RESULT
Classical measurement error in \(y\) does not create slope attenuation under the maintained assumptions. It increases disturbance variance and reduces precision.
BIG PICTURE Vertical Noise Only

Noise in the outcome makes the data noisier vertically, but it does not contaminate the explanatory variable.

UNBIASED, BUT LESS PRECISE

Under the classical assumptions, measurement error in \(y\) leaves the OLS slope centered on \(\beta\) but increases sampling uncertainty.

Section 3

3. Measurement Error in the Independent Variable

ALGEBRAIC DERIVATION

Measurement Error in the Independent Variable

GOAL: Show why classical measurement error in \(x\) creates endogeneity.
1

Observed Regressor

The observed regressor \(x\) is contaminated by measurement noise \(u\):

\[x = x^* + u \implies x^* = x - u\]
2

Substitute Into the Model

Substitute \(x^* = x - u\) into the true equation \(y = \alpha + x^*\beta + \varepsilon\) (assuming \(y = y^*\)):

\[y = \alpha + (x - u)\beta + \varepsilon = \alpha + x\beta + (\varepsilon - \beta u)\]

Define the composite disturbance \(\omega = \varepsilon - \beta u\):

\[y = \alpha + x\beta + \omega\]
3

Notice the Shared Noise

Observed \(x\) contains \(+u\), while composite disturbance \(\omega\) contains \(-\beta u\):

\[x = x^* + u \quad \text{and} \quad \omega = \varepsilon - \beta u\]

Therefore, \(x\) and \(\omega\) share the same measurement-error component.

4

Compute Covariance

Under the maintained classical assumptions:

\[\text{Cov}(x, \omega) = \text{Cov}(x^* + u, \varepsilon - \beta u) = -\beta \text{Var}(u) = -\beta \sigma^2_u\]

Therefore, \(\text{Cov}(x, \omega) \neq 0\) whenever \(\beta \neq 0\) and measurement-error variance is positive.

KEY RESULT
Observed \(x\) is endogenous.
BIG PICTURE Structural Contamination

Measurement error in \(x\) contaminates both the regressor and the composite disturbance. This is why the problem is much more serious than measurement error in \(y\).

NEXT LESSON

In Lesson 12.5, we will derive the exact attenuation factor \(\lambda\) and show how instrumental variables can restore consistency.

Section 4

4. Comparing Measurement Error in y and x

INFORMATION / NEUTRAL

Error in Outcome (y)

Where noise enters: Outcome (\(y = y^* + v\))
Composite error: \(\varepsilon' = \varepsilon + v\)
Exogeneity: Preserved under classical assumptions
Main effect: Lower precision; inflated standard errors
AMBER CAUTION

Error in Regressor (x)

Where noise enters: Regressor (\(x = x^* + u\))
Composite error: \(\omega = \varepsilon - \beta u\)
Exogeneity: Violated (\(\text{Cov}(x, \omega) = -\beta \sigma^2_u \neq 0\))
Main effect: Attenuation bias / inconsistency toward zero

6. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Why is classical measurement error in \(x\) more damaging to OLS than classical measurement error in \(y\)?

HINT: Ask whether the observed regressor becomes correlated with the regression disturbance.

ANSWER: Measurement error in \(x\) causes regressor endogeneity.

WHY? Measurement error in \(x\) enters both the observed regressor and the composite disturbance, creating endogeneity. Classical error in \(y\) instead enters the outcome disturbance and mainly reduces precision under the maintained assumptions.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Classical measurement error means observed variables differ from their underlying true values because of noise.
  • Under the maintained classical assumptions, error in \(y\) increases disturbance variance but does not attenuate the OLS slope.
  • Error in \(x\) enters both the observed regressor and the composite disturbance.
  • This creates endogeneity and leads to attenuation bias under the classical setup.
  • The next lesson derives the attenuation factor and shows how IV can address the problem.
