LESSON 12.3 Instrument Strength

Instrument Strength & Weak-Instrument Diagnostics

Explore why instrument validity is insufficient without strong relevance, evaluate finite-sample problems associated with weak instruments, and interpret first-stage diagnostic rules of thumb.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why IV precision deteriorates when instruments weakly predict \(X\).
  • Recognize the major finite-sample problems associated with weak instruments.
  • Interpret first-stage \(F\)-statistics as strength diagnostics at the source's level.
  • Distinguish strength diagnostics from instrument validity.
FOUNDATIONAL PREMISE

Relevance Is Indispensable

Weak instruments can produce large variance and severe finite-sample problems. When an instrument satisfies orthogonality with the structural error but correlates weakly with the endogenous regressor, 2SLS precision deteriorates rapidly.
Section 1

1. Instrument Relevance & Asymptotic Variance

DEFINITION

Instrument Relevance

An instrument must predict the endogenous regressor. Weak relevance means the first stage provides little independent variation in \(X\).

ASYMPTOTIC VARIANCE

Asymptotic Variance of the IV Estimator

$$\text{AsyVar}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \left[ \left(\frac{X'Z}{n}\right) \left(\frac{Z'Z}{n}\right)^{-1} \left(\frac{Z'X}{n}\right) \right]^{-1}$$

Notice the sample covariance term \(\frac{Z'X}{n}\) appearing inside the matrix inverse.

AS RELEVANCE WEAKENS

Information from \(Z\) about \(X\) decreases  →  IV variance increases  →  estimates become less precise.

Five Major Finite-Sample Problems of Weak Instruments

1. LARGE VARIANCE

Imprecise Estimates

IV estimates become imprecise with wide confidence intervals as the denominator covariance approaches zero.

2. FINITE-SAMPLE BIAS

Shift Toward OLS

Weak instruments can generate substantial finite-sample bias, pulling 2SLS estimates directly toward the OLS estimate.

3. MANY WEAK INSTRUMENTS

Compounding Bias

Adding weak instruments can make the finite-sample problem worse, as fitted values \(\hat{X}\) overfit sample noise in the disturbance.

4. SENSITIVITY TO VIOLATIONS

Magnified departures

Weak relevance can magnify the effect of small departures from instrument validity.

5. POOR NORMAL APPROXIMATION

Distorted Inference

Conventional large-sample Gaussian approximations can perform poorly, producing heavy tails and distorted Wald test coverage.

SENSITIVITY TO VALIDITY VIOLATIONS
$$\text{plim}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}}) - \beta = \frac{\text{Cov}(z, \varepsilon)}{\text{Cov}(z, x)}$$

When \(\text{Cov}(z, x)\) is small, a given \(\text{Cov}(z, \varepsilon)\) can have a larger effect on the IV estimate.

Section 2

2. First-Stage Regression & Diagnostic Thresholds

FIRST-STAGE REGRESSION
$$X = Z_C \Theta + Z_E \theta + \nu$$

Regress endogenous regressor \(X\) on included controls \(Z_C\) and excluded instruments \(Z_E\). Test joint significance: $$H_0: \theta = 0$$

COURSE RULES OF THUMB

First-Stage F-Statistic Thresholds

  • Multiple Instruments (\(L > 1\)): The first-stage \(F\)-statistic satisfies \(F \ge 10\).
  • Single Instrument (\(L = 1\)): The first-stage \(F\)-statistic satisfies \(F \ge 16\), which implies \(|t| \ge 4\) (since \(F = t^2\)).

These thresholds are empirical rules of thumb from this module, not universal mathematical laws. Instrument relevance and validity are distinct: a strong first stage does not verify exogeneity.

Weak-Identification Diagnostic Tools

CRITICAL VALUES

Stock–Yogo

Critical values used to assess weak-instrument concerns based on maximum allowable relative bias or size distortion.

USE WHEN: Evaluating relative bias or test size under homoskedasticity.
HOMOSKEDASTIC TEST

Cragg–Donald

A weak-identification diagnostic discussed for the classical setting with multiple endogenous regressors.

USE WHEN: Testing matrix rank with multiple regressors under homoskedasticity.
ROBUST INFERENCE

Kleibergen–Paap

A robust weak-identification diagnostic mentioned for settings with heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation, or clustering.

USE WHEN: Assessing instrument rank under non-spherical or clustered errors.

What If Instruments Are Weak?

OPTION 1

Reconsider OLS

Use only in the stated context when IV finite-sample bias and variance exceed OLS bias.

OPTION 2

Find Stronger Instruments

Seek instruments with stronger first-stage relevance through sharper institutional or policy variation.

OPTION 3

Use Robust Inference

Apply weak-IV-robust confidence intervals such as the Anderson–Rubin or Moreira conditional likelihood ratio tests.

OPTION 4

Reduce Weak Instruments

Avoid relying on many weak predictors when stronger individual instruments are available.

WHY IT MATTERS

Reporting an IV estimate without reporting first-stage diagnostics leaves the estimator's precision and finite-sample stability unknown.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

The first-stage \(F\)-statistic measures independent variation injected by the instruments. When \(F\) is low, finite-sample bias can dominate asymptotic consistency.

4. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Does obtaining a very high first-stage \(F\)-statistic (e.g., \(F = 85\)) prove that an instrumental variable is valid?

HINT: Distinguish carefully between the two fundamental IV criteria: instrument relevance and instrument validity (exclusion).

ANSWER: No, a strong first stage does not prove that an instrument is valid.

WHY? A high first-stage \(F\)-statistic evaluates only instrument relevance: \(\text{Cov}(Z, X) \neq 0\). It provides zero empirical proof of instrument validity: \(\text{Cov}(Z, \varepsilon) = 0\). An invalid instrument (such as an omitted confounder) can have strong explanatory power for \(X\) while violating the exclusion restriction.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Valid instruments must also be sufficiently relevant.
  • Weak instruments can produce large variance and serious finite-sample problems.
  • First-stage diagnostics assess relevance, not instrument validity.
  • The module presents \(F\)-statistic rules of thumb as diagnostics rather than universal laws.
  • Stronger instruments generally provide more informative IV estimation.
