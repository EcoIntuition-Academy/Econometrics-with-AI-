LESSON 12.1 Exogeneity Testing

Do We Need IV? Hausman & Wu Tests for Endogeneity

Before paying the precision price of instrumental variables, economists test whether ordinary least squares is actually inconsistent. Learn how the Hausman contrast and Wu residual regression detect endogeneity.

MODULE 11 REMINDER

The Starting Question: Do We Actually Need IV?

In Module 11, we saw that instrumental variables cure endogeneity bias. But IV estimators have substantially higher variance than OLS. If explanatory variables are actually exogenous, OLS is consistent and more efficient. Therefore, the first operational question in empirical research is: Do we need IV at all?

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • State the exogeneity hypotheses and explain why OLS is preferred under \(H_0\) due to efficiency.
  • Understand the Hausman test logic under the maintained assumption that the IV estimator is consistent.
  • Compute the Hausman test statistic \(W\) and interpret its degrees of freedom \(K^*\).
  • Distinguish the IV-vs-OLS Hausman test from the Module 10 Fixed Effects vs. Random Effects Hausman test.
  • Explain why imprecise IV estimates lead to low statistical power against endogeneity.
  • Implement the Wu auxiliary regression test using first-stage residuals \(\hat{u}\).

1. Hausman Test for Exogeneity

DEFINITION

Hausman Test for Exogeneity

The Hausman test checks whether regressors are exogenous by evaluating whether the difference between the OLS estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}\) and the IV estimator \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}}\) is statistically distinguishable from zero, under the maintained hypothesis that the IV estimator is consistent.

NULL HYPOTHESIS

\(H_0: \mathbb{E}[x_i \varepsilon_i] = 0\)

Interpretation: \(X\) is exogenous.

Under \(H_0\), \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} \approx 0\). Both OLS and IV are consistent.

Decision: Prefer OLS (more efficient; lower variance)
ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESIS

\(H_1: \mathbb{E}[x_i \varepsilon_i] \neq 0\)

Interpretation: \(X\) is endogenous.

Under \(H_1\), \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} \neq 0\). OLS is biased and inconsistent, while IV remains consistent.

Decision: Prefer IV (consistent despite higher variance)
HAUSMAN TEST STATISTIC
$$\begin{align*} W &= (\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}})' \left[ \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}}) - \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}) \right]^{-1} (\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}) \end{align*}$$
Reference Distribution: \(W \sim \chi^2_{K^*}\) Where: \(K^* =\) number of endogenous regressors tested

FE vs. RE

Question: Is individual effect \(c_i\) uncorrelated with regressors? Compares \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{FE}}\) (consistent) against \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{RE}}\) (efficient under \(H_0\)).

MODULE 12 HAUSMAN

IV vs. OLS

Question: Is \(X\) exogenous, or is IV needed? Compares \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}}\) (maintained consistent) against \(\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}\) (efficient under \(H_0\)).

POWER CAUTION

Imprecise IV and Low Test Power

IV estimators are typically much less precise than OLS. When \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}})\) is large, the denominator \([\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}}) - \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}})]\) is large, which can give the Hausman test low statistical power. Failing to reject exogeneity does not prove that endogeneity is zero; it may simply reflect noisy IV estimates.

2. The Wu Test

DEFINITION

Wu Test

The Wu test asks: Does the portion of \(X\) not explained by the instruments still help explain \(y\)? If the first-stage residuals add no explanatory power to the structural equation, evidence is consistent with exogenous \(X\).

ALGEBRAIC DERIVATION

Why IV Minus OLS Depends on Residuals

Goal

Understand why the difference between IV and OLS is related to the correlation between instruments and OLS residuals.

1

Recall Estimator Formulas

$$\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} = (X'X)^{-1}X'y, \quad \hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} = (Z'X)^{-1}Z'y$$
Why? We want to compare the estimators directly.
2

Decompose \(y\) Using OLS Fit

$$y = X\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} + \hat{\varepsilon}_{\text{OLS}}$$
Why? Decomposing \(y\) isolates the OLS fitted part from the residual variation.
3

Substitute into IV Estimator

$$\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} = (Z'X)^{-1}Z'(X\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} + \hat{\varepsilon}_{\text{OLS}})$$
Why? Distributes the instrument matrix \(Z'\) across both components.
4

Simplify

$$\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} = \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} + (Z'X)^{-1}Z'\hat{\varepsilon}_{\text{OLS}}$$
Key Result $$\hat{\beta}_{\text{IV}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} = (Z'X)^{-1}Z'\hat{\varepsilon}_{\text{OLS}}$$
BIG PICTURE

If the instruments \(Z\) are orthogonal to the OLS residual component (\(Z'\hat{\varepsilon}_{\text{OLS}} \approx 0\)), the two estimators are close under the maintained assumptions.

General Wu Test Procedure

In structural model \(y = X\beta + Z_C \Gamma + \varepsilon\) with included controls \(Z_C\) and excluded instruments \(Z_E\):

STEP 1 · FIRST STAGE

Regress each suspected \(X\) on controls \(Z_C\) and excluded IVs \(Z_E\):

\(x = Z_C \theta_1 + Z_E \theta_2 + u\)

Obtain residuals \(\hat{u}\).

STEP 2 · AUGMENTED REGRESSION

Add \(\hat{u}\) back into the structural model:

\(y = X\beta + Z_C \Gamma + \hat{u}\pi + \varepsilon^*\)

Estimate by OLS.

STEP 3 · HYPOTHESIS TEST

Test \(H_0: \pi = 0\):

• \(\pi = 0\): residual adds no explanatory power \(\to\) consistent with exogeneity.
• \(\pi \neq 0\): leftover variation in \(X\) explains \(y\) \(\to\) endogeneity.

ROBUSTNESS NOTE

Heteroskedasticity Warning

Classical versions of these tests assume homoskedasticity. Use an appropriate heteroskedasticity-robust version when required.

4. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Why might OLS be preferred when the exogeneity null hypothesis is not rejected?

HINT: Consider the trade-off between consistency and variance (efficiency) under exogeneity.

ANSWER: Under the exogeneity null hypothesis, both OLS and IV are consistent, but OLS is more efficient.

WHY? When regressors are exogenous, OLS achieves the minimum variance among linear unbiased estimators. The IV estimator only utilizes the fraction of variation in \(X\) explained by \(Z\), producing substantially higher variance without providing any bias reduction.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The IV-vs-OLS Hausman test asks whether \(X\) appears exogenous under a maintained valid-IV assumption.
  • Under exogeneity, OLS and IV should estimate the same parameter asymptotically.
  • A large estimator difference provides evidence against OLS exogeneity.
  • The test can have low power when IV estimates are imprecise.
  • The Wu test offers an alternative residual-augmentation approach to testing endogeneity.
