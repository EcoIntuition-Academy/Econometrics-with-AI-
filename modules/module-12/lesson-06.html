LESSON 12.6 Synthesis & Review

Propagation of Error & Module Review

Constructed variables inherit uncertainty from the measurements used to create them, and some transformations can make that uncertainty more consequential. Here we review error propagation and synthesize the complete Module 12 econometric toolkit.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Understand how measurement error propagates through mathematical transformations into constructed variables.
  • Analyze three canonical transformations: ratio construction (wages), nonlinear scaling (log income), and differencing (test score gains).
  • Trace the sequential diagnostic questions in the IV decision architecture.
  • Synthesize the complete econometric toolkit of Module 12 using structured review cards and diagnostic flow maps.

RATIO CONSTRUCTION

Wage = Income / Hours

\[\text{Wage} = \frac{\text{Income}^* + u_{\text{inc}}}{\text{Hours}^* + u_{\text{hrs}}}\]

Both numerator and denominator contain reporting noise. Mismeasurement in hours worked enters the denominator, destabilizing the computed wage quotient.

LOGARITHMIC SCALING

Log Income = \(\ln(y^* + v)\)

\[\ln(y) \approx \ln y^* + \frac{v}{y^*}\]

The effective measurement error becomes relative (\(v/y^*\)). For observations with low true income \(y^*\), modest absolute errors create large proportional distortions.

PANEL DIFFERENCING

Difference in test scores = \(y_2 - y_1\)

\[\text{Var}(\Delta v) = \sigma_{v_2}^2 + \sigma_{v_1}^2 = 2\sigma_v^2\]

Differencing eliminates time-invariant fixed effects \(\alpha_i\), but doubles measurement-error variance while true signal variation shrinks, worsening attenuation bias.

Section 3

3. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Why can taking differences of a variable across time (e.g., test score gains \(\Delta y = y_2 - y_1\)) worsen attenuation bias, even though it successfully eliminates unobserved individual fixed effects?

HINT: Examine what happens to the variance of the differenced noise \(\text{Var}(v_2 - v_1)\) relative to the true differenced signal \(\text{Var}(y_2^* - y_1^*)\) when variables are highly persistent.

ANSWER: Differencing doubles measurement error variance while reducing true signal variance, causing the reliability ratio \(\lambda\) to collapse.

WHY? When variables are differenced across time, the error variance doubles: \(\text{Var}(v_2 - v_1) = \sigma_{v_1}^2 + \sigma_{v_2}^2 = 2\sigma_v^2\). Meanwhile, because persistent economic series have small differenced variation \(\text{Var}(\Delta y^*)\), the signal-to-noise ratio \(\lambda = \text{Var}(\Delta y^*)/[\text{Var}(\Delta y^*) + 2\sigma_v^2]\) plunges, creating much more severe attenuation bias than in levels regression.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Hausman and Wu tests provide evidence about whether regressors appear endogenous under the maintained assumptions.
  • Overidentification tests such as Sargan assess whether an overidentified instrument set is jointly compatible with the model; rejection indicates tension with the maintained assumptions rather than identifying a specific failing instrument.
  • Instrument strength matters because weak instruments can make IV estimates highly imprecise and unreliable in finite samples; first-stage diagnostics help assess instrument relevance, while instrument strength and validity remain distinct concepts.
  • Classical measurement error in the outcome mainly reduces precision, while classical measurement error in a regressor creates endogeneity and attenuation bias toward zero.
  • A valid and relevant instrument can restore consistency in the classical measurement-error setting under the maintained assumptions; meanwhile, derived variables inherit measurement error from their inputs, and some transformations can make that uncertainty more consequential.
