LESSON 11.1 Endogeneity & OLS Failure

Endogeneity & Why OLS Can Fail

Examine the fundamental OLS exogeneity requirement, understand why overlapping variation between regressors and regression disturbances corrupts causal identification, and explore Ballantine diagrams.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Formulate the linear regression framework \(y = X\beta + \varepsilon\) and the core exogeneity condition \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] = 0\).
  • Interpret variance partitioning using the Ballantine diagram (explained variation vs. unexplained error).
  • Identify what happens when the disturbance \(\varepsilon\) overlaps with regressor \(X\), violating \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] = 0\).
  • Understand why endogeneity produces both finite-sample bias and large-sample inconsistency in OLS.
  • Connect the formal definition of endogeneity to empirical mechanisms: omitted variables, reverse causality, and simultaneity.
01
Theoretical Foundation

The OLS Exogeneity Requirement

Recall the standard linear econometric specification relating outcome variable \(y\) to explanatory regressor \(X\):

\[y = X\beta + \varepsilon\]

Here, \(\beta\) represents the structural causal parameter of interest, and \(\varepsilon\) represents the unobserved disturbance term containing all idiosyncratic, unmeasured factors that influence \(y\).

THE OLS EXOGENEITY REQUIREMENT

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) produces an unbiased and consistent estimator of \(\beta\) if and only if the explanatory variables do not correlate with the regression error term:

\[\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] = 0\]

When this moment condition holds, regressor \(X\) is said to be exogenous. In geometric and variance-decomposition terms, all variation in \(y\) can be cleanly partitioned into two orthogonal components:

  • Explained variation: The portion of variance in \(y\) that is systematically associated with changes in \(X\).
  • Unexplained variation (Error \(\varepsilon\)): The residual variance in \(y\) that cannot be accounted for by \(X\).

Intuition: The Clean Division of Variance

If the disturbance \(\varepsilon\) lies entirely within the unexplained region and shares no mutual variation with \(X\), OLS compares outcomes across units with different levels of \(X\) without confusing the effect of \(X\) with unobserved background shocks.

02
Variance Visualization

The Ballantine Diagram & Endogeneity

Econometricians traditionally represent regression variance partitioning using a Ballantine Diagram (an overlapping area diagram showing shared variance between variables).

STATE 1: EXOGENOUS X \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] = 0\)

The variance circles for \(X\) and \(y\) overlap in blue (explained variation). The error \(\varepsilon\) lies entirely in the unexplained region (yellow). There is zero overlap between \(X\) and \(\varepsilon\).

✓ OLS is unbiased: \(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}] = \beta\)
✓ OLS is consistent: \(\text{plim } \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} = \beta\)
STATE 2: ENDOGENOUS X \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] \ne 0\)

The error term \(\varepsilon\) shares an overlapping red region with \(X\). The regressor carries variation that also belongs to the disturbance term.

✗ OLS is biased: \(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}}] \ne \beta\)
✗ OLS is inconsistent: \(\text{plim } \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} \ne \beta\)

CORE CONSEQUENCE: ENDOGENEITY VIOLATION

If regressor \(X\) overlaps with disturbance \(\varepsilon\), then \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] \ne 0\) and \(X\) is endogenous.

Under this violation, OLS cannot determine whether an observed change in \(y\) was caused by \(X\) or by the unobserved disturbance \(\varepsilon\) that moves alongside \(X\). Consequently, OLS estimates are corrupted by bias and inconsistency.

04
Empirical Mechanisms

Three Primary Sources of Endogeneity

In applied economic research, why does \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] \ne 0\) occur? The primary lecture notes identify three classic structural mechanisms that introduce an endogenous overlap:

1

Omitted / Unobserved Confounders

When an unmeasured factor determines both the regressor \(X\) and the outcome \(y\), that factor gets absorbed into the disturbance \(\varepsilon\). A classic example is unobserved ability in wage regressions: individuals with higher innate ability may obtain more schooling (\(X\)) and also earn higher wages (\(y\)), causing schooling to correlate positively with \(\varepsilon\).

2

Reverse Causality

While researchers aim to estimate the effect of \(X\) on \(y\), changes in \(y\) simultaneously induce feedback changes in \(X\). In crime economics, higher incarceration rates may deter or incapacitate criminal activity, but areas experiencing high crime surges also respond by sentencing and incarcerating more offenders.

3

Simultaneity in Equilibrium Systems

In market equilibrium, observed outcomes are jointly determined by intersecting schedules. In supply and demand systems, market-clearing prices and quantities are simultaneous solutions. Regressing quantity on price via naive OLS fails because price is intrinsically correlated with the demand shock \(\varepsilon\).

💡

QUICK CHECK

What condition fails when an explanatory variable \(X\) overlaps with the regression error term \(\varepsilon\)?

HINT: Consider the expected product or covariance between the regressor and the disturbance term in OLS theory.

ANSWER: The exogeneity requirement fails: \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] \ne 0\).

WHY? When \(X\) and \(\varepsilon\) share common variation, the regressor is correlated with unobserved determinants of \(y\). OLS cannot separate the true structural effect of \(X\) from the effect of the disturbance, causing the estimator to be both biased in finite samples and inconsistent asymptotically.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • The standard linear regression model \(y = X\beta + \varepsilon\) requires the exogeneity condition \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] = 0\) for OLS to be unbiased and consistent.
  • Ballantine diagrams visualize variance partitioning: blue represents explained variance, yellow represents unexplained disturbance variance.
  • When regressor \(X\) and disturbance \(\varepsilon\) overlap, \(\mathbb{E}[X\varepsilon] \ne 0\) and \(X\) is classified as endogenous.
  • Under endogeneity, the OLS estimator attributes the red overlapping variance to \(\beta\), inducing persistent asymptotic bias.
  • Endogeneity arises naturally in empirical economics from omitted confounders, reverse causality, and simultaneous equilibrium determination.
