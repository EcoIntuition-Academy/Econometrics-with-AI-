LESSON 11.7 2SLS & Projection

2SLS, Projection Matrices & Overidentification

Formulate Two-Stage Least Squares when instruments exceed regressors, derive the projection matrix onto the instrument subspace, compare just-identified and overidentified systems, and review Module 11.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Understand why the simple IV matrix formula fails when instruments exceed endogenous regressors (\(L > K\)).
  • Define the projection matrix \(\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{Z})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\) and interpret it geometrically.
  • Derive the First Stage in matrix form: \(\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X}\).
  • Derive the Second Stage 2SLS estimator: \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{y}\).
  • Compare just-identified (\(L = K\)) and overidentified (\(L > K\)) systems, recognizing when 2SLS reduces to simple IV.
  • Synthesize the complete Module 11 curriculum using the final comparison table and comprehensive review cards.
01
Overidentified Setup

The Overidentified Case: \(L > K\)

Let outcome \(\mathbf{y}\) be \(n \times 1\), endogenous regressors \(\mathbf{X}\) be \(n \times K\), and instruments \(\mathbf{Z}\) be \(n \times L\), where:

\[L > K\]

WHY THE SIMPLE IV FORMULA NO LONGER APPLIES

In the exactly identified case, the IV estimator was \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{IV}} = (\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{y}\). But when \(L > K\), the cross-product matrix \(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X}\) has dimension \(L \times K\). It is a rectangular matrix and cannot be inverted.

There are more moment equations than unknown parameters. To use all the available information, we must project \(\mathbf{X}\) into the column space of \(\mathbf{Z}\).

02
Geometric Projection

The Projection Matrix \(\mathbf{P_Z}\)

The projection matrix onto the column space of instruments \(\mathbf{Z}\) is defined as:

\[\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{Z})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\]

\(\mathbf{P_Z}\) is an \(n \times n\) symmetric and idempotent matrix possessing two fundamental properties:

Symmetric:
\[\mathbf{P_Z}' = \mathbf{P_Z}\]

Transposing the projection matrix yields the same matrix.

Idempotent:
\[\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{P_Z}\]

Projecting a second time onto the same space leaves vectors unchanged.

Projection Intuition: Fitted Values

Multiplying any vector or matrix by \(\mathbf{P_Z}\) produces the fitted values from an OLS regression on \(\mathbf{Z}\). Therefore, \(\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X}\) represents the fitted values from regressing the endogenous regressors \(\mathbf{X}\) onto the full set of instruments \(\mathbf{Z}\).

03
Derivation Journey

Step-by-Step Derivation of 2SLS

DERIVATION GOAL

Show How 2SLS Uses the Projection of \(\mathbf{X}\) onto \(\mathbf{Z}\)

STEP 1: FIRST STAGE (PROJECT X ONTO Z)

Project the endogenous regressors \(\mathbf{X}\) onto the column space of instruments \(\mathbf{Z}\):

\[\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{Z})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X}\]
STEP 2: SECOND STAGE (RUN OLS OF y ON X̂)

Run Ordinary Least Squares of \(\mathbf{y}\) on the fitted regressors \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}\):

\[\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} = (\hat{\mathbf{X}}'\hat{\mathbf{X}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{X}}'\mathbf{y}\]
STEP 3: SUBSTITUTE PROJECTION DEFINITION

Substitute \(\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X}\) into the second-stage estimator:

\[\begin{aligned} \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} &= [(\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})'(\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})]^{-1} (\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})'\mathbf{y} \\ &= [\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X}]^{-1} \mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}'\mathbf{y} \end{aligned}\]
STEP 4: APPLY SYMMETRY & IDEMPOTENCY

Because \(\mathbf{P_Z}' = \mathbf{P_Z}\) and \(\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{P_Z}\), we have \(\mathbf{P_Z}'\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{P_Z}\). This yields the famous closed-form 2SLS matrix formula:

\[\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{y}\]

Intuition: You can think of this as “instrumenting” \(\mathbf{X}\) with its orthogonal projection onto \(\mathbf{Z}\), then applying OLS.

04
Equivalence Analysis

Just-Identified vs. Overidentified Systems

The lecture notes provide a critical conceptual link between simple IV and 2SLS:

JUST IDENTIFIED (\(L = K\))

2SLS = IV

When \(L = K\), \(\mathbf{Z}\) and \(\mathbf{X}\) are both \(n \times K\). The cross-product \(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X}\) is square and invertible.

\[\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{IV}} = (\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{y}\]

In this case, \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}\) spans the exact same column space as \(\mathbf{Z}\), and 2SLS simplifies algebraically to simple IV.

OVERIDENTIFIED (\(L > K\))

2SLS Required

When \(L > K\), there are more instruments than endogenous regressors. \(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X}\) is not square and cannot be inverted.

\[\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{y}\]

2SLS optimally combines the \(L\) available moment conditions into a \(K\)-dimensional weighted linear combination.

Efficiency vs. Validity Note from Lecture:

The lecture notes note that overidentification (having more instruments than regressors) may improve efficiency by utilizing additional variation, but each additional instrument also raises instrument-validity concerns that must be justified economically.

💡

QUICK CHECK

What is the algebraic expression for the projection matrix \(\mathbf{P_Z}\), and what does \(\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X}\) represent geometrically?

HINT: Consider the standard OLS “hat matrix” when regressing onto the columns of \(\mathbf{Z}\).

ANSWER: \(\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{Z})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\), and \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}\) represents the fitted values from regressing \(\mathbf{X}\) onto \(\mathbf{Z}\).

WHY? \(\mathbf{P_Z}\) is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the subspace spanned by the columns of \(\mathbf{Z}\). Multiplying \(\mathbf{X}\) by \(\mathbf{P_Z}\) projects each endogenous regressor onto this instrument subspace, extracting only the exogenous variation \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}\) to be used in the second-stage causal regression.

📋

What You Should Now Understand

  • When instruments exceed endogenous regressors (\(L > K\)), \(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{X}\) is not square; 2SLS resolves this by projecting onto \(\mathbf{Z}\).
  • The projection matrix \(\mathbf{P_Z} = \mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{Z})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'\) projects variables into the instrument column space.
  • The First Stage produces \(\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X}\), and the Second Stage yields \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{2SLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{P_Z}\mathbf{y}\).
  • When \(L = K\), \(\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{Z}\) algebraically and 2SLS reduces to the classical just-identified IV estimator.
  • Overidentification can improve asymptotic efficiency but requires careful economic justification of instrument validity.
  • Proper econometric software must be used to ensure standard errors account for generated regressor uncertainty.
