LESSON 8.5 Weighted Estimation

Weighted Least Squares

When the mathematical structure of heteroskedasticity is known up to a scalar multiple, we do not need to rely solely on asymptotic standard-error adjustments. Weighted Least Squares (WLS) re-weights every data point inversely proportional to its error variance, restoring BLUE efficiency in finite samples.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Conceptualize Weighted Least Squares (WLS) as a diagonal special case of Generalized Least Squares: \(\text{WLS} \subset \text{GLS}\).
  • Master the precision-weighting principle: observations with higher variance receive lower relative weight.
  • Analyze the canonical classroom-average height model where \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \sigma^2 / \text{girls}_i\).
  • Prove how multiplying observations by \(\sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\) produces homoskedastic disturbances.
  • Formulate the WLS estimator in both matrix form \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{W}\mathbf{y}\) and summation notation.

1. The Weighted Least Squares Framework

Weighted Least Squares is a special case of Generalized Least Squares that arises when heteroskedasticity is present but disturbances remain uncorrelated across observations. In this setting, the covariance matrix is purely diagonal:

KNOWN HETEROSKEDASTIC VARIANCE STRUCTURE
$$\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) = \sigma_i^2 = \sigma^2 \omega_i$$

where \(\omega_i > 0\) is a known observation-specific proportionality factor and \(\sigma^2\) is an unknown scalar parameter.

PRECISION WEIGHTS

Inverse-Variance Definition

$$w_i \equiv \frac{1}{\omega_i} = \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma_i^2}$$

Observations with smaller error variance (greater precision) receive proportionally higher weight in the objective function.

WEIGHT MATRIX

Diagonal Formulation

$$\mathbf{W} \equiv \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n)$$

Because \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) has zero off-diagonals, inverting \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) simply requires taking the reciprocal of each diagonal element.

2. Primary Source Example: Classroom-Average Height

To make the WLS transformation intuitive, the lecture notes introduce the classic grouped-data model:

THE CLASSROOM GROUPING MODEL

Suppose \(y_i\) represents the average height of girls in classroom \(i\), and \(\text{girls}_i\) is the number of girls in that classroom. If individual girl heights have independent variance \(\sigma^2\), the variance of the classroom sample mean is:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \frac{\sigma^2}{\text{girls}_i}$$

A classroom with \(40\) girls provides a far more stable, precise estimate of average height than a small classroom with only \(5\) girls. Consequently, the precision weight for classroom \(i\) is directly proportional to its class size: $$\text{weight}_i = \text{girls}_i$$

Matrix Inversion in the Classroom Model

Constructing \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) and its inverse illustrates how class sizes become weights:

$$\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\Omega} &= \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{\text{girls}_1} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{\text{girls}_2} & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \frac{1}{\text{girls}_n} \end{bmatrix} \\[10pt] \implies \mathbf{W} \equiv \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1} &= \begin{bmatrix} \text{girls}_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \text{girls}_2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \text{girls}_n \end{bmatrix} \end{aligned}$$
HOMOSKEDASTIC TRANSFORMATION

Multiplying by \(\sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\)

Since \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2} = \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\text{girls}_1}, \dots, \sqrt{\text{girls}_n})\), we multiply each observation's regressors and dependent variable by \(\sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\):

$$\begin{aligned} y_i^* &= \sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\, y_i, \quad \mathbf{x}_i^* = \sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\, \mathbf{x}_i \\ \varepsilon_i^* &= \sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\, \varepsilon_i \end{aligned}$$

Evaluating the transformed disturbance variance confirms that homoskedasticity is restored: $$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i^*) &= \operatorname{Var}\big(\sqrt{\text{girls}_i}\,\varepsilon_i\big) \\ &= \text{girls}_i \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \text{girls}_i \left(\frac{\sigma^2}{\text{girls}_i}\right) = \sigma^2 \end{aligned}$$

3. Mathematical Forms of the WLS Estimator

The Weighted Least Squares estimator can be expressed in two complementary mathematical representations:

MATRIX NOTATION

Compact Formulation

$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{WLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{W}\mathbf{y}$$

Where \(\mathbf{W} = \operatorname{diag}(w_1, \dots, w_n)\). This highlights WLS as an exact instance of \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{y}\).

SUMMATION NOTATION

Observation-Level Formulation

$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{WLS}} = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i' \right]^{-1} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \mathbf{x}_i y_i \right]$$

Each observation's cross-products \(\mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i'\) and \(\mathbf{x}_i y_i\) are scaled directly by their individual precision weight \(w_i\).

GENERAL HETEROSKEDASTIC WEIGHT FUNCTION

If error variance is known to be proportional to a function of predictors \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) = \sigma^2 f(\mathbf{x}_i)\), the weights are simply:

$$\text{weight}_i = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(\mathbf{x}_i)}}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{f(\mathbf{x}_i)}$$

Dividing the regression equation by \(\sqrt{f(\mathbf{x}_i)}\) produces disturbances with constant variance \(\sigma^2\).

5. Quick Check & Understanding Test

CHECK 8.5 In the classroom height example, which classroom receives greater weight: 5 girls or 40 girls? Click to Reveal Solution

Answer: The classroom with 40 girls receives 8 times as much weight as the classroom with 5 girls.

Why? The variance of the classroom average is \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \sigma^2 / \text{girls}_i\). Therefore: $$\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_{\text{class 40}}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{40}, \quad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_{\text{class 5}}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{5}$$

Because precision weight is defined as \(w_i \propto 1 / \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i)\), we have: $$\begin{aligned} w_{40} &= 40, \quad w_5 = 5 \\ \implies \frac{w_{40}}{w_5} &= \frac{40}{5} = 8 \end{aligned}$$ A 40-girl classroom produces a much more precise group average, so WLS weights it 8 times more heavily than a 5-girl classroom.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Weighted Least Squares is a special case of Generalized Least Squares where the covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is purely diagonal.
  • WLS assigns weights inversely proportional to variance (\(w_i \propto 1/\sigma_i^2\)), prioritizing high-precision observations.
  • In grouped data where \(y_i\) is an average of \(n_i\) units, error variance is \(\sigma^2 / n_i\), meaning weights are directly proportional to group size \(n_i\).
  • Multiplying regressors and dependent variables by \(\sqrt{w_i}\) transforms the system into a homoskedastic regression that satisfies Gauss–Markov.
  • The WLS estimator is \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{WLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{W}\mathbf{y} = [\sum w_i \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i']^{-1} [\sum w_i \mathbf{x}_i y_i]\).
