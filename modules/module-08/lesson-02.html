LESSON 8.2 Efficient Estimation

Generalized Least Squares & Feasible GLS

When the disturbance covariance structure is non-spherical, OLS is no longer the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE). We investigate how Generalized Least Squares (GLS) transforms the empirical coordinates to restore spherical errors, and how Feasible GLS (FGLS) operationalizes this transformation when the covariance matrix must be estimated from data.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Formulate the Generalized Least Squares model under known positive-definite covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\).
  • Derive the canonical \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\) coordinate transformation that produces spherical disturbances.
  • Derive the GLS estimator \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}}\) and its variance-covariance matrix \(\sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).
  • Trace the three-step empirical workflow of Feasible Generalized Least Squares (FGLS).
  • Contrast the theoretical requirements and practical trade-offs between GLS and FGLS.

1. The GLS Model Setup & Coordinate Transformation

Suppose the true data generating process is linear, but disturbances satisfy the non-spherical condition where \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is a known, symmetric, positive-definite matrix:

GENERALIZED MODEL SPECIFICATION
$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{y} &= \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}, \quad \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}] &= \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega} \end{aligned}$$

Because \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is positive definite, its inverse square root \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\) exists, is symmetric, and satisfies \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2} = \mathbf{I}\).

DERIVATION JOURNEY · THE GLS TRANSFORMATION
Goal: Transform the regression system so that the transformed disturbance vector has a spherical covariance matrix \(\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\).
Step 1 · Premultiply by \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\):
$$\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{y} = \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$$
Step 2 · Define Transformed Variables:
$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{y}^* &\equiv \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{y}, \quad \mathbf{X}^* \equiv \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X} \\ \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^* &\equiv \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \end{aligned}$$
Step 3 · State the Transformed Regression Model:
$$\mathbf{y}^* = \mathbf{X}^*\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^*$$
Step 4 · Prove Transformed Disturbances Are Spherical:
$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^*(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^*)' \mid \mathbf{X}] &= \mathbb{E}\big[(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})' \mid \mathbf{X}\big] \\[4pt] &= \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\,\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}]\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2} \\[4pt] &= \boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\big(\sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega}\big)\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2} \\[4pt] &= \sigma^2 \big(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\big) = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_n \end{aligned}$$
THE GLS MECHANISM AT A GLANCE
Non-Spherical Error Space: \(\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega}\)
↓ transform by \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\)
Spherical Transformed Error Space: \(\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^* \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\)
↓ apply ordinary least squares
Efficient Estimator: \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{y}\)

2. The Generalized Least Squares Estimator

Applying OLS directly to the transformed variables yields the generalized estimator:

THE GLS ESTIMATOR
$$\begin{aligned} \widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} &= (\mathbf{X}^{*\prime}\mathbf{X}^*)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{*\prime}\mathbf{y}^* \\[6pt] &= \big[(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X})'(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X})\big]^{-1} (\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X})'(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{y}) \\[6pt] &= (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\mathbf{y} \\[6pt] &= (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{y} \end{aligned}$$

Finite-Sample Properties of GLS

Because \(\mathbf{y}^* = \mathbf{X}^*\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^*\) satisfies all classical Gauss–Markov assumptions, \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}}\) inherits BLUE optimality:

UNBIASEDNESS
$$\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \boldsymbol{\beta}$$

Linear transformation preserves mean exogeneity without inducing any finite-sample distortion.

EXACT COVARIANCE
$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}$$

By the Gauss–Markov Theorem on transformed data, this variance is strictly smaller than (or equal to) \(\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}})\).

3. Feasible Generalized Least Squares (FGLS)

The theoretical beauty of GLS faces a practical obstacle: in real empirical work, \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is virtually never known. Feasible GLS solves this by estimating \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) from data residuals before executing the GLS formula.

STAGE 1

Preliminary OLS

Run ordinary least squares \(\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) and retrieve the sample residuals \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = \mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\).

STAGE 2

Covariance Modeling

Use the squared residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) (or lagged cross-products) to estimate an empirical covariance matrix \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\).

STAGE 3

Plug-In Estimation

Substitute \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\) directly into the GLS formula: $$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{FGLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}^{-1}\mathbf{y}$$

THEORETICAL BENCHMARK

Generalized Least Squares (GLS)

  • Assumes covariance structure \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is known a priori.
  • Strictly linear estimator in \(\mathbf{y}\).
  • Guaranteed strictly unbiased in finite samples: \(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}}] = \boldsymbol{\beta}\).
PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION

Feasible GLS (FGLS)

  • Replaces \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) with estimated matrix \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\).
  • Nonlinear in \(\mathbf{y}\) because \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\) depends on OLS residuals.
  • Asymptotically efficient, though finite-sample properties depend on \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\) accuracy.

5. Quick Check & Conceptual Reflection

CHECK 8.2 Why is FGLS called "Feasible" Generalized Least Squares? Click to Reveal Solution

Answer: Because it replaces the unknown theoretical covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) with a sample estimate \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\) computed from preliminary OLS residuals.

Why? Pure GLS is an ideal mathematical benchmark requiring the analyst to know the exact positive-definite matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\). In real observational settings, \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) contains \(n(n+1)/2\) distinct variance and covariance elements which cannot be known a priori.

By first obtaining consistent residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i\) from preliminary OLS, specifying an econometric structure for the variances (e.g., \(\sigma_i^2 = \exp(\mathbf{z}_i'\boldsymbol{\gamma})\)), and plugging \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\) into the GLS equation, the procedure becomes computationally and empirically feasible.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • GLS transforms a non-spherical regression model by premultiplying by \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1/2}\), restoring spherical disturbance geometry.
  • The GLS estimator is \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{GLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{y}\), with variance \(\sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).
  • Under known \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\), GLS satisfies Gauss–Markov conditions and attains minimum variance among all linear unbiased estimators.
  • In practical research where \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) is unknown, Feasible GLS (FGLS) estimates \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\) from preliminary OLS residuals.
  • FGLS achieves asymptotic efficiency while adapting GLS to real-world datasets with heteroskedastic or autocorrelated disturbances.
