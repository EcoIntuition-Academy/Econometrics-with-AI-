LESSON 8.3 Robust Inference

White’s Heteroskedasticity-Robust Standard Errors

Rather than attempting to model or transform the unknown variance structure, Halbert White’s celebrated insight allows researchers to retain ordinary least squares coefficients and construct a consistent estimator of their sampling variance directly from sample residuals.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Clarify the core strategy of robust inference: keep OLS coefficients \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\), correct the covariance matrix.
  • Derive White’s heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC0) covariance estimator in both summation and matrix forms.
  • Interpret the academic "sandwich" structure: bread \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) enclosing the empirical meat \(\mathbf{X}'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\mathbf{X}\).
  • Recognize that robust standard errors do NOT alter point estimates or eliminate heteroskedasticity.
  • Analyze how increasing heteroskedasticity widens the gap between conventional and robust standard errors.

1. Strategy & Setup: Keeping OLS Coefficients

In Lesson 8.1, we proved that under the standard exogeneity condition \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\), ordinary least squares estimates are strictly unbiased:

$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{y}$$

We do not need to discard \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\). The challenge is that its conditional variance under heteroskedastic disturbances \(\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \boldsymbol{\Omega} = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_n^2)\) takes the general form:

TRUE CONDITIONAL VARIANCE OF OLS
$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}) = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{X} (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}$$

Notation Note: In this specification, \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \equiv \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_n^2)\) absorbs the individual variances directly. Conventional software estimates \(\sigma^2(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\), assuming \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\). When \(\sigma_i^2\) varies, conventional standard errors fail completely.

OLS POINT ESTIMATES

Strictly Unchanged (\(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\))

White's correction does not alter the estimated slope or intercept coefficients. The fitted line is identical.

STANDARD ERRORS & INFERENCE

Consistent Covariance (\(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}\))

Standard errors are recalculated to account for unequal error variance, restoring valid \(t\)-statistics, \(p\)-values, and confidence intervals.

2. White’s Heteroskedasticity-Consistent (HC0) Estimator

White (1980) proved that although we cannot consistently estimate each individual variance \(\sigma_i^2\) from a single observation, we can consistently estimate the inner matrix product \(\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{X}\) by replacing \(\sigma_i^2\) with squared OLS residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\):

WHITE (HC0) COVARIANCE ESTIMATOR
$$\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}) = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i' \right] (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}$$
In compact matrix notation:
$$\begin{aligned} \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}) &= (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\mathbf{X} (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \\ \text{where } \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}} &= \operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\varepsilon}_1^2, \dots, \widehat{\varepsilon}_n^2) \end{aligned}$$
THE "SANDWICH" COVARIANCE ANATOMY
BREAD (Left Inverse Hessian): \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\)
×
MEAT (Empirical Outer Product): \(\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i' = \mathbf{X}'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\mathbf{X}\)
×
BREAD (Right Inverse Hessian): \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\)
=
ROBUST COVARIANCE ESTIMATOR: \(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{\text{White}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\)
DERIVATION · WHY WHITE'S ESTIMATOR WORKS
Step 1 · Recall OLS Estimation Error:
$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} - \boldsymbol{\beta} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$$
Step 2 · Conditional Covariance:
$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}) = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X})(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}$$
Step 3 · Evaluate the Score Variance:
$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}) &= \sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i' \\ &= \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i' \end{aligned}$$
Step 4 · Substitute Sample Residuals:
By the Law of Large Numbers, \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i'\) converges in probability to \(\operatorname{plim} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i'\).

4. Quick Check & Understanding Test

CHECK 8.3 Do White robust standard errors replace or alter the OLS point estimates? Click to Reveal Solution

Answer: No. White robust standard errors do NOT alter the OLS point estimates \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) in any way.

Why? The vector of estimated regression coefficients remains strictly \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{y}\). What changes is the estimator of the covariance matrix of \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\).

Instead of computing \(\mathrm{s}^2(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\), we evaluate the sandwich formula \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}[\sum \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i'](\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\). The point estimates are preserved, but confidence intervals and hypothesis tests are corrected.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • White’s heteroskedasticity-consistent estimator leaves OLS point estimates unchanged while correcting inference.
  • The HC0 covariance estimator replaces the unobserved \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) with sample outer products of squared residuals: \(\mathbf{X}'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\mathbf{X} = \sum_{i=1}^n \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2 \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i'\).
  • The sandwich structure encloses the empirical score covariance between two inverse Gram matrices \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).
  • White standard errors are asymptotically valid under arbitrary conditional heteroskedasticity without requiring knowledge of its functional form.
  • Using conventional standard errors when heteroskedasticity is present typically understates uncertainty, yielding spuriously high \(t\)-statistics.
