LESSON 8.1 Variance & Assumptions

OLS Under Non-Spherical Errors

What happens when the classical assumption of spherical disturbances fails? We formalize the two principal forms of non-spherical errors, prove that OLS point estimates remain unbiased under strict exogeneity, and derive the exact covariance matrix that makes conventional standard errors invalid.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define the classical spherical-error assumption and decompose it into homoskedasticity and zero autocorrelation.
  • Distinguish between heteroskedasticity (diagonal inequality) and autocorrelation (off-diagonal covariance).
  • Prove that OLS remains strictly unbiased when \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\) even if \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\).
  • Derive the correct small-sample variance-covariance matrix of \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) under non-spherical errors.
  • Explain why conventional standard errors \(\mathrm{s}^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) produce distorted hypothesis tests and confidence intervals.

1. The Classical Spherical-Error Assumption

In the Classical Linear Regression Model, Assumption 4 states that the conditional variance-covariance matrix of the disturbance vector \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) given the regressor matrix \(\mathbf{X}\) is scalar:

ASSUMPTION 4 · SPHERICAL DISTURBANCES
$$\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_n$$

This matrix condition bundles two distinct economic requirements across the \(n\) sample observations:

CONDITION 1

Homoskedasticity

Constant conditional error variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n$$

Every observation has the exact same conditional dispersion around the population regression line.

CONDITION 2

No Autocorrelation

Zero covariance across observations:

$$\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_j \mid \mathbf{X}) = 0 \quad \forall i \neq j$$

The error in observation \(i\) provides strictly zero information about the error realization in observation \(j\).

GEOMETRIC INTUITION

Why Is It Called "Spherical"?

Under \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\), the isoprobability contours of the disturbance vector in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) form perfectly symmetric hyperspheres. Uncertainty is equally scaled in every observational direction, and the principal axes of error dispersion align with the standard Cartesian coordinate axes without tilting or stretching.

2. Violation: Non-Spherical Disturbances (\(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\))

When either condition is violated, the disturbance covariance matrix is written in generalized form:

$$\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega} \quad \text{where} \quad \boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}_n$$

Econometric theory categorizes non-spherical disturbances into two primary empirical scenarios:

CASE 1 · CROSS-SECTION DATA

Heteroskedasticity

Diagonal elements differ while off-diagonal covariances remain zero:

$$\sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_1^2 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma_2^2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \sigma_n^2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Typical Context: Cross-sectional samples where residual variance scales with firm size, household income, or city population.

CASE 2 · TIME-SERIES OR PANEL DATA

Autocorrelation

Off-diagonal elements are nonzero, capturing temporal persistence:

$$\sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega} = \sigma^2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \rho_1 & \cdots & \rho_{n-1} \\ \rho_1 & 1 & \cdots & \rho_{n-2} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \rho_{n-1} & \rho_{n-2} & \cdots & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Typical Context: Time series or panel data where shocks linger across quarters or years (\(\rho > 0\)).

CONCEPTUAL MATRIX VISUAL

5×5 Disturbance Covariance Structure \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\)

Spherical Structure (\(\mathbf{I}\)): Constant unit diagonal variance (\(\sigma^2\)), strictly zero off-diagonal covariances.

3. Finite-Sample Properties of OLS When \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\)

When \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\), does the ordinary least squares estimator become biased? We trace the small-sample expectation directly from the definition:

DERIVATION · OLS UNBIASEDNESS UNDER NON-SPHERICAL ERRORS
Goal: Evaluate \(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}]\) when \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega}\).
Step 1 · Substitute Data Generating Process:
$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{y} = (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'(\mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon})$$
Step 2 · Expand Linear Terms:
$$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} = \boldsymbol{\beta} + (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$$
Step 3 · Take Conditional Expectation:
$$\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \boldsymbol{\beta} + (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}]$$
Key Result · Strict Unbiasedness Holds:
$$\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \boldsymbol{\beta} \quad \text{because} \quad \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}$$
CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Non-Spherical Errors DO NOT Cause OLS Bias

A frequent misconception among practitioners is that heteroskedasticity or autocorrelation biases OLS coefficients. It does not. Unbiasedness depends exclusively on the first moment (\(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\)). Non-spherical errors are a failure of the second moment (\(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}]\)). The estimator remains centered at the truth, but its precision and reported standard errors are distorted.

Derivation of the True OLS Covariance Matrix

Evaluating the variance of \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) reveals where \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) alters the mathematics:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}) &= \mathbb{E}\big[(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} - \boldsymbol{\beta})(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} - \boldsymbol{\beta})' \mid \mathbf{X}\big] \\[6pt] &= \mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}'\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \mid \mathbf{X}\big] \\[6pt] &= (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\,\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}' \mid \mathbf{X}]\,\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \\[6pt] &= \sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \end{aligned}$$
SPHERICAL BENCHMARK (\(\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \mathbf{I}\))
$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}$$

The terms \((\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{I}\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) collapse neatly into a single inverse matrix.

NON-SPHERICAL GENERAL CASE (\(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\))
$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sigma^2 &(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega} \\ &\times \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

What changed? \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) appears trapped between \(\mathbf{X}'\) and \(\mathbf{X}\). The matrix cannot collapse, rendering conventional OLS standard errors wrong.

EXACT FINITE-SAMPLE DISTRIBUTION

Sampling Distribution Under Normal Errors

If the disturbance vector follows a multivariate normal distribution \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{\Omega})\), then \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) conditionally follows:

$$\begin{aligned} \widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X} &\sim \mathcal{N}\big(\boldsymbol{\beta},\; \mathbf{V}_{\text{true}}\big) \\[4pt] \text{where } \mathbf{V}_{\text{true}} &= \sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \\[4pt] &\neq \sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

Linear regression packages that compute \(s^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) inadvertently calculate the wrong covariance matrix, invalidating standard \(t\)-statistics and \(F\)-tests.

5. Quick Check & Understanding Test

CHECK 8.1 If \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\) but \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\), is OLS necessarily biased? Click to Reveal Solution

Answer: No, OLS is strictly unbiased.

Why? The expected value of \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) is: $$\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \boldsymbol{\beta} + (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \boldsymbol{\beta}$$ As long as the conditional mean assumption \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\) holds, the estimator is centered directly at \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\).

The Problem: The conventional standard error formula \(s^2(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\) relies on \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \mathbf{I}\). When \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\), the reported standard errors are wrong, leading to invalid \(t\)-tests, distorted \(p\)-values, and false confidence intervals.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The classical spherical assumption requires both homoskedasticity (\(\sigma^2\) constant) and absence of autocorrelation (\(\operatorname{Cov} = 0\)).
  • Non-spherical errors (\(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\)) commonly arise in cross-sectional data (heteroskedasticity) and time-series/panel data (autocorrelation).
  • OLS point estimates remain strictly unbiased under non-spherical disturbances as long as strict exogeneity \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\) holds.
  • The true small-sample OLS covariance matrix is \(\sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\), not \(\sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).
  • Standard software running default OLS will report invalid standard errors and test statistics whenever \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \neq \mathbf{I}\).
