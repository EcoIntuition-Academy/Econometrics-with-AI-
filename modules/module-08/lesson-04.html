LESSON 8.4 Specification Testing

Testing for Heteroskedasticity

Before deciding whether to report conventional standard errors, robust standard errors, or implement weighted estimation, researchers test whether error variance is constant. We formalize the Breusch–Pagan and White auxiliary regression tests, examine their asymptotic chi-square statistics, and analyze their detection capabilities.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Explain why squared sample residuals \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) act as empirical proxies for conditional variance.
  • Execute the Breusch–Pagan Lagrange Multiplier test and derive its asymptotic \(\chi^2_k\) null distribution.
  • Implement the White general test incorporating linear terms, quadratic squares, and cross-products.
  • Compare the power, specification assumptions, and trade-offs between Breusch–Pagan and White tests.
  • Internalize that rejecting homoskedasticity signals inference failure, NOT coefficient bias.

1. The Breusch–Pagan Test

The Breusch–Pagan (1979) test investigates whether the conditional variance of \(\varepsilon_i\) is linearly related to the explanatory variables in the regression model.

STEP 1

Primary OLS Regression

Estimate the main regression model by OLS and save the residuals: $$\widehat{\varepsilon}_i = y_i - \mathbf{x}_i'\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}$$

STEP 2

Compute Squared Residuals

Calculate the squared residuals to serve as the proxy for error variance: $$\widehat{u}_i \equiv \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2$$

STEP 3

Auxiliary Regression

Regress \(\widehat{u}_i\) on the original \(k\) explanatory variables: $$\widehat{u}_i = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1 x_{i1} + \dots + \gamma_k x_{ik} + v_i$$

STEP 4

Test Statistic Computation

Retrieve \(R^2\) from the auxiliary regression and evaluate: $$n R^2 \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_k$$

BREUSCH–PAGAN TEST WORKFLOW
OLS → Residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i\) → Square \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) → Auxiliary OLS → Test Stat \(n R^2\) → Compare to \(\chi^2_k\)
TEST INTUITION

What Is the Breusch–Pagan Test Asking?

Under the null hypothesis of homoskedasticity \(H_0: \gamma_1 = \dots = \gamma_k = 0\), the explanatory variables have zero predictive power over error magnitude; the auxiliary regression should yield an \(R^2\) close to zero. If the regressors systematically explain fluctuations in squared residuals, \(n R^2\) exceeds the critical value of \(\chi^2_k\), and homoskedasticity is rejected.

2. The White General Test for Heteroskedasticity

The Breusch–Pagan test assumes heteroskedasticity is linear in the regressors. However, variance could depend nonlinearly on the predictors (e.g., \(x^2\)) or on interactions between them (\(x_1 x_2\)). Halbert White (1980) formulated a general test that captures nonlinear variance functions:

WHITE TEST AUXILIARY REGRESSION

Regress squared OLS residuals \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) on all original regressors, their squares, and all distinct cross-products:

$$\begin{aligned} \widehat{u}_i = \gamma_0 &+ \sum_{j=1}^k \gamma_j x_{ij} + \sum_{j=1}^k \delta_j x_{ij}^2 \\ &+ \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum_{m=j+1}^k \lambda_{jm} x_{ij}x_{im} + v_i \end{aligned}$$

Under the null hypothesis of homoskedasticity \(H_0\):

$$n R^2 \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q$$

where \(q\) is the total number of non-constant slope regressors in the expanded auxiliary model.

Methodological Comparison: Breusch–Pagan vs. White

Feature Breusch–Pagan Test White Test
Dependent Variable Squared OLS residuals \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) Squared OLS residuals \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\)
Auxiliary Regressors Original regressors \(x_{i1}, \dots, x_{ik}\) Regressors, squared regressors \(x_{ij}^2\), cross-products \(x_{ij}x_{im}\)
Degrees of Freedom \(k\) \(q = 2k + k(k-1)/2\)
Sensitivity Detects linear variance trends; higher power against monotonic heteroskedasticity Detects general nonlinear variance patterns, but consumes more degrees of freedom
METHODOLOGICAL CAUTION

Rejection of \(H_0\) Does NOT Mean OLS Is Biased

Rejecting the null hypothesis of homoskedasticity (\(p < 0.05\)) informs the researcher that conventional standard errors and \(t\)-statistics are invalid. It does not imply that \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}}\) is biased or inconsistent. Under strict exogeneity \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0}\), OLS point estimates remain unbiased; researchers should simply report White robust standard errors or apply weighted least squares.

4. Quick Check & Understanding Test

CHECK 8.4 Why are squared residuals \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) used as the dependent variable in these tests? Click to Reveal Solution

Answer: Because under the zero conditional mean assumption \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i \mid \mathbf{X}] = 0\), the conditional variance of the disturbance is mathematically defined as \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid \mathbf{X}) = \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i^2 \mid \mathbf{X}]\).

Why? Since the true disturbance \(\varepsilon_i\) is unobservable, the squared OLS residual \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) serves as the natural observable sample proxy for the underlying conditional error variance.

Regressing \(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) on explanatory variables directly tests whether the conditional variance is a function of the regressors, which would directly contradict the constant-variance assumption \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i^2 \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2\).

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The Breusch–Pagan test regresses squared OLS residuals \(\widehat{u}_i = \widehat{\varepsilon}_i^2\) on the original regressors to detect linear heteroskedasticity.
  • The White test expands the auxiliary regression to include squares and cross-products, capturing arbitrary nonlinear variance patterns.
  • Both procedures evaluate the Lagrange Multiplier test statistic \(n R^2\), which asymptotically follows a chi-square distribution under the null hypothesis of homoskedasticity.
  • Rejecting homoskedasticity signals that conventional standard errors are invalid, but does NOT imply that OLS point estimates are biased.
  • When tests reject homoskedasticity, researchers should report White robust standard errors or apply Weighted Least Squares.
