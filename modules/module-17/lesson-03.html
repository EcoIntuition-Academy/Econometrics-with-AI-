LESSON 17.3 Lagrange Multiplier & Trinity

LM / Score Tests & Comparing LR, Wald, LM

Evaluate parameter restrictions solely through the restricted model, formulate the Lagrange Multiplier test from the constrained score vector, construct the Outer Product of Gradients (OPG) estimator, and synthesize the classical trinity: LR, Wald, and LM.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand why the Lagrange Multiplier (Score) test requires only the restricted parameter estimates \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\).
  • Set up the constrained Lagrangian function and derive the first-order conditions.
  • Formulate the LM statistic using both the expected information matrix and the Outer Product of Gradients (OPG) approach.
  • Visually and geometrically contrast the three tests on the likelihood curve (likelihood drop, parameter distance, score slope).
  • Establish that LR, Wald, and LM are asymptotically equivalent under regularity conditions while differing in finite samples.

1. The Lagrange Multiplier (Score) Test

The Lagrange Multiplier (LM) test evaluates whether relaxing the restriction would increase the likelihood significantly, without computing the unrestricted model.

If the constraint is valid, the Lagrange multipliers should be small because the unconstrained maximum naturally satisfies the restriction. If the constraint is false, the objective function (likelihood) would increase substantially if the constraint were relaxed.

CONSTRAINED OPTIMIZATION

The Lagrangian and Constrained First-Order Conditions

\[ \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \ln L(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \boldsymbol{\lambda}' \left[ \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \right] \] \[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} = \frac{\partial \ln L(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} - \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}'} \boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0} \] \[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\lambda}} = \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{0} \]
WHERE:
\(\ln L(\boldsymbol{\theta})\) The unconstrained log-likelihood function.
\(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) Vector of Lagrange multipliers representing shadow prices of relaxing restrictions.
\(\frac{\partial \ln L}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}}\) The score (gradient) vector of the log-likelihood function.
\(\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{0}\) The set of \(q\) restrictions evaluated at the constrained maximum.

💡 Score Principle

At the unrestricted peak \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_U\), the score vector is exactly zero by definition: \(\frac{\partial \ln L(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_U)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} = \mathbf{0}\). At the restricted estimate \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\), the score vector will generally be non-zero unless the restriction happens to sit precisely at the peak. The LM test checks: Is the gradient at \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\) close enough to zero to be sampling noise?

2. Information Matrix and Outer Product of Gradients (OPG) Form

The standard LM statistic standardizes the score vector evaluated at the restricted estimates by the expected information matrix:

TEST STATISTIC · SCORE TEST

The Lagrange Multiplier / Score Statistic

\[ LM = \left( \frac{\partial \ln L(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} \right)' \left[ \mathbf{I}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R) \right]^{-1} \left( \frac{\partial \ln L(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} \right) \] \[ LM \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q \quad \text{under } H_0 \]
WHERE:
\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\) The restricted maximum likelihood parameter estimator.
\(\mathbf{I}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R)\) Expected information matrix evaluated at the restricted parameter estimates.
\(q\) Number of restrictions, serving as degrees of freedom.

In practice, computing the analytical expected information matrix \(\mathbf{I}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R)\) can be cumbersome. Econometricians often utilize the Outer Product of Gradients (OPG) alternative:

COMPUTATIONAL FORM · OPG APPROACH

The Outer Product of Gradients (OPG) Score Statistic

\[ \hat{\mathbf{g}}_{iR} = \frac{\partial \ln f(x_i, \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{g}}_R = \sum_{i=1}^n \hat{\mathbf{g}}_{iR}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{G}}_R = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1R}' \\ \hat{\mathbf{g}}_{2R}' \\ \vdots \\ \hat{\mathbf{g}}_{nR}' \end{bmatrix} \] \[ LM = \hat{\mathbf{g}}_R' \left( \hat{\mathbf{G}}_R' \hat{\mathbf{G}}_R \right)^{-1} \hat{\mathbf{g}}_R \]
WHERE:
\(\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{iR}\) Observation-level gradient evaluated at restricted estimates.
\(\hat{\mathbf{g}}_R\) Total sample score vector at \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\).
\(\hat{\mathbf{G}}_R' \hat{\mathbf{G}}_R\) Sum of outer products of observation gradients, estimating the information matrix.

3. Relationship Between the Three Classical Tests

The source outlines the fundamental relationships and distinctions among the classical testing trinity:

Likelihood Ratio (LR)

Requirement: Needs both restricted (\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\)) and unrestricted (\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_U\)) estimates.

Core Question: How much likelihood is lost when imposing the restriction? (Measures vertical drop).

Wald Test

Requirement: Needs only unrestricted (\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_U\)) estimates.

Core Question: How far is the unrestricted estimate from satisfying \(H_0\), scaled by variance? (Measures horizontal distance).

Lagrange Multiplier (LM)

Requirement: Needs only restricted (\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\)) estimates.

Core Question: Is the likelihood still trying to move away from the restricted point? (Measures tangent slope/score).

⚠️

Common Student Mistake: Finite Sample Equality

MISCONCEPTION: "Because LR, Wald, and LM are asymptotically equivalent, they yield identical numerical test statistics and decisions in every sample."

CORRECTION: As the source emphasizes, "The three tests are asymptotically equivalent under standard regularity conditions. But they differ in finite samples." In finite samples, the inequality \(W \ge LR \ge LM\) frequently holds in linear models, meaning they can yield different p-values in small datasets!

SCENARIO INSIGHT

Under a quadratic log-likelihood, LR, Wald, and LM coincide exactly at 2.88. All three test statistics fall below the critical threshold 3.84, failing to reject \(H_0\).

👁 WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

The log-likelihood curve with peak at \(\hat{\theta}_U = 1.0\). Blue indicates the vertical drop for LR; orange marks the horizontal distance for Wald; green depicts the tangent slope at \(\theta_0\) for LM.

🔎 WHAT TO NOTICE

As \(\theta_0\) approaches \(\hat{\theta}_U = 1.0\), three things happen simultaneously: the vertical height drop vanishes, the horizontal distance goes to zero, and the tangent line becomes completely horizontal (zero slope).

◆ WHY IT MATTERS

Each test represents an alternative computational route to the same core question. If estimating the unconstrained model is difficult, LM needs only the restricted model. If estimating constraints is hard, Wald needs only the unrestricted model.

📊 ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

Under regularity conditions and as sample size \(n \to \infty\), the log-likelihood becomes locally quadratic, making all three statistics asymptotically equivalent: \(LR, W, LM \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q\).

🎮 TRY THIS: Move \(\theta_0\) out to 1.70. Watch the slope of the green tangent line steepen dramatically while the blue height drop widens, sending all three test statistics past 10.0!

5. Quick Knowledge Check

CONCEPT CHECK Trinity Comparison

Which of the three classical tests can be computed solely from the restricted estimator \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\), without estimating the unconstrained model?

Hint: Think about which test evaluates the slope (score) at the constrained point under the null hypothesis.

Solution: The Lagrange Multiplier (LM) or Score test. It checks whether the score vector at the restricted estimate \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\) is significantly different from zero. If the restrictions hold, the restricted estimate is close to the peak, where the score is near zero.

Lesson 17.3 Summary

The classical trinity of likelihood-based specification tests provides complementary perspectives:

  • LM / Score Test: Computed entirely from the restricted model \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_R\), testing whether the gradient vector is statistically indistinguishable from zero.
  • OPG Construction: Uses the outer product of individual observation gradients \(\hat{\mathbf{G}}_R'\hat{\mathbf{G}}_R\) to approximate the information matrix without requiring second derivatives.
  • Trinity Perspective: LR inspects vertical height drop; Wald inspects horizontal parameter gap; LM inspects tangent slope at the restricted candidate.
  • Asymptotic Equivalence: All three statistics converge to \(\chi^2_q\) under \(H_0\), but differ in finite samples.
