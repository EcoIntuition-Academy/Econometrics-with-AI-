LESSON 17.2 Wald & Delta Method

The Wald Test & Delta-Method Variance

Learn how the Wald test evaluates parameter hypotheses using solely the unrestricted model, derive the asymptotic covariance matrix via the Delta Method, and construct the quadratic Wald test statistic.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand why the Wald test avoids computing the restricted model and relies solely on unconstrained estimates \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_U\).
  • Express general linear and nonlinear parameter restrictions in the vector form \(H_0: \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{q}\).
  • Derive the asymptotic covariance matrix of the restriction discrepancy using the Delta Method.
  • Formulate the quadratic Wald statistic \(W\) and verify its asymptotic \(\chi^2_q\) distribution under \(H_0\).
  • Examine the finite-sample parameterization invariance caveat of nonlinear Wald tests.

1. Testing Restrictions via Unrestricted Estimates

The Wald test only requires computation of the unrestricted parameters. It tests whether a set of restrictions is approximately true by checking if the estimated unrestricted parameters satisfy the restriction.

Suppose our null hypothesis specifies \(q\) independent parametric restrictions:

NULL HYPOTHESIS

General Vector Restriction Formulation

\[ H_0 : \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{q} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \mathbf{q} = \mathbf{0} \]
WHERE:
\(\mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\) A \(q \times 1\) vector of continuous, differentiable functions of parameters \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\).
\(\mathbf{q}\) A \(q \times 1\) vector of hypothesized constants (such as \(\mathbf{0}\) or \(\mathbf{q}_0\)).
\(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}\) The raw restriction error vector evaluated at unrestricted estimates.

💡 Core Pedagogical Intuition

"If the restrictions are valid, then \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}\) should be approximately \(\mathbf{0}\)."
This test avoids recalculating the restricted likelihood — it just checks if the unrestricted estimator lies "close enough" to satisfying the restriction, adjusted for sampling variance.

Because \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\) is estimated from random sample data, \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}\) will almost never equal zero exactly in finite samples. We must evaluate whether the observed discrepancy is statistically distinguishable from zero given the sampling uncertainty.

2. The Wald Statistic & Delta-Method Covariance

To standardize the discrepancy vector, the Wald statistic forms a quadratic distance weighted by the inverse of its asymptotic covariance matrix:

TEST STATISTIC · WALD TEST

The Quadratic Wald Test Statistic

\[ W = \left[ \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q} \right]' \left[ \widehat{\text{Avar}}\left( \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q} \right) \right]^{-1} \left[ \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q} \right] \] \[ W \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q \quad \text{under } H_0 \]
WHERE:
\(W\) The scalar Wald test statistic.
\(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}\) Discrepancy vector between unrestricted estimates and hypothesized values.
\(\widehat{\text{Avar}}\left(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right)\) Estimated asymptotic covariance matrix of the restriction vector.
\(q\) Degrees of freedom, equal to the number of restrictions.
STEP-BY-STEP DERIVATION

Asymptotic Variance of \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\) via the Delta Method

GOAL: Understand how sampling uncertainty in \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\) translates into uncertainty in the transformed restriction \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\).

1

Measure Deviation from Restriction

Compute the empirical discrepancy vector between the evaluated function and the hypothesized constant:

\[ \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q} \]
WHY? Because exact satisfaction of the hypothesis in the population would produce \(\mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \mathbf{q} = \mathbf{0}\). Any non-zero \(\mathbf{d}\) represents sample divergence.
2

Linearize \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\) via First-Order Taylor Expansion

Expand \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\) around the true parameter vector \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\):

\[ \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \approx \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}'} \left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta} \right) \] \[ \text{Avar}\left(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right) \approx \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}'} \text{Avar}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} \]
WHY? The function \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\) is a transformation of random estimates and inherits sampling error. The Jacobian matrix \(\frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}'}\) scales the parameter variance to the restriction space.
3

Standardize the Quadratic Form

Substitute the estimated asymptotic covariance matrix directly into the quadratic distance metric:

\[ W = \left[ \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q} \right]' \left[ \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}'} \widehat{\text{Avar}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}} \right]^{-1} \left[ \mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q} \right] \]
WHY? Inverting the variance matrix standardizes the multivariate error vector, eliminating cross-correlation and scaling so that the sum of squared standardized normals converges to a chi-squared distribution.

🔑 KEY RESULT

Under \(H_0\), the Wald statistic \(W \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q\), where \(q\) is the number of restrictions. We reject \(H_0\) at significance level \(\alpha\) if \(W > \chi^2_q(1 - \alpha)\).

📌 Essential Notes on the Wald Test Formulation

  • Computational Convenience: The Wald test only requires the unrestricted estimates. There is no need to formulate or maximize the constrained likelihood function.
  • Invariance Caveat: For nonlinear restrictions, different algebraically equivalent parameterizations can yield different Wald test statistics in finite samples (e.g., testing \(\theta_1 / \theta_2 = 1\) vs \(\theta_1 - \theta_2 = 0\)).

👁 WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

The green vertical line marks the hypothesized value \(q = 0\). The blue circle marks the estimated unrestricted quantity \(c(\hat{\theta})\), centered in its shaded 95% confidence interval \([c(\hat{\theta}) - 1.96\text{SE}, c(\hat{\theta}) + 1.96\text{SE}]\).

🔎 WHAT TO NOTICE

When you increase standard error \(\text{SE}\) without altering raw deviation, the confidence interval expands to cover \(0\). The Wald statistic plummets from decisive rejection to non-rejection!

◆ WHY IT MATTERS

A raw discrepancy of 1.20 has zero statistical meaning without sampling variance. In noisy datasets (large SE), a gap of 1.20 is pure sampling noise; in massive samples (small SE), it represents incontrovertible evidence against \(H_0\).

📊 ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

The Wald test standardizes the discrepancy vector via the Delta Method covariance \([\mathbf{G}\hat{V}\mathbf{G}']^{-1}\). For \(q = 1\), \(W = t^2\), where \(t = (c(\hat{\theta}) - q) / \text{SE}\).

🎮 TRY THIS: Set raw deviation to 1.50 and drag SE from 0.40 to 1.50. Notice how the Wald statistic falls from 14.06 (p < 0.001) to 1.00 (p = 0.317), completely reversing the hypothesis testing conclusion!

4. Quick Knowledge Check

CONCEPT CHECK Wald Mechanics

Why does the Wald statistic scale the restriction discrepancy vector \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}\) by the inverse of its covariance matrix?

Hint: Think about units of measurement, sampling variation, and cross-parameter correlation. What distribution does a sum of squared standardized normals follow?

Solution: Because parameter estimates inherit sampling uncertainty, raw distances \(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}\) depend on arbitrary scaling units and random variance. Weighting by \([\widehat{\text{Avar}}(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}))]^{-1}\) standardizes each linear combination into unit variance and orthogonalizes correlated restrictions, ensuring that under \(H_0\) the resulting quadratic form follows an exact asymptotic \(\chi^2_q\) distribution.

Lesson 17.2 Summary

The Wald test provides the unrestricted branch of the classical hypothesis testing framework:

  • Unrestricted Estimation: Tests \(H_0: \mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{q}\) using only \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_U\), entirely avoiding computation of restricted estimates.
  • Delta Method Covariance: Approximates the asymptotic variance of nonlinear functions as \(\text{Avar}(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})) \approx \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}'} \text{Avar}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \frac{\partial \mathbf{c}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}}\).
  • Quadratic Form: \(W = [\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}]' [\widehat{\text{Avar}}(\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}))]^{-1} [\mathbf{c}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \mathbf{q}] \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q\).
  • Invariance Consideration: For nonlinear restrictions, alternative algebraic formulations of \(\mathbf{c}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\) can lead to different Wald statistics in finite samples.
