LESSON 17.6 Efficient GMM & Synthesis

Efficient GMM, Hansen J & Module Review

Derive the optimal weighting matrix that minimizes estimator asymptotic variance, execute Hansen's J-test of overidentifying restrictions, and synthesize the complete hypothesis testing and GMM framework.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Derive the optimal weighting matrix \(\mathbf{W}^* = \mathbf{S}^{-1}\) as the inverse asymptotic variance of the sample moments.
  • Explain how Efficient GMM accounts for arbitrary heteroskedasticity and cross-instrument correlation.
  • Formulate Hansen's J-test statistic \(J = n \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})' \hat{\mathbf{W}} \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\).
  • Establish that \(J \sim \chi^2_{L-K}\) under \(H_0\) and explain why \(L > K\) is mandatory.
  • Understand proper interpretation: a large \(J\) rejects joint validity, but does not identify which specific instrument is invalid.
  • Synthesize the unified econometric framework across Likelihood Ratio, Wald, LM, GMM, and Hansen J.

1. Efficient GMM and the Optimal Weighting Matrix

While any positive semi-definite matrix \(\mathbf{W}\) produces a consistent GMM estimator when the moment conditions hold, different choices of \(\mathbf{W}\) yield different asymptotic variances.

According to the source, the optimal weighting matrix in GMM is the inverse of the asymptotic variance of the moment conditions:

OPTIMAL INFERENCE · EFFICIENT GMM

The Optimal GMM Weighting Matrix and Estimator

\[ \mathbf{W}^* = \left[ \text{Var}\left( \sqrt{n} \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) \right) \right]^{-1} = \mathbf{S}^{-1} \] \[ \mathbf{S} = \text{plim} \left[ \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{g}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta}) \mathbf{g}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta})' \right] \] \[ \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{GMM} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \left[ \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})' \mathbf{S}^{-1} \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) \right] \]
WHERE:
\(\mathbf{S}\) Asymptotic covariance matrix of the empirical moment conditions.
\(\mathbf{S}^{-1}\) Optimal weighting matrix, precision-weighting the moment vector.
\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{GMM}\) The efficient GMM estimator achieving the minimum possible asymptotic covariance.

💡 Core Pedagogical Intuition

"Efficient GMM chooses the weighting matrix that minimizes the asymptotic variance of the estimator. It accounts for heteroskedasticity and correlation across instruments."
Just as GLS weights observations by their variance, Efficient GMM weights moment conditions by their precision. Highly noisy moments receive little weight, while precise, low-variance moments receive heavy weight.

2. Testing Overidentifying Restrictions: Hansen's J-Test

Recall that when \(L > K\), we have more instruments than regressors. The model cannot generally set every sample moment exactly to zero.

Under the null hypothesis, all \(L\) instruments are valid and uncorrelated with the structural error term:

SPECIFICATION TEST · HANSEN J-TEST

Hansen's J-Statistic for Overidentifying Restrictions

\[ H_0 : \mathbb{E}\left[ \mathbf{z}_i (y_i - \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}) \right] = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{(all instruments are valid)} \] \[ J = n \cdot \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})' \cdot \hat{\mathbf{W}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) \] \[ J \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{L - K} \quad \text{under } H_0 \]
WHERE:
\(J\) Hansen's J-test statistic of overidentifying restrictions.
\(\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\) Sample moment residual vector evaluated at the efficient GMM estimates.
\(\hat{\mathbf{W}} = \hat{\mathbf{S}}^{-1}\) Consistent estimate of the optimal weighting matrix.
\(L - K\) Degrees of freedom, equal to the number of overidentifying restrictions.

Source-Supported Interpretation

If \(J\) is too large: the moment conditions cannot all be simultaneously satisfied, leading to rejection of the null hypothesis. The source states that this indicates that some instruments may be invalid.

Mandatory Applicability Condition

Hansen's J-test is valid only when the model is overidentified (\(L > K\)). When \(L = K\), \(\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = \mathbf{0}\) by algebraic construction, making \(J = 0\) identically with \(0\) degrees of freedom.

⚠️

Common Student Mistake: Pinpointing Instrument Failure

MISCONCEPTION: "A statistically significant Hansen J-statistic proves exactly which specific instrument is invalid."

CORRECTION: As econometrics demands, do not claim that \(J\) identifies which instrument is invalid. The J-test is an omnibus test of the joint validity of the entire system of restrictions. Rejection indicates that the instruments are mutually incompatible; it cannot diagnose whether Instrument 1, Instrument 2, or structural misspecification is the culprit.

👁 WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

🔎 WHAT TO NOTICE

When you switch to "Conflicting Instruments", the moment vector leaps far outside the acceptance ellipse. Even though GMM chooses \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) to minimize distance, the moments cannot be simultaneously brought near zero!

◆ WHY IT MATTERS

Hansen's J-test is the primary omnibus specification test for overidentified models. If \(J\) rejects, the structural model or instrument exogeneity assumptions are fundamentally flawed.

📊 ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

Under \(H_0\), \(J = n \hat{\mathbf{g}}'\hat{\mathbf{W}}\hat{\mathbf{g}} \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{L - K}\). Notice that \(J\) scales directly with \(n\): in large samples, even modest correlation with disturbances drives \(J\) to significant rejection.

🎮 TRY THIS: Switch to Conflicting Instruments and increase sample size \(n\) from 100 to 400. Watch the J-statistic explode from 7.84 to 31.36, highlighting how large samples sharpen power against invalid instruments!

5. Quick Knowledge Check

CONCEPT CHECK Hansen J Preconditions

Why can Hansen's J-test of overidentifying restrictions NOT be computed in a just-identified model where \(L = K\)?

Hint: What are the degrees of freedom \(L - K\) when \(L = K\)? What value does the sample moment vector \(\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\) take?

Solution: When \(L = K\), the number of moment equations exactly equals the number of parameters. The sample moment vector can be solved exactly to zero: \(\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = \mathbf{0}\). Consequently, \(J = n \hat{\mathbf{g}}'\mathbf{W}\hat{\mathbf{g}} = 0\) by algebraic necessity, and the degrees of freedom \(L - K = 0\). The test has no overidentifying information with which to test validity; it is applicable strictly when \(L > K\).

Lesson 17.6 Summary & Module Review

Efficient GMM and Hansen's J-test complete the modern econometric specification toolkit:

  • Efficient Weighting: Choosing \(\mathbf{W}^* = \mathbf{S}^{-1}\) minimizes estimator variance and accounts for heteroskedasticity and cross-instrument correlation.
  • Hansen's J-Test: Tests \(H_0: \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}_i(y_i - \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta})] = \mathbf{0}\) via \(J = n \hat{\mathbf{g}}' \hat{\mathbf{W}} \hat{\mathbf{g}} \sim \chi^2_{L-K}\).
  • Proper Interpretation: Rejection indicates joint incompatibility of moments, not specific individual instrument culpability. Requires \(L > K\).
  • Mastery Achieved: From likelihood-based testing (LR, Wald, LM) to moment-based estimation and overidentification testing (GMM, Hansen J), you have mastered the foundational econometric principles of specification testing!
