LESSON 7.4 Changes & Covariates

DDD in Changes & the Role of Controls

When repeated cross-sections or panel data are observed before and after treatment, economists can formulate the DDD model directly in changes. We also analyze how conditioning on covariates affects precision and interpretation.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Formulate the first-differenced DDD specification in changes (\(\Delta \text{score}_{itc}\)).
  • Understand why differencing over time eliminates time-invariant city, grade, and interaction levels.
  • Derive the group means of the changes specification and verify that the double difference in changes equals \(\beta_7\).
  • Compare the structural trade-offs between the Levels model and the Changes model.
  • Distinguish between Raw DDD (unadjusted group means) and Controlled DDD (\(\mathbb{E}[\text{score} \mid W]\)).
  • Recognize how observable controls reduce standard errors and guard against compositional differences.

1. Alternative Specification: The DDD Model in Changes

Instead of estimating the saturated levels regression across eight group-time combinations, an equivalent and popular empirical approach is to first-difference the data over time. In this specification, the dependent variable is the pre-to-post change in test score:

DDD SPECIFICATION IN CHANGES Pre-to-Post First-Differenced Model
\[\begin{aligned} \Delta \text{score}_{itc} = \; & \beta_4 + \beta_5 \text{Philly}_i + \beta_6 \text{Junior}_i \\ & + \mathbf{\beta_7 (\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i)} + \Delta W'_{ic}\Gamma + u_{itc} \end{aligned}\]
where \(\Delta \text{score}_{itc} = \text{score}_{itc, \text{Post}} - \text{score}_{itc, \text{Pre}}\), and \(u_{itc} = \varepsilon_{itc, \text{Post}} - \varepsilon_{itc, \text{Pre}}\).
INTUITION: WHY USE THE CHANGES SPECIFICATION?

Differencing over time automatically sweeps away all time-invariant components from the levels model.

Notice what happened to \(\beta_0\), \(\beta_1\), \(\beta_2\), and \(\beta_3\): because permanent city differences (\(\beta_1\)), permanent grade gaps (\(\beta_2\)), and their baseline interaction (\(\beta_3\)) do not change over time, subtracting Pre from Post removes them entirely.

In the changes model, the constant is the common time trend \(\beta_4\), the city dummy captures the city shock \(\beta_5\), the grade dummy captures the grade trend \(\beta_6\), and the two-way interaction term \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i\) carries the DDD treatment effect \(\beta_7\)!

2. Group Means and Derivation in Changes

Evaluating the expected value \(\mathbb{E}[\Delta \text{score}_{itc}]\) for each of the four city-by-grade groups yields:

Subgroup Pittsburgh (\(\text{Philly}=0\)) Philadelphia (\(\text{Philly}=1\)) Change Difference (\(\text{Philly} - \text{Pitt}\))
Sophomores (\(\text{Junior}=0\)) \(\beta_4\) \(\beta_4 + \beta_5\) \(\beta_5\)
Juniors (\(\text{Junior}=1\)) \(\beta_4 + \beta_6\) \(\beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7\) \(\beta_5 + \beta_7\)
Difference Across Grades (\(\text{Jr} - \text{Soph}\)) \(\beta_6\) \(\beta_6 + \beta_7\) \(\mathbf{\beta_7}\)
ALGEBRAIC CHECK

Double Differencing in Changes

Subtract the change in Pittsburgh juniors from Philadelphia juniors, and subtract the change in Pittsburgh sophomores from Philadelphia sophomores:

DOUBLE DIFFERENCE IN CHANGES Subgroup Contrast of Over-Time Differences
\[\begin{aligned} \text{DDD} &= \Big( \mathbb{E}[\Delta \text{score} \mid \text{Phil, Jr}] - \mathbb{E}[\Delta \text{score} \mid \text{Pitt, Jr}] \Big) \\ &\quad - \Big( \mathbb{E}[\Delta \text{score} \mid \text{Phil, Soph}] - \mathbb{E}[\Delta \text{score} \mid \text{Pitt, Soph}] \Big) \\[8pt] &= \Big[ (\beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7) - (\beta_4 + \beta_6) \Big] - \Big[ (\beta_4 + \beta_5) - \beta_4 \Big] \\[8pt] &= (\beta_5 + \beta_7) - \beta_5 \\[8pt] &= \mathbf{\beta_7} \end{aligned}\]

3. Levels Model vs. Changes Model

LEVELS SPECIFICATION

Full Saturated Model

Includes the full set of time-invariant main effects, baseline group interactions, and time interactions:

  • Dependent Variable: \(\text{score}_{itc}\) (untransformed level).
  • Parameters: 8 coefficients (\(\beta_0\) through \(\beta_7\)).
  • Baseline Transparency: Explicitly reveals permanent level differences (\(\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3\)) across cities and grades.
  • DDD Parameter: Loaded on the three-way interaction \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i \times \text{Time}_t\).
CHANGES SPECIFICATION

First-Differenced Model

First differencing removes time-invariant terms; the Philadelphia × Junior interaction then carries the DDD parameter \(\beta_7\):

  • Dependent Variable: \(\Delta \text{score}_{itc}\) (over-time change).
  • Parameters: 4 coefficients (\(\beta_4, \beta_5, \beta_6, \beta_7\)).
  • Absorbed Terms: \(\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3\) are differenced out completely.
  • DDD Parameter: Loaded on the two-way interaction \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i\).

4. Raw DDD vs. Controlled DDD

An essential methodological distinction in empirical practice is whether the DDD estimator includes observable covariates (\(W_{ic}\)).

SPECIFICATION 1

Raw DDD (Without Controls)

When no control variables are included (\(W_{ic}\) omitted), the regression estimates correspond strictly to unadjusted sample group means across the eight cells:

\[\widehat{\beta}_7^{\text{raw}} = (\Delta \bar{Y}_{\text{Phil, Jr}} - \Delta \bar{Y}_{\text{Pitt, Jr}}) - (\Delta \bar{Y}_{\text{Phil, Soph}} - \Delta \bar{Y}_{\text{Pitt, Soph}})\]

Equivalent to calculating simple group arithmetic without conditioning on student demographics or baseline ability.

SPECIFICATION 2

Controlled DDD (With Covariates)

When student and classroom controls \(W_{ic}\) are added, comparisons become conditional expectations:

\[\mathbb{E}[\text{score}_{itc} \mid \text{Philly}_i, \text{Junior}_i, \text{Time}_t, W_{ic}]\]

Examples of relevant pre-treatment controls include student gender, baseline test scores, and school resource indicators.

METHODOLOGICAL PRECISION

How Controls Benefit the DDD Estimator

Adding observable pre-treatment controls in a DDD model serves two key purposes:

  • 1. Improves Estimation Precision (Lowers Standard Errors): Controlling for strong predictors of student achievement (such as baseline ability) soaks up residual variance (\(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\)), leading to narrower confidence intervals and smaller standard errors for \(\widehat{\beta}_7\).
  • 2. Mitigates Omitted-Variable Bias from Compositional Shifts: If the demographic composition of Philadelphia juniors shifted between the pre- and post-periods relative to other groups, conditioning on \(W_{ic}\) helps account for these observable imbalances.
CAUTION ECONOMETRIC CAUTION: Choosing Controls

Relevant pre-treatment covariates may improve precision and help adjust for observable imbalance. Post-treatment variables or poorly chosen controls can alter the estimand or introduce bias.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The DDD model can be written in first differences (\(\Delta \text{score}_{itc}\)), eliminating time-invariant baseline terms \(\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3\).
  • In the changes specification, the two-way interaction \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i\) carries the DDD parameter \(\beta_7\).
  • Levels and changes specifications are mathematically complementary representations of the same underlying triple-difference logic.
  • Raw DDD evaluates unadjusted sample group averages, whereas controlled DDD evaluates conditional expectations \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid W]\).
  • Relevant pre-treatment covariates reduce residual variance (lowering standard errors) and adjust for observable compositional shifts.
