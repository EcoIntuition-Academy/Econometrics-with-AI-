LESSON 7.2 Model Specification

The DDD Regression & Coefficient Interpretation

To estimate a triple difference in an econometric regression, we specify a saturated model containing main effects, all pairwise two-way interactions, and a single triple-interaction term.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Formalize the Philadelphia vs. Pittsburgh extra-math tutoring empirical design.
  • Write down the complete 8-parameter saturated DDD regression equation.
  • Decompose all 8 expected group means across City, Grade, and Time for both Pre and Post periods.
  • Interpret each individual coefficient from the baseline constant \(\beta_0\) to the double-interactions \(\beta_3, \beta_5, \beta_6\).
  • Understand why the triple-interaction parameter \(\beta_7\) captures the true differential post-treatment gain.

1. Empirical Research Design: Extra Math in Philadelphia

Consider an educational intervention where an extra-math tutoring program is specifically targeted at high school juniors in Philadelphia public schools. We want to estimate whether this program improved standardized math test scores (\(\text{score}_{itc}\)).

THE THREE DIMENSIONS

Defining the Indicator Variables

1. City Dimension (\(\text{Philly}_i\))
  • \(\text{Philly}_i = 1\): Philadelphia (Treated City)
  • \(\text{Philly}_i = 0\): Pittsburgh (Control City)
2. Grade Dimension (\(\text{Junior}_i\))
  • \(\text{Junior}_i = 1\): Juniors (Targeted Grade)
  • \(\text{Junior}_i = 0\): Sophomores (Placebo Grade)
3. Time Dimension (\(\text{Time}_t\))
  • \(\text{Time}_t = 1\): Post-Treatment School Year
  • \(\text{Time}_t = 0\): Pre-Treatment School Year
Targeted Group: The only students who participate in the extra-math tutoring program are Philadelphia Juniors in the Post-Treatment period (\(\text{Philly}_i = 1, \text{Junior}_i = 1, \text{Time}_t = 1\)).

2. Full Saturated DDD Regression Model

To allow complete flexibility for all main effects, permanent baseline gaps, and grade- and city-specific time shocks, the DDD regression includes all three main indicators, all three two-way interaction products, and the single three-way interaction product:

DDD SPECIFICATION Philadelphia × Junior × Post
\[\begin{aligned} \text{score}_{itc} = \; & \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Philly}_i + \beta_2 \text{Junior}_i + \beta_3 (\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i) \\ & + \beta_4 \text{Time}_t + \beta_5 (\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Time}_t) + \beta_6 (\text{Junior}_i \times \text{Time}_t) \\ & + \beta_7 (\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i \times \text{Time}_t) + W'_{ic}\Gamma + \varepsilon_{itc} \end{aligned}\]
where \(W'_{ic}\) is a vector of pre-treatment student and classroom covariates, and \(\varepsilon_{itc}\) is the idiosyncratic error term.
KEY PARAMETER WHERE / KEY PARAMETER: \(\beta_7\)

Notice that the triple-interaction term \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i \times \text{Time}_t\) equals \(1\) if and only if the student is a junior in Philadelphia during the post-treatment period. \(\beta_7\) is the DDD estimand in this specification. Under the stated identifying assumptions, it has the causal treatment-effect interpretation.

3. Decomposing the Eight Group Means

With three binary dimensions (\(2 \times 2 \times 2\)), there are exactly eight possible group-by-time cells. Evaluating the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[\text{score}_{itc}]\) for each cell across both the PRE-TREATMENT PERIOD (\(\text{Time}_t = 0\)) and POST-TREATMENT PERIOD (\(\text{Time}_t = 1\)) reveals how each \(\beta\) coefficient enters the model:

PITTSBURGH (\(\text{Philly} = 0\)) — CONTROL CITY

Comparison City · No Tutoring Policy
Grade Level PRE-TREATMENT PERIOD (\(\text{Time}=0\)) POST-TREATMENT PERIOD (\(\text{Time}=1\)) \(\Delta\) (Time Change)
Sophomores (\(\text{Junior}=0\)) \(\beta_0\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_4\) \(\Delta_{\text{Pitt Soph}} = \beta_4\)
Juniors (\(\text{Junior}=1\)) \(\beta_0 + \beta_2\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_2 + \beta_4 + \beta_6\) \(\Delta_{\text{Pitt Jr}} = \beta_4 + \beta_6\)
Within-Pittsburgh DD (Grade Secular Difference): \(\text{DD}_{\text{Pitt}} = \Delta_{\text{Pitt Jr}} - \Delta_{\text{Pitt Soph}} = (\beta_4 + \beta_6) - \beta_4 = \beta_6\)

PHILADELPHIA (\(\text{Philly} = 1\)) — TREATED CITY

Target City · Intensive Tutoring Reform for Juniors
Grade Level PRE-TREATMENT PERIOD (\(\text{Time}=0\)) POST-TREATMENT PERIOD (\(\text{Time}=1\)) \(\Delta\) (Time Change)
Sophomores (\(\text{Junior}=0\)) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_4 + \beta_5\) \(\Delta_{\text{Philly Soph}} = \beta_4 + \beta_5\)
Juniors (\(\text{Junior}=1\)) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_3\) \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_3 + \beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \mathbf{\beta_7}\) \(\Delta_{\text{Philly Jr}} = \beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7\)
Within-Philadelphia DD (Treated Grade vs Placebo Grade): \(\text{DD}_{\text{Philly}} = \Delta_{\text{Philly Jr}} - \Delta_{\text{Philly Soph}} = \beta_6 + \beta_7\)

4. Exact Interpretation of All Eight Parameters

Every coefficient in the saturated DDD equation corresponds to a specific structural component of the data-generating process:

\(\beta_0\) (Baseline Constant)

Average pre-period score for the baseline reference group: Pittsburgh Sophomores (\(\text{Philly}=0, \text{Junior}=0, \text{Time}=0\)).

\(\beta_1\) (City Baseline Gap)

Permanent pre-treatment baseline difference between Philadelphia sophomores and Pittsburgh sophomores.

\(\beta_2\) (Grade Baseline Gap)

Permanent pre-treatment baseline difference between juniors and sophomores in the control city (Pittsburgh).

\(\beta_3\) (City × Grade Baseline Interaction)

Permanent Philadelphia × Junior baseline interaction: the additional pre-period difference between juniors and sophomores specific to Philadelphia beyond \(\beta_1\) and \(\beta_2\).

\(\beta_4\) (Common Time Trend)

Over-time change in test scores experienced by the baseline group (Pittsburgh sophomores) between pre- and post-periods.

\(\beta_5\) (City × Time Shock)

The confounding shock: Additional over-time change common to all Philadelphia students (both sophomores and juniors), such as a district-wide curriculum reform.

\(\beta_6\) (Grade × Time Trend)

Additional over-time change common to all juniors across both cities (e.g., statewide changes in junior-year standardized testing formats).

\(\beta_7\) (Triple Interaction Estimand)

The DDD estimand: additional post-treatment change for Philadelphia juniors beyond lower-order group, geography, and time effects. Under the stated identifying assumptions, it has the causal treatment-effect interpretation.

WHAT DOES \(\beta_7\) ISOLATE?

\(\beta_7\) is the additional post-treatment change for Philadelphia juniors after the specification accounts for:

  • The permanent Philadelphia level difference (\(\beta_1\)).
  • The permanent junior-group level difference (\(\beta_2\)).
  • The permanent Philadelphia × Junior baseline interaction (\(\beta_3\)).
  • The common post-period change (\(\beta_4\)).
  • The Philadelphia-specific time change (\(\beta_5\)).
  • The junior-specific time change (\(\beta_6\)).
TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The full DDD specification includes 3 main effects (\(\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_4\)), 3 two-way interactions (\(\beta_3, \beta_5, \beta_6\)), and 1 triple-interaction term (\(\beta_7\)).
  • The triple interaction \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i \times \text{Time}_t\) activates only for Philadelphia juniors in the post-treatment period.
  • Decomposing the saturated model yields all 8 expected group means across City, Grade, and Time.
  • The parameter \(\beta_5\) absorbs city-specific time shocks, while \(\beta_6\) absorbs grade-specific time shocks.
  • \(\beta_7\) is the DDD estimand. Under the stated identifying assumptions, it has the causal treatment-effect interpretation.
