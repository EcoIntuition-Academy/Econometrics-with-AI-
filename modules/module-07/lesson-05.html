LESSON 7.5 Identification & Review

DDD Assumptions, Interpretation & Module Review

We conclude Module 7 by formalizing the three essential identification assumptions underpinning the DDD estimator, tying its causal interpretation strictly to empirical conditions, and synthesizing the entire architecture.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • State and scrutinize the three formal identifying assumptions required for a valid DDD design.
  • Articulate why the causal interpretation of \(\beta_7\) is conditional on these assumptions rather than guaranteed unconditionally.
  • Synthesize the core purposes, identifying logic, and formal definition of the DDD design.
  • Trace the complete conceptual map from standard DD through concurrent shocks to the triple interaction.
  • Review all 11 core concepts of Module 7 through modular reference cards.

1. The Three DDD Identifying Assumptions

The Difference-in-Difference-in-Differences design relies on three distinct identifying conditions to establish a credible counterfactual:

ASSUMPTION 1

Appropriate Counterfactual Trends

Treated and control groups would follow the relevant common counterfactual trends absent treatment.

In our tutoring example, absent policy intervention or localized shocks, test scores across Pittsburgh and Philadelphia across both grades would have moved along parallel trajectories over time.

ASSUMPTION 2

Comparable Shock Exposure

Placebo and treated subgroups are similarly exposed to the common confounding shock that the third difference is intended to remove.

The concurrent Philadelphia-specific educational shock (\(\beta_5\)) must impact sophomores and juniors equally. If the shock disproportionately impacted juniors, subtracting the sophomore DD will not completely purge \(\beta_5\).

ASSUMPTION 3

No Treatment on Placebo Subgroup

Only the targeted subgroup receives the treatment effect being identified; the placebo subgroup is unaffected.

The placebo subgroup (sophomores in Philadelphia) must experience strictly zero direct benefit or indirect spillover from the extra-math tutoring program targeted at juniors.

INTERPRETATION

Conditional Causal Claim

Under the stated DDD identifying assumptions, \(\beta_7\) has the causal treatment-effect interpretation in this design.

Causality is not an inherent mathematical property of the OLS formula; it holds if and only if the identifying counterfactual conditions are economically valid.

FAILURE MODES

Identification Breakdown Scenarios

  • The placebo subgroup receives its own unique or disproportionate shock.
  • Spillover effects contaminate the placebo subgroup (e.g., resources reallocated away from sophomores).
  • Treatment resources are reallocated between groups.
  • Relevant counterfactual trends differ across groups absent the intervention.

2. Synthesis: What DDD Does

Difference-in-Difference-in-Differences extends the standard DD framework by introducing a third comparison dimension. The third difference is designed to remove an additional common shock shared by the treatment and placebo subgroups.

DEFINITION

Difference-in-Difference-in-Differences Estimator

Model Component: \(D_i \times \text{Post}_t \times G_i\)
Population Parameter: \(\beta_7\)
Estimated Coefficient: \(\widehat{\beta}_7\)

Interpretation: Under the identifying assumptions, \(\beta_7\) captures the additional post-treatment change for the targeted subgroup in the treated geography beyond the lower-order group, geography, and time effects.

CAPABILITIES

What DDD Can Do

  • Extends DD with a Third Comparison Dimension: Leverages geography, time, and subgroup eligibility simultaneously.
  • Can Remove Shocks Shared by Treated and Placebo Subgroups: Purges common environmental or district-wide changes that violate standard parallel trends.
  • Useful When Treatment Affects One Subgroup: Operates effectively when an intervention is targeted at one specific cohort while leaving peers in the same jurisdiction untreated.
  • Requires a Credible Comparison Subgroup: Identification hinges on having an unaffected comparison subgroup exposed to the same broader institutional environment.

4. Module 7 Core Knowledge Cards

Review the 11 foundational concepts established across all lessons of Module 7:

01 · DD

Difference-in-Differences

Compares over-time change in treated group minus over-time change in control group to eliminate time-invariant group traits and macro time trends.

02 · DDD

Difference-in-Diff-in-Diff

The difference between two DD estimates: one computed on the targeted group, and the second on an unaffected placebo subgroup.

03 · PLACEBO

Placebo Group

A group residing in the treated environment that is ineligible for or unaffected by the treatment, exposing common environmental shocks.

04 · THIRD DIFF

Third Difference

Subtracting the placebo DD from the treated DD, canceling out location-specific concurrent shocks (\(\beta_5\)).

05 · INTERACTION

Triple Interaction

The regressor product \(D_i \times \text{Post}_t \times G_i\), switching to \(1\) only for treated units in the post-treatment era.

06 · ESTIMAND

Parameter \(\beta_7\) vs Estimate \(\widehat{\beta}_7\)

The population parameter \(\beta_7\) represents the true triple-difference estimand, with \(\widehat{\beta}_7\) as its OLS sample estimate matching \(\text{DD}_1 - \text{DD}_2\).

07 · LEVELS

Levels Specification

The 8-parameter regression estimating \(Y_{itc}\) directly, explicitly preserving baseline group level differences \(\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3\).

08 · CHANGES

Changes Specification

The first-differenced regression on \(\Delta Y_{itc}\), sweeping away time-invariant baseline terms where \(\beta_7\) attaches to \(\text{Philly} \times \text{Junior}\).

09 · RAW DDD

Raw DDD

Unadjusted group mean arithmetic across the eight cells without conditioning on student or school covariates.

10 · CONTROLLED

Controlled DDD

DDD estimating conditional expectations \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid W]\), improving precision (lowering SEs) and adjusting for observable demographic shifts.

11 · IDENTIFICATION

DDD Assumptions

Common counterfactual trends, equal exposure of placebo and treated subgroups to confounding shocks, and strictly no treatment spillovers.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The DDD causal claim rests on three assumptions: parallel counterfactual trends, equal shock exposure, and no placebo spillovers.
  • Causal attribution to \(\beta_7\) is always conditional on these identification assumptions holding in the empirical context.
  • DDD is especially valuable for targeted state or municipal policies where an unaffected local group can be observed.
  • The analytical equivalence between the levels regression, changes regression, and cell subtraction gives DDD robust interpretability.
  • Module 7 completes the advanced study of Difference-in-Differences quasi-experimental methodologies.
