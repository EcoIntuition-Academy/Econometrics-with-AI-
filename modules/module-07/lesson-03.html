LESSON 7.3 Derivation & Interactive Lab

Deriving the Triple-Interaction Effect

In this lesson, we prove algebraically that subtracting the Difference-in-Differences of the placebo group from that of the treated group isolates the triple-interaction coefficient \(\beta_7\), netting out city-wide concurrent shocks.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Derive the junior-subgroup difference-in-differences: \(\text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} = \beta_5 + \beta_7\).
  • Derive the sophomore-subgroup placebo difference-in-differences: \(\text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = \beta_5\).
  • Perform the third differencing step proving \(\text{DDD} = \beta_7\).
  • Understand intuitively how subtracting \(\text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}}\) purges the local confounding trend \(\beta_5\).
  • Experiment with arbitrary group means in the Interactive DDD Table Builder to see the arithmetic in real time.

1. Derivation Journey: Proving \(\text{DDD} = \beta_7\)

We now evaluate the algebraic subtractions using the group means established in Lesson 7.2.

DERIVATION GOAL

What Are We Trying to Show?

Show that under the saturated regression model, the sample Difference-in-Difference-in-Differences estimator identically recovers the triple-interaction coefficient: \[\text{DDD} \equiv \text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} - \text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = \beta_7\]

A

Part A: Over-Time Change & DD for Juniors

\(\text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} = \beta_5 + \beta_7\)

Compute the pre-to-post change for juniors in each city:

\[\begin{aligned} \Delta \text{Philly}_{\text{Jr}} &= (\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_3 + \beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7) - (\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_3) \\[4pt] &= \beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7 \\[8pt] \Delta \text{Pitt}_{\text{Jr}} &= (\beta_0 + \beta_2 + \beta_4 + \beta_6) - (\beta_0 + \beta_2) = \beta_4 + \beta_6 \end{aligned}\]

Subtracting Pittsburgh juniors from Philadelphia juniors yields the standard junior DD:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} &= \Delta \text{Philly}_{\text{Jr}} - \Delta \text{Pitt}_{\text{Jr}} \\[6pt] &= (\beta_4 + \beta_5 + \beta_6 + \beta_7) - (\beta_4 + \beta_6) = \mathbf{\beta_5 + \beta_7} \end{aligned}\]
What this shows

A standard DD on juniors alone is contaminated by \(\beta_5\)—the city-wide shock common to all Philadelphia schools.

B

Part B: Over-Time Change & DD for Sophomores

\(\text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = \beta_5\)

Now compute the pre-to-post change for the placebo subgroup (sophomores):

\[\begin{aligned} \Delta \text{Philly}_{\text{Soph}} &= (\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_4 + \beta_5) - (\beta_0 + \beta_1) = \beta_4 + \beta_5 \\[6pt] \Delta \text{Pitt}_{\text{Soph}} &= (\beta_0 + \beta_4) - \beta_0 = \beta_4 \end{aligned}\]

Subtracting Pittsburgh sophomores from Philadelphia sophomores gives:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} &= \Delta \text{Philly}_{\text{Soph}} - \Delta \text{Pitt}_{\text{Soph}} \\[6pt] &= (\beta_4 + \beta_5) - \beta_4 = \mathbf{\beta_5} \end{aligned}\]
What this shows

Because sophomores received no tutoring, their difference-in-differences measures purely the Philadelphia-specific concurrent time shock \(\beta_5\).

C

Part C: The Third Difference

\(\text{DDD} = \beta_7\)

Finally, take the difference between the two Difference-in-Differences:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{DDD} &= \text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} - \text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} \\[6pt] &= (\beta_5 + \beta_7) - \beta_5 = \mathbf{\beta_7} \end{aligned}\]
The Core Algebraic Mechanism

The third difference cancels the confounding city-wide shock \(\beta_5\), leaving strictly the triple interaction \(\beta_7\).

KEY RESULT · DDD ESTIMATOR Equivalence with Triple Interaction Coefficient
\[\mathbf{\text{DDD} = \beta_7}\]

Under the stated model assumptions, the triple-difference sample estimator identically equals the OLS coefficient on the triple interaction \(\text{Philly}_i \times \text{Junior}_i \times \text{Time}_t\).

THE BIG PICTURE: WHY THE THIRD DIFFERENCE WORKS

The third difference removes the extra Philadelphia-wide time change \(\beta_5\) that affects both junior and sophomore groups. The remaining coefficient \(\beta_7\) captures the differential post-treatment effect specific to Philadelphia juniors under the model and identifying assumptions.

3. Visual Intuition: Five Perspectives on the DDD Table

WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

A complete \(2 \times 2 \times 2\) table showing all eight group-by-time means across Grade, City, and Time, alongside their first differences (\(\Delta\)) and subgroup DDs.

WHAT TO NOTICE

Even though sophomores received zero tutoring, their DD is \(+5.0\) in the baseline. This demonstrates that Philadelphia experienced a city-wide shock that lifted all students' test scores over time.

WHY IT MATTERS

If we had evaluated this policy with standard DD using juniors only, we would have erroneously claimed the tutoring improved scores by \(+13.0\) points! DDD prevents this \(+5.0\) point overestimation.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

Under the assumption that the confounding shock \(\beta_5\) impacted sophomores and juniors equally, the difference \(\text{DD}_{\text{Jr}} - \text{DD}_{\text{Soph}}\) strictly identifies the triple-interaction parameter \(\beta_7\).

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The junior Difference-in-Differences is \(\text{DD}_{\text{Juniors}} = \beta_5 + \beta_7\), which includes the common city shock.
  • The sophomore Difference-in-Differences is \(\text{DD}_{\text{Sophomores}} = \beta_5\), which isolates the common city shock.
  • Subtracting the two DDs cancels \(\beta_5\), leaving \(\text{DDD} = \beta_7\).
  • The third difference removes concurrent city-wide reforms that would otherwise bias a standard DD.
  • The interactive table demonstrates that altering the city shock shifts both DDs equally, leaving the triple difference invariant.
