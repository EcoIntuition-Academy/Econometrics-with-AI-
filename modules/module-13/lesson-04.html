LESSON 13.4 Experimental Design

Blocked Randomization & Fisher's Exact Test

Simple randomization can produce chance imbalances on key baseline covariates in small samples. Learn how stratified or blocked randomization ensures group balance and improves precision, and explore Fisher's exact test for contingency tables.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define stratified (blocked) randomization and conceptualize experiments as mini-experiments within strata.
  • Explain how blocking guarantees balance on critical covariates and enhances treatment-effect precision.
  • Specify the regression model with block indicator fixed effects \(Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 T_i + \sum \gamma_b D_{ib} + \varepsilon_i\).
  • Distinguish blocked randomization from cluster-robust inference.
  • Implement Fisher's exact test on a \(2 \times 2\) contingency table under the sharp null.

1. Stratified & Blocked Randomization

DESIGN DEFINITION

Blocked (Stratified) Randomization

In stratified or blocked randomization, the sample is divided into mutually exclusive subgroups (strata or blocks) based on observable baseline characteristics—such as age, income bracket, or gender—and treatment is randomized independently within each subgroup.

Architecture: One Experiment vs. Mini-Experiments

COMPLETE RANDOMIZATION
Full Sample (N)
↓ (Single Lottery)
Treated (n₁)  |  Control (n₀)

Subject to finite-sample chance imbalance. For instance, treatment might inadvertently receive a disproportionate number of older or higher-income participants.

BLOCKED RANDOMIZATION
Block 1 (Young):       Treated  |  Control
Block 2 (Middle-aged): Treated  |  Control
Block 3 (Older):       Treated  |  Control

Guarantees equal allocation within every block by design. Eliminates chance covariate imbalance across the blocked variables.

1. GUARANTEED BALANCE

Pre-empts chance imbalance on critical baseline attributes by forcing treatment and control arms to have identical proportions in each stratum.

2. INCREASED PRECISION

Reduces the unexplained outcome variance by isolating between-block differences, thereby lowering the standard error of \(\hat{\beta}_1\).

2. Analysis: Accounting for Strata

If an experiment is randomized within blocks, standard econometric practice dictates that the empirical specification should reflect the experimental design:

BLOCK-INDICATOR REGRESSION SPECIFICATION
$$Y_i = \alpha + \beta_1 T_i + \sum_{b=1}^{B-1} \gamma_b D_{ib} + \varepsilon_i$$
where \(D_{ib} = 1\) if individual \(i\) belongs to block \(b\), and \(0\) otherwise. The stratum fixed effects \(\gamma_b\) absorb between-block variation.
ANALYSIS CAUTION

Blocking vs. Clustering: Do Not Confuse the Two

Stratification (blocking) assigns treatment within groups of individuals to guarantee balance. In contrast, cluster randomization assigns entire clusters to treatment or control simultaneously. Randomizing within blocks does not mean you must cluster standard errors by block. Block indicators in OLS respect the design by absorbing stratum-level fixed effects.

3. Fisher's Exact Test for Contingency Tables

When outcomes are binary (success or failure), researchers frequently evaluate treatment efficacy using a \(2 \times 2\) contingency table:

2 × 2 CONTINGENCY TABLE SETUP
Group Success Failure Total
Treatment a b a + b = n₁
Control c d c + d = n₀
Total a + c b + d N
HYPERGEOMETRIC EXACT PROBABILITY
$$P(X = a) = \frac{\binom{a+b}{a}\binom{c+d}{c}}{\binom{N}{a+c}} = \frac{\binom{n_1}{a}\binom{n_0}{c}}{\binom{N}{m}}$$
Under the sharp null that treatment affects no one, the marginal totals (\(n_1, n_0, m, N-m\)) are strictly fixed. The probability of observing exactly \(a\) treated successes follows the hypergeometric distribution.

Fisher's exact test sums the hypergeometric probabilities of all tables that are as extreme as or more extreme than the observed table.

5. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Why can blocked randomization improve the precision of the estimated treatment effect?

HINT: Think about what happens to unexplained outcome variation when comparisons are restricted to homogeneous subgroups.

ANSWER: Because randomization occurs within more homogeneous groups, reducing chance imbalance and allowing block indicators to absorb between-block outcome variation.

WHY? When strata explain a portion of the outcome variance, stratifying ensures treatment and control groups are balanced across those strata. In the analysis regression, stratum fixed effects account for between-block differences, reducing the residual variance \(\sigma^2\) and lowering the standard error of the estimated treatment effect.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Blocked randomization conducts mini-experiments within pre-defined demographic or baseline strata.
  • Blocking guarantees balance on stratified baseline covariates, preventing finite-sample chance disparities.
  • Stratum fixed effects in regression (\(\sum \gamma_b D_{ib}\)) absorb between-block variance to boost precision.
  • Stratified assignment within blocks is fundamentally distinct from cluster-randomized treatments.
  • Fisher's exact test uses hypergeometric probabilities to test the sharp null on \(2 \times 2\) contingency tables.
