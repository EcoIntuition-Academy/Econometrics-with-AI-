LESSON 13.5 Diagnostics

Balance Tests in Randomized Experiments

Random assignment removes systematic treatment selection in expectation, but realized finite samples can still exhibit chance imbalance. Learn how to diagnose baseline comparability using t-tests, balance regressions, and standardized mean differences.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define baseline balance and explain why random assignment does not guarantee identical samples in finite data.
  • Identify the three primary motivations for checking balance: precision, implementation diagnostics, and finite-sample reality.
  • Specify the balance regression model \(X_i = \alpha + \delta T_i + \varepsilon_i\) and test \(H_0: \delta = 0\).
  • Compute and interpret standardized mean differences across pre-treatment covariates.
  • Recognize that chance imbalances occur naturally and that a single balance rejection does not disprove randomization.

1. What is Balance & Why Do We Check It?

CORE DEFINITION

Baseline Balance

Balance means that pre-treatment covariates have similar empirical distributions across the treatment and control groups prior to the intervention.

CENTRAL PRINCIPLE

Randomization Does Not Guarantee Perfect Realized Balance

Random assignment ensures exogeneity in expectation: \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid T=1] = \mathbb{E}[X \mid T=0]\). However, in any realized finite sample, pure random chance will generate small or moderate discrepancies between treated and control sample means.

1. PRECISION CONCERN

Large chance differences in prognostic covariates can increase sampling variance and reduce the precision of the estimated treatment effect.

2. IMPLEMENTATION DIAGNOSTIC

Pervasive or severe imbalances across multiple baseline traits can reveal flaws in the randomization protocol, cheating, or attrition.

3. FINITE-SAMPLE REALITY

Checking balance documents the empirical profile of the randomized arms, informing appropriate covariate adjustments.

2. Balance Testing Methodologies

Researchers check whether treated sample mean \(\bar{X}_1\) differs meaningfully from control sample mean \(\bar{X}_0\) using three standard tools:

THE BALANCE REGRESSION
$$X_i = \alpha + \delta T_i + \varepsilon_i$$
Test the null hypothesis \(H_0: \delta = 0\). Under genuine randomization, the slope \(\delta\) should be statistically indistinguishable from zero.
STANDARDIZED MEAN DIFFERENCE (NORMALIZED DIFFERENCE)
$$\Delta_{\text{std}} = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_0}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2 + s_0^2}{2}}}$$
Scales the baseline gap by the pooled standard deviation, providing a scale-free measure of balance that does not mechanically inflate with sample size.
MULTIPLE TESTING CAUTION

Do Not Over-Interpret a Single Rejection

When testing balance across 20 pre-treatment covariates at the \(\alpha = 0.05\) level, the probability of rejecting at least one test purely by chance is \(1 - (0.95)^{20} \approx 64\%\). A single statistically significant balance test does not prove that randomization failed.

4. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

Does baseline imbalance on a single covariate automatically invalidate a randomized experiment?

HINT: Consider whether random sampling guarantees identical realized samples in finite data, and reflect on multiple testing.

ANSWER: No. Finite-sample imbalance can occur purely by chance, though substantial or systematic imbalance warrants investigation of implementation.

WHY? Randomization guarantees exogeneity in expectation, but chance variations arise in every realized finite sample. With multiple covariates tested, occasional false rejections are mathematically expected. Only systematic, severe, or widespread imbalance suggests a failure in protocol administration or differential attrition.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • Baseline balance evaluates whether pre-treatment covariates are similarly distributed across arms.
  • Randomization guarantees exogeneity in expectation, but finite samples naturally exhibit chance imbalance.
  • The balance regression \(X_i = \alpha + \delta T_i + \varepsilon_i\) formally tests for mean differences.
  • Standardized mean differences scale disparities by pooled standard deviations, providing scale-free metrics.
  • Testing numerous covariates introduces multiple-testing caveats; a single rejection does not disprove randomization.
