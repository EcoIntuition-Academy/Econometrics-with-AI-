LESSON 13.3 Inference

Randomization Inference

Traditional hypothesis tests rely on asymptotic normality and homoskedastic error distributions. Randomization inference tests hypotheses directly using the experimental lottery itself under the sharp null hypothesis of no treatment effect.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Define randomization inference and formulate the sharp null hypothesis of zero effect for every unit.
  • Identify the observed test statistic \(\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}} = \bar{Y}_1 - \bar{Y}_0\).
  • Execute the five-step randomization testing procedure via treatment shuffling.
  • Calculate exact and Monte Carlo simulation approximation p-values.
  • Understand why randomization inference is distribution-free and robust in finite samples.

1. The Goal & The Sharp Null Hypothesis

CORE DEFINITION

Randomization Inference

Randomization inference (or permutation testing) is an assignment-based testing approach that evaluates how unusual the observed test statistic would be under alternative treatment allocations permitted by the experimental design, assuming the null hypothesis is true.

THE SHARP NULL HYPOTHESIS
$$H_0: Y_i(1) = Y_i(0) \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \dots, N$$
Under this sharp null, no individual's outcome is altered by treatment status. Observed outcomes \(Y_i\) are fixed constants; only the assignment vector \(T\) varies across counterfactual lotteries.

In the actual experiment, we observe a sample difference in means:

OBSERVED STATISTIC
$$\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}} = \bar{Y}_1 - \bar{Y}_0$$
For example, suppose the realized experiment produces an observed difference of \(\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}} = 3.10\).

2. The 5-Step Randomization Procedure

Rather than appealing to an imaginary population from which the data were drawn, randomization inference derives the null distribution directly from the experimental assignment mechanism:

STEP 1 · OBSERVE

Compute the observed treatment-control mean difference \(\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}} = \bar{Y}_1 - \bar{Y}_0\).

STEP 2 · REASSIGN

Shuffle treatment labels among participants while preserving the number of treated units.

STEP 3 · RECALCULATE

Compute the pseudo-treatment effect \(\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{rand}}\) for this simulated assignment.

STEP 4 · REPEAT

Repeat across all allowable allocations (or a large Monte Carlo sample, e.g. 10,000 permutations).

STEP 5 · EVALUATE

Calculate the p-value as the proportion of reassignments yielding statistics at least as extreme as \(\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}}\).

TWO-SIDED RANDOMIZATION P-VALUE
$$p = \frac{\sum_{m=1}^M \mathbf{1}\left( |\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{rand}, m}| \ge |\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}}| \right)}{M}$$
where \(M\) is the total number of re-randomizations evaluated, and \(\mathbf{1}(\cdot)\) is the indicator function.

3. Exact Finite-Sample vs. Monte Carlo Approximation

EXACT RANDOMIZATION P-VALUE

Full Combinatorial Enumeration

When the total number of combinations \(\binom{N}{n_1}\) is small, software enumerates every allowable assignment. The resulting p-value is mathematically exact in finite samples.

MONTE CARLO APPROXIMATION

Simulation Approximation

When \(\binom{N}{n_1}\) is massive (e.g., \(\binom{100}{50} \approx 10^{29}\)), researchers draw a random sample of assignments (such as 10,000 draws). This provides a Monte Carlo simulation approximation to the randomization p-value.

DISTRIBUTIONAL ADVANTAGE

No Normal Distribution or Homoskedasticity Required

Unlike standard t-tests or F-tests, randomization inference does not assume that outcomes are normally distributed or homoskedastic. The reference distribution is generated entirely by the physical act of random assignment.

5. Quick Check

QUICK CHECK

What generates the null distribution in randomization inference?

HINT: Consider where the variation comes from under the sharp null hypothesis that outcomes are fixed.

ANSWER: The treatment-assignment mechanism under the sharp null hypothesis.

WHY? Under the sharp null that treatment has no effect on any unit, each individual's observed outcome is fixed regardless of assignment. The null distribution is constructed solely by evaluating all allowable permutations of the treatment vector generated by the experimental design.

TAKEAWAYS

What You Should Now Understand

  • The sharp null posits that treatment has exactly zero effect for every single individual.
  • Randomization inference uses treatment reassignment to generate the empirical reference distribution.
  • The two-sided p-value is the fraction of re-randomizations with \(|\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{rand}}| \ge |\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{obs}}|\).
  • Large samples of permutations yield a Monte Carlo simulation approximation to the exact p-value.
  • The method is distribution-free, requiring neither normality nor homoskedastic disturbances.
