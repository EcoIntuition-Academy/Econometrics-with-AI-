LESSON 1.6 CLRM Assumptions (Part 2)

CLRM: Exogeneity & Spherical Errors

The crown jewel of econometric identification: why zero conditional mean ensures unbiased estimates, and how spherical error structures protect statistical inference.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand strict exogeneity \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i \mid x_i] = 0\) and why it is not just "mean zero".
  • Trace how omitted variable bias causes endogeneity, and why gathering more data cannot resolve it.
  • Distinguish between homoskedasticity and heteroskedasticity, identifying their impact on efficiency vs. unbiasedness.
  • Diagnose autocorrelation in time-series data and inspect the spherical covariance matrix \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon\varepsilon' \mid X] = \sigma^2 I\).

1. Assumption A3: Strict Exogeneity

Assumption A3 Strict Conditional Exogeneity
\[ \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i \mid x_i] = 0 \quad \iff \quad \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] = 0 \]
MASTER KEY TO UNBIASEDNESS:
\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] = 0\)Disturbances average zero for every observed combination of \(X\)
\(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta} \mid X] = \beta\)Guarantees OLS is unconditionally centered on the true population parameter

Do NOT simply say: "The error has mean zero."
An unconditional mean of zero (\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon] = 0\)) is trivial; it is automatically satisfied as long as an intercept is included. Conditional mean zero is far stronger: it demands that for any specific value of \(X\), the average of the unobserved disturbances is zero. In plain English:

"Knowing the value of the explanatory variables tells you nothing systematic about the unobserved disturbances."

WHY DOES IT MATTER?

Exogeneity is the master key to unbiasedness! Taking the conditional expectation of the OLS estimator: \[ \mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta} \mid X] = \beta + (X'X)^{-1}X'\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X]}_{=0} = \mathbf{\beta} \] Without exogeneity, OLS does not center around the truth!

WHAT HAPPENS IF IT FAILS?

If \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] \neq 0\), the regressors are endogenous. OLS is biased (\(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta}] \neq \beta\)) and inconsistent (the bias does not disappear even with infinite sample size).

WHY IT MATTERS

When \(\varepsilon\) correlates with \(X\), OLS mistakenly credits the effect of the omitted error to \(X\), distorting the estimated slope.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

Omitted Variable Bias: If \(y = \beta_1 + \beta_2 x + \gamma z + u\) and \(z\) is omitted into \(\varepsilon = \gamma z + u\), then \(\text{Cov}(x, \varepsilon) \neq 0\).

⚡ Critical Econometric Insight: More Data Does Not Fix Endogeneity!

In statistical sampling, larger sample sizes shrink variance (\(\sigma^2 / n \to 0\)). However, if your estimator is aiming at the wrong target due to omitted variable endogeneity, collecting 10 million observations simply ensures that you estimate the wrong number with supreme precision! Endogeneity is an identification flaw that can only be solved through experimental design, instrumental variables, or controlling for the confounder.

2. Assumption A4: Spherical Errors

Assumption A4 requires that the disturbances have constant conditional variance and are uncorrelated across different observations.

Part A: Homoskedasticity

\[ \text{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid X) = \sigma^2 \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \dots, n \]
WHAT IT MEANS

Equal conditional variance across observations: every observation's disturbance is drawn from a distribution with the identical conditional variance \(\sigma^2\).

WHEN IT FAILS (HETEROSKEDASTICITY)

The conditional variance changes across observations: \(\text{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid X) = \sigma_i^2\). For example, in cross-state traffic-fatality data, observations from larger states may exhibit greater error variability than observations from smaller states.

WHY IT MATTERS

Under exogeneity, heteroskedasticity does not bias OLS coefficients. However, conventional homoskedastic standard errors are invalid, and OLS is no longer generally efficient (not BLUE).

Part B: No Autocorrelation

\[ \text{Cov}(\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_j \mid X) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i \neq j \]
WHAT IT MEANS

Conditional on \(X\), disturbances from different observations are mutually uncorrelated: one observation's error conveys zero information about another's.

WHEN IT FAILS (AUTOCORRELATION)

Disturbances move together across observations or time. In time-series data, a macroeconomic shock may persist over time, causing neighboring disturbances to move in the same direction.

WHY IT MATTERS

Under exogeneity, OLS remains unbiased, but conventional standard errors and classical efficiency results no longer apply; inference requires serial-correlation robust standard errors or alternative estimators.

ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION

Does heteroskedasticity bias \(\hat{\beta}\)? NO! If exogeneity holds, OLS remains unbiased, but it loses efficiency (it is no longer BLUE among linear unbiased estimators).

3. The Spherical Error Covariance Matrix

When Homoskedasticity and No Autocorrelation both hold, we say the disturbances are Spherical. In matrix notation, this elegant condition is summarized as:

Assumption A4 Spherical Error Covariance Matrix
\[ \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon\varepsilon' \mid X] = \sigma^2 I_n = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma^2 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma^2 & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & \sigma^2 \end{bmatrix} \]
DUAL CONDITIONS:
\(\text{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid X) = \sigma^2\)Homoskedasticity: Constant error variance \(\sigma^2\) along the diagonal
\(\text{Cov}(\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_j \mid X) = 0\)No Autocorrelation: Zero correlation on all off-diagonal entries (\(i \neq j\))
Matrix Element Mathematical Value Economic Meaning
Diagonal Entries \(\sigma^2\) (Constant) Homoskedasticity: Every observation has identical variance \(\sigma^2\).
Off-Diagonal Entries \(0\) (Zero) No Autocorrelation: No two observations share common unmodeled shocks.
⚠️ Common Student Mistake
MISCONCEPTION: "My dataset has heteroskedasticity, so my estimated slope coefficients \(\hat{\beta}\) are biased and incorrect."
CORRECTION: Heteroskedasticity does NOT bias OLS coefficients! Look at the proof of unbiasedness: \(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta} \mid X] = \beta + (X'X)^{-1}X'\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X]\). As long as Exogeneity holds (\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] = 0\)), \(\hat{\beta}\) remains perfectly unbiased. What breaks is the variance formula and standard errors. To fix this, you do not throw away OLS; you simply use Huber-White Robust Standard Errors!
🤖 AI Insight Heteroskedasticity in Machine Learning vs. Econometrics

In standard machine learning, algorithms like XGBoost minimize mean squared error without reporting hypothesis tests, so practitioners often ignore heteroskedasticity. In econometrics, because we must decide whether a policy parameter is statistically different from zero (p < 0.05), failing to correct for heteroskedasticity leads to wildly overconfident claims and false discoveries!

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: An economist regresses municipal police expenditures on crime rates across 400 cities. A diagnostic test detects severe heteroskedasticity (large cities have huge variance in expenditure errors). (a) Are the OLS slope estimates biased? (b) What happens if the economist uses standard textbook formulas for standard errors and t-statistics?

Separate the conditions required for unbiasedness (\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon|X]=0\)) from the conditions required for classical standard error validity (\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon\varepsilon'|X]=\sigma^2 I\)).

Solution:
(a) No, the OLS slope estimates are not biased, provided strict exogeneity holds.
(b) If standard textbook standard errors are used, they will be invalid and typically underestimated. The resulting t-statistics will be artificially inflated, leading to overly narrow confidence intervals and falsely declaring regressors to be statistically significant when they are not! The researcher must report heteroskedasticity-robust (Huber-White) standard errors.

Lesson 1.6 Summary

Strict exogeneity \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] = 0\) guarantees that OLS is unbiased. Spherical errors (\(\sigma^2 I\)) guarantee efficiency and valid classical standard errors.
